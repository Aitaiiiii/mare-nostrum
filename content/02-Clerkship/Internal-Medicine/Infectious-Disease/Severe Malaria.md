# Malaria & Severe Malaria

A mosquito-borne (*Anopheles*) protozoal infection that remains endemic along Thailand's forested borders. The clinical imperative is to **think of it in any febrile patient with relevant exposure, confirm species/parasitaemia quickly, and recognise severe (usually *falciparum*) malaria — a medical emergency treated with intravenous artesunate.** Untreated *falciparum* malaria kills within days.

## 🧬 Species & Pathophysiology

Five species infect humans: **_P. falciparum_ (the killer — sequesters in microvasculature → organ dysfunction), _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ (form dormant liver hypnozoites → relapse), _P. malariae_, and _P. knowlesi_** (a zoonosis, prominent in Malaysia/Indonesia, can be severe with high parasitaemia). *Falciparum* causes cytoadherence and rosetting in cerebral, renal, and placental microvessels → **cerebral malaria, AKI, ARDS, lactic acidosis, and hypoglycaemia.** In Thailand *P. falciparum* is drug-resistant (chloroquine, mefloquine, and emerging artemisinin partner-drug resistance in the east).

## 🩺 Clinical presentation & severe-malaria criteria

Non-specific: fever (classically paroxysmal/cyclical but often not), chills, headache, myalgia, malaise, sometimes GI symptoms; splenomegaly, anaemia, jaundice. **A febrile patient with travel to/residence in an endemic border area (or returning traveller) has malaria until excluded** — the presentation overlaps [[Dengue|dengue]], leptospirosis, and typhoid.

**Severe malaria (WHO / Thai CPG 2568) — any of, mostly *falciparum*:**
- **Impaired consciousness (cerebral malaria)/coma, seizures**, prostration
- **Respiratory distress / pulmonary oedema / ARDS**, metabolic acidosis (high lactate, low bicarbonate)
- **AKI**, jaundice/hepatic dysfunction
- **Hypoglycaemia**, severe anaemia, spontaneous bleeding/DIC, shock
- **Hyperparasitaemia — *P. falciparum* > 10%** of red cells (or *P. knowlesi* > 100,000/µL, or > 20,000/µL with jaundice)

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Thick and thin blood films (microscopy)** — species ID + parasitaemia quantification (thick = detection, thin = speciation/percentage); **repeat every 12–24 h** if initially negative but suspicion persists (up to three sets).
- **Rapid diagnostic test (RDT)** — antigen-based, separates *P. falciparum* from non-*falciparum*; useful when microscopy is unavailable.
- **G6PD status** (before primaquine/tafenoquine for *vivax*/*ovale*) — ask about dark urine/haemolysis history.
- Glucose (hypoglycaemia), CBC, renal/liver function, venous gas/lactate, coagulation; monitor parasitaemia response.

## 💊 Management (Thai CPG 2568 — confirm the current national policy before prescribing)

**1. Severe malaria → IV artesunate immediately.** **Artesunate 2.4 mg/kg IV bolus at 0, 12, and 24 hours, then once daily** (**3 mg/kg if < 20 kg**), for at least 24 h and until able to take oral therapy, **then complete a full oral ACT course.** IV artesunate is superior to quinine (SEAQUAMAT/AQUAMAT). Manage in a hospital able to support organ failure; watch for **delayed post-artesunate haemolysis** (check Hb 1–2 weeks later).

**2. Uncomplicated *P. falciparum* (Thailand) — oral ACT + single-dose primaquine.** First-line **dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine + single-dose primaquine (0.25 mg/kg, gametocidal)**; in **Sisaket/Ubon Ratchathani** (partner-drug resistance) the national policy uses **artesunate-pyronaridine.** (Regimens change with resistance — check the current Thai malaria policy.)

**3. *P. vivax* / *P. ovale* — treat blood stage + eradicate hypnozoites.** Chloroquine (or an ACT where chloroquine resistance) for the blood stage, **PLUS primaquine 14 days (or single-dose tafenoquine) to prevent relapse — only after confirming G6PD normal; NEVER in pregnancy.**

**4. Supportive care (severe).** Careful fluids (avoid overload/ARDS), **treat hypoglycaemia** (frequent glucose checks — quinine and the infection both cause it), transfuse severe anaemia, dialyse for AKI, treat seizures, and manage as [[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)|sepsis]]; exchange transfusion is not routinely recommended. Screen for concurrent bacteraemia in severe disease.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

**IV artesunate, oral ACTs (DHA-piperaquine, artesunate-pyronaridine), chloroquine, primaquine, and tafenoquine are provided under the Thai national malaria programme**, and the **Thai malaria CPG 2568 (2025)** is on disk in the reference library. Transmission concentrates along the **Thai–Myanmar and Thai–Cambodia borders and forested areas**; artemisinin partner-drug resistance in the east drove the regional switch to artesunate-pyronaridine. Dose by ideal body weight (max 80 kg), and **never give primaquine/tafenoquine in pregnancy or G6PD deficiency.**

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Fever + endemic-area/border exposure = malaria until excluded** → films/RDT now; repeat films if negative.
> - **Severe *falciparum* malaria (coma, > 10% parasitaemia, organ failure) → IV artesunate immediately** — do not delay for transfer.
> - **Check G6PD before primaquine/tafenoquine; never in pregnancy** (haemolysis).
> - **Watch for hypoglycaemia** (infection + quinine) and **delayed post-artesunate haemolysis** (Hb at 1–2 wk).
> - **Treat *vivax*/*ovale* relapse** with a hypnozoite course — blood-stage treatment alone lets it recur.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Thick/thin films (+ RDT); repeat if negative**; *falciparum* is the killer, *vivax/ovale* relapse (hypnozoites).
> - **Severe malaria → IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg at 0/12/24 h then daily (3 mg/kg if < 20 kg)**, then oral ACT.
> - **Uncomplicated Pf (Thailand): DHA-piperaquine + single-dose primaquine** (artesunate-pyronaridine in Sisaket/Ubon).
> - ***Vivax/ovale*: blood-stage drug + primaquine 14 d (or tafenoquine) after G6PD** — never in pregnancy.
> - Watch **hypoglycaemia** and **delayed post-artesunate haemolysis**; supportive care for organ failure.

# Urinary Tract Infection & Pyelonephritis

Bacterial infection anywhere along the urinary tract, spanning trivial cystitis to life-threatening **urosepsis**. In the ER the two decisions that matter are **how sick / how complicated** (site of care and empiric spectrum) and **is there obstruction** — an **obstructed, infected kidney is a urological emergency requiring drainage, not just antibiotics.** Empiric choice in Thailand is dominated by **high *E. coli* resistance to fluoroquinolones and co-trimoxazole and a rising ESBL rate.**

## 🧬 Microbiology & Pathophysiology

Most UTI is ascending: peri-urethral/faecal flora colonise the urethra → bladder (cystitis) → ureter/kidney (pyelonephritis); haematogenous seeding (e.g. *S. aureus*) is rarer. Uropathogens: **_E. coli_ (~75–85%)**, then *Klebsiella*, *Proteus* (urease → struvite stones), *Enterococcus*, *S. saprophyticus* (young women), and *Pseudomonas*/*Candida* in catheterised/instrumented patients. Virulence (adhesins, pili) plus host factors (obstruction, stones, catheter, diabetes, pregnancy, neurogenic bladder) determine invasion.

**Definitions that drive management:**
- **Uncomplicated** — non-pregnant, premenopausal woman, normal tract, no systemic features (cystitis; sometimes uncomplicated pyelonephritis).
- **Complicated** — any structural/functional abnormality, obstruction, stone, catheter, **male sex, pregnancy, immunocompromise, diabetes, or systemic sepsis** — a higher-resistance, higher-relapse group.
- **Asymptomatic bacteriuria** — treat **only** in **pregnancy** and **before urologic procedures with mucosal trauma**; otherwise do not (over-treatment breeds resistance).

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

- **Cystitis:** dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain, no fever.
- **Acute pyelonephritis:** fever/rigors, **flank pain, costovertebral-angle tenderness**, nausea/vomiting ± cystitis symptoms.
- **Urosepsis:** SIRS/qSOFA features, hypotension, confusion (especially the elderly, who may present only with delirium or a fall).
- **Emphysematous pyelonephritis / obstructive pyonephrosis:** severe sepsis, poorly-controlled diabetes, gas on imaging — a surgical emergency.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Urinalysis + urine culture** (pyuria, nitrite, leukocyte esterase; culture before antibiotics in all but simple cystitis).
- **Blood cultures** if pyelonephritis/sepsis; CBC, electrolytes, **BUN/creatinine**, glucose, lactate.
- **Imaging (do not skip when complicated/septic/not improving):** **renal ultrasound or CT** to find **obstruction, stone, abscess, or emphysematous change** — mandatory in a septic or non-responding patient to catch a surgical problem.
- Pregnancy test in women of childbearing age.

## 💊 Management

**1. Uncomplicated cystitis (outpatient, short course):**
- **Nitrofurantoin 100 mg PO bid × 5 days**, or **fosfomycin 3 g single dose**, or **co-trimoxazole** (only where local *E. coli* resistance < 20% — often exceeded in Thailand). Reserve fluoroquinolones (high Thai resistance + toxicity).

**2. Acute pyelonephritis / complicated UTI:**
- **Outpatient (mild, tolerating oral, not pregnant):** an oral agent guided by susceptibility; empirical fluoroquinolones are limited by Thai resistance, so many need an initial IV dose.
- **Inpatient (systemic features, vomiting, pregnancy, obstruction, comorbidity):** **empiric IV — ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV OD** first-line; **piperacillin-tazobactam or a carbapenem (ertapenem/meropenem) if ESBL risk** (prior ESBL, recent broad antibiotics, healthcare exposure); add an aminoglycoside or antipseudomonal cover for catheter/instrumented/*Pseudomonas* risk. **In septic shock, cover broadly (carbapenem) given the high Thai ESBL prevalence, then de-escalate.**
- **Switch IV → PO** once afebrile and improving (≈ 48 h), tailored to culture.
- **Duration (IDSA 2025, shorter): 5–7 days for a fluoroquinolone, ~7 days for a non-fluoroquinolone** in complicated UTI/pyelonephritis improving on effective therapy (10–14 days only for slow response, abscess, or bacteraemia with a difficult source).

**3. The surgical emergency — obstructed, infected kidney.** An **infected obstructed system (stone, tumour, stricture) or a renal/perinephric abscess or emphysematous pyelonephritis** will not resolve on antibiotics alone → **urgent decompression** (ureteric stent or percutaneous nephrostomy) and urology/interventional radiology referral; drain abscesses. Delay causes rapid septic death.

**4. Pregnancy:** treat asymptomatic bacteriuria and cystitis (β-lactams, nitrofurantoin [avoid at term], fosfomycin — **avoid fluoroquinolones and co-trimoxazole in the first trimester/at term**); admit pyelonephritis for IV β-lactam.

**5. Catheter-associated UTI:** treat only if symptomatic; **remove or replace the catheter**; do not treat asymptomatic catheter bacteriuria.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & resistance

All agents are on the Thai NLEM (nitrofurantoin, fosfomycin, ceftriaxone, piperacillin-tazobactam, ertapenem/meropenem, amikacin). **Thailand has high community *E. coli* resistance to fluoroquinolones and co-trimoxazole and a substantial ESBL rate**, so these are poor empiric choices for anything beyond simple cystitis; **ceftriaxone is the practical empiric backbone for inpatient pyelonephritis, escalating to a carbapenem when ESBL is likely or the patient is in shock.** Always de-escalate on the local antibiogram and culture.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Obstructed + infected kidney (stone/hydronephrosis, emphysematous pyelonephritis, pyonephrosis) → EMERGENCY decompression** (stent/nephrostomy) — antibiotics alone will fail.
> - **Don't rely on fluoroquinolones/co-trimoxazole empirically in Thailand** (high resistance); cover ESBL with a carbapenem in the septic/high-risk patient.
> - **Do NOT treat asymptomatic bacteriuria** except in **pregnancy** or before urologic mucosal procedures.
> - Elderly with new delirium/fall + fever → look for urosepsis, but don't over-diagnose UTI in asymptomatic bacteriuria.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Cystitis (no fever) vs pyelonephritis (fever + **CVA tenderness**) vs urosepsis; complicated = male/pregnant/obstruction/catheter/immunocompromised.
> - Cystitis: **nitrofurantoin 5 d / fosfomycin single dose**. Pyelonephritis inpatient: **ceftriaxone IV**, **carbapenem if ESBL/shock**.
> - **Durations are short (IDSA 2025): 5–7 d FQ, ~7 d non-FQ.**
> - **Obstructed infected kidney = drain it** (urology/IR) — the one thing that must not be missed.
> - Treat **asymptomatic bacteriuria only in pregnancy / pre-urologic procedure.**

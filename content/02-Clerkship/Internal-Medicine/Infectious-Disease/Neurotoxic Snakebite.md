# Neurotoxic Snakebite (Elapid Envenoming)

Envenoming by neurotoxic snakes (Elapidae) that causes a **descending flaccid paralysis culminating in respiratory failure**. In Thailand the culprits are **cobras, king cobra, and kraits**; the death is from **ventilatory failure**, so the single life-saving intervention is **airway/breathing support**, with antivenom and an anticholinesterase trial alongside. Kraits are the treacherous ones — minimal local signs, delayed and often nocturnal paralysis.

## 🧬 Thai neurotoxic snakes & venom mechanism

Venom neurotoxins block neuromuscular transmission:
- **α-neurotoxins (postsynaptic)** — bind the ACh receptor (curare-like); **reversible**, so **antivenom and neostigmine can work** (cobra).
- **β-neurotoxins (presynaptic)** — destroy the nerve terminal, blocking ACh release; **poorly reversible** once established, so recovery awaits terminal regeneration (kraits) — antivenom prevents progression but does not reverse fixed paralysis.

| Snake (Thai) | Genus/species | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Monocled cobra** | *Naja kaouthia* | neurotoxic + **local necrosis**; spitting varieties → ocular injury |
| **King cobra** | *Ophiophagus hannah* | large venom load, rapid neurotoxicity |
| **Banded krait** | *Bungarus fasciatus* | pure neuro, little local sign |
| **Malayan krait** | *Bungarus candidus* | **nocturnal bite, minimal local pain, delayed (hours) severe paralysis**, autonomic features |

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Descending paralysis: ptosis (earliest, most reliable sign) → ophthalmoplegia → dysarthria/dysphagia → bulbar and neck weakness → diaphragm/respiratory muscle failure.** Cobra bites usually show **local pain, swelling, and tissue necrosis**; **krait bites often have almost no local signs** and present hours later with paralysis (classically a person who wakes at night unable to see/swallow/breathe). Watch for **abdominal pain, hypertension, and hyponatraemia** with Malayan krait. **A single-breath count, ptosis, and neck-lift are practical bedside markers of progression.**

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Serial neurological assessment** (ptosis, extra-ocular movements, single-breath count, neck flexion, **respiratory rate/SpO₂, vital capacity**) — the key monitoring.
- **20-minute whole-blood clotting test (20-min WBCT)** and coagulation/platelets/renal function **to distinguish/exclude concurrent haemotoxic envenoming** (viper) — some snakes and mixed pictures overlap (see [[Hematotoxin Snake Bite]]).
- Identify the snake if safely possible (photo); electrolytes (hyponatraemia with krait), CK, ECG.

## 💊 Management

**1. Airway & breathing FIRST — this is what saves lives.** Anticipate respiratory failure: give oxygen, and **intubate + ventilate (or bag-valve-mask in a resource-poor setting) at the first sign of ventilatory compromise** (falling vital capacity/single-breath count, bulbar weakness, hypoventilation). Do not wait for arrest. Full recovery is expected with ventilatory support even in complete paralysis (especially krait) once the venom effect wanes.

**2. First aid / transport.** **Pressure-immobilisation bandage + splint** is appropriate for **elapid (neurotoxic) bites** to slow lymphatic venom spread; keep the limb immobilised, remove rings, reassure. **Do NOT** cut, suck, apply arterial tourniquets, or use electric shock/traditional remedies.

**3. Antivenom — for systemic neurotoxicity.** Give **Neuro Polyvalent antivenom (Queen Saovabha Memorial Institute, QSMI)** when there are neurotoxic signs (ptosis/ophthalmoplegia/bulbar/respiratory involvement) or a definite dangerous-species bite with progression; use species-specific monovalent antivenom if the snake is identified and available. Give IV with **adrenaline, hydrocortisone, and antihistamine ready for anaphylaxis** (antivenom reactions are common); repeat if neurotoxicity progresses.

**4. Anticholinesterase trial (Tensilon/neostigmine test).** For neurotoxic envenoming, **atropine 0.6 mg IV then neostigmine 1.5–2.5 mg IV/SC** (with atropine to block muscarinic effects); a response (improved ptosis/strength) supports a **postsynaptic (cobra)** mechanism and neostigmine can be continued — **kraits (presynaptic) respond poorly.** It is an adjunct, not a substitute for ventilation/antivenom.

**5. Local wound & supportive care.** Wound care and surgical debridement for cobra necrosis; **tetanus prophylaxis**; antibiotics only for evident secondary infection; analgesia; treat hyponatraemia. Observe krait/asymptomatic bites for a prolonged period (**paralysis can be delayed many hours**) before discharge.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

**QSMI (Thai Red Cross) produces the Neuro Polyvalent antivenom** (covers *N. kaouthia*, *O. hannah*, *B. fasciatus*, *B. candidus*) and species-specific monovalents, stocked across Thai hospitals; ventilators, neostigmine, and atropine are standard. Snakebite is a notified/common rural Thai emergency — the **Ramathibodi Poison Center (1367)** and QSMI provide 24-h advice. Distinguish neurotoxic (elapid) from **haemotoxic (viper — Russell's/green pit viper/Malayan pit viper)** envenoming early with the 20-min WBCT, as the antivenom and monitoring differ ([[Hematotoxin Snake Bite]]).

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Respiratory failure is the killer — intubate/ventilate early** at the first sign of bulbar/ventilatory weakness; full recovery follows support.
> - **Krait bites can look trivial and paralyse hours later (often at night)** — observe long, don't discharge early.
> - **Give antivenom for neurotoxic signs** with adrenaline/steroid/antihistamine ready (reaction risk); repeat if progressing.
> - **Neostigmine helps cobra (postsynaptic), poorly for krait (presynaptic)** — an adjunct, never a substitute for ventilation.
> - **No tourniquet/incision/suction** — use pressure-immobilisation for elapid bites; always exclude concurrent haemotoxic envenoming (20-min WBCT).

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Descending paralysis: ptosis → ophthalmoplegia → bulbar → respiratory failure**; kraits = minimal local signs + delayed/nocturnal.
> - **Airway/ventilation first** (intubate early) — patients recover fully with support.
> - **QSMI Neuro Polyvalent antivenom** for neurotoxic signs; **atropine + neostigmine trial** (works for cobra, not krait).
> - **Pressure-immobilisation** for elapid bites; **no tourniquet/incision/suction**; tetanus prophylaxis.
> - **20-min WBCT** to exclude haemotoxic envenoming; Ramathibodi Poison Center **1367** / QSMI for advice.

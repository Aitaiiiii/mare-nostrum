# Acute Bacterial Meningitis

Purulent infection of the leptomeninges and subarachnoid space — one of the few true "minutes matter" infections. Untreated it is nearly always fatal, and each hour of delayed antibiotics worsens outcome, so the governing rule is **do not let any diagnostic step delay antibiotics + dexamethasone.** In Thailand the differential must always include **tuberculous meningitis** and, in the HIV-positive, **cryptococcal meningitis**.

## 🧬 Microbiology & Pathophysiology

Bacteria reach the meninges by haematogenous spread (naso-pharyngeal colonisation → bacteraemia) or contiguous spread (otitis, sinusitis, skull fracture). Subarachnoid multiplication triggers a cytokine storm → blood-brain-barrier breakdown, vasogenic + cytotoxic + interstitial oedema, raised ICP, impaired cerebral autoregulation, and vasculitis/infarction — the substrate of the neurological damage that **dexamethasone** blunts.

| Host | Likely organisms | Empiric implication |
|---|---|---|
| Adult 16–50, immunocompetent | *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis* | ceftriaxone + vancomycin |
| **> 50, alcoholic, immunocompromised, pregnant** | add ***Listeria monocytogenes***, aerobic GNB | **add ampicillin** |
| Post-neurosurgery / penetrating head trauma / CSF shunt | *S. aureus*, coag-neg staph, *Pseudomonas*, GNB | vancomycin + antipseudomonal β-lactam (cefepime/meropenem) |
| Basilar skull fracture / CSF leak | *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, group A strep | ceftriaxone + vancomycin |
| **Thailand / HIV / immunocompromised** | **TB meningitis, *Cryptococcus neoformans***, *Strongyloides*, melioidosis | add TB/antifungal work-up + therapy |

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Classic triad — **fever, neck stiffness, altered mental status** — but **all three are present in < 50%**; nearly all have at least two of fever, headache, stiff neck, or altered consciousness. Look for **Kernig's** (resistance/pain on knee extension with hip flexed) and **Brudzinski's** (involuntary hip/knee flexion on neck flexion) signs, photophobia, seizures, focal deficits, and cranial-nerve palsies. **A petechial/purpuric non-blanching rash suggests meningococcaemia** — a fulminant emergency. Papilloedema and a depressed GCS signal raised ICP.

> [!note] 💎 Pearls
> - **Purpura fulminans + shock** in a young adult = meningococcal sepsis → antibiotics *immediately*, do not wait to move rooms.
> - **Subacute meningitis (days–weeks), basal predominance, cranial-nerve palsies, hydrocephalus, low CSF glucose with lymphocytosis** in a Thai/HIV patient = **TB meningitis** until excluded — start anti-TB + steroid on suspicion; don't wait for culture.
> - **HIV + headache/fever + raised opening pressure + lymphocytic CSF** → **cryptococcal meningitis**: CSF/serum CrAg, India ink; needs amphotericin + flucytosine and **serial therapeutic LPs** to control ICP.

## 🩻 Investigations & the LP-vs-CT decision

**Order of actions:** blood cultures → **dexamethasone + empiric antibiotics** → then LP (do not reverse this if LP is delayed).

- **CT head BEFORE LP only if a risk factor for herniation/mass lesion is present:** immunocompromise, history of CNS disease, **new-onset seizure (≤ 1 week)**, papilloedema, abnormal level of consciousness, or focal neurological deficit. **Otherwise, LP first** — a normal CT does not guarantee it is safe, but these features flag who needs imaging. **If CT is required, take blood cultures and give dexamethasone + antibiotics BEFORE the scan.**
- **Lumbar puncture — opening pressure + CSF for cell count/differential, glucose (with paired serum glucose), protein, Gram stain, culture**, and (per suspicion) PCR (meningococcus/pneumococcus/HSV), **CrAg, India ink, AFB/TB PCR (Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra), VDRL**.

| CSF | Bacterial | Viral | TB / fungal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening pressure | ↑ | normal/↑ | ↑↑ |
| Cells | **neutrophils, often > 1000** | lymphocytes | lymphocytes |
| Glucose (CSF:serum) | **low (< 0.4)** | normal | **low** |
| Protein | high | normal/mildly ↑ | **very high** |

- **Bloods:** CBC, electrolytes, glucose, renal/liver function, coagulation, procalcitonin/CRP; **HIV test in everyone** (changes the differential).

## 🗂️ Differential diagnosis

Viral (aseptic) meningitis, **HSV encephalitis** (fever + altered mentation + focal/temporal features → add empiric aciclovir), TB and cryptococcal meningitis, subarachnoid haemorrhage (thunderclap headache, xanthochromia), brain abscess, cerebral malaria, and non-infective causes (drug-induced, autoimmune). **When encephalitis is possible, add IV aciclovir empirically.**

## 💊 Management

**1. Do not delay — give within 1 hour of arrival.** Sequence: blood cultures → **dexamethasone** → **empiric antibiotics** → LP (or LP first if no CT indication).

**2. Dexamethasone — the only proven adjunct.** **Dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h for 4 days**, first dose **with or just before (10–20 min) the first antibiotic dose.** It reduces mortality and hearing loss in **pneumococcal** meningitis; **continue only if pneumococcus is confirmed/likely, otherwise stop.** (In suspected **TB meningitis**, use a separate corticosteroid course — dexamethasone tapered over ~6–8 weeks — which is beneficial there.)

**3. Empiric antibiotics (adult), adjust to age/risk and local resistance:**
- **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + Vancomycin** (loading then dose to trough/AUC; covers resistant *S. pneumoniae*).
- **Add Ampicillin 2 g IV q4h** if **> 50 years, alcoholic, pregnant, or immunocompromised** (Listeria cover).
- **Post-neurosurgery/shunt/trauma:** Vancomycin + **cefepime or meropenem** (antipseudomonal).
- **Severe β-lactam allergy:** vancomycin + moxifloxacin (± co-trimoxazole for Listeria).
- **Add aciclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h** if HSV encephalitis is possible.

**4. Targeted therapy once identified** (e.g. penicillin/ceftriaxone for susceptible meningococcus/pneumococcus; ampicillin/co-trimoxazole for Listeria). Duration: meningococcus 7 days, pneumococcus 10–14 days, Listeria ≥ 21 days.

**5. Supportive & ICP care:** ICU for depressed GCS/shock; head-up 30°, treat seizures, manage raised ICP, correct hyponatraemia (SIADH), and support sepsis physiology (see [[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)]]).

**6. Public health.** **Meningococcal disease is notifiable** and needs **droplet isolation for 24 h**; give **chemoprophylaxis to close contacts** (rifampicin, ciprofloxacin single dose, or ceftriaxone). Notify the case per Thai communicable-disease reporting.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

Ceftriaxone, vancomycin, ampicillin, meropenem, cefepime, dexamethasone, aciclovir, amphotericin B, and fluconazole are all on the Thai NLEM. Thailand's key differences from Western cohorts: **TB meningitis is common** (start empiric anti-TB + adjunctive dexamethasone on strong suspicion, especially with subacute basal disease and HIV), and **cryptococcal meningitis is a leading cause in advanced HIV** (amphotericin B + flucytosine/fluconazole induction, with therapeutic LPs for ICP). Melioidosis and *Strongyloides* hyperinfection are further local considerations in the immunocompromised. Reporting follows the Thai communicable-disease system.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Never delay antibiotics + dexamethasone for CT or LP** — cultures, then treat, then image/tap.
> - **Purpuric rash + shock** = meningococcal sepsis → antibiotics *now*, droplet isolation, contact prophylaxis.
> - **Give dexamethasone WITH or just before the first antibiotic** — it does not work if given late.
> - In Thailand/HIV, **do not anchor on pyogenic bacteria** — actively exclude **TB and cryptococcal** meningitis (they need entirely different therapy).
> - CT-before-LP triggers: immunocompromise, CNS disease, new seizure, papilloedema, ↓consciousness, focal deficit.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Bundle within 1 h**: blood cultures → **dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h ×4 d (with/just before 1st antibiotic)** → **ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + vancomycin** → LP.
> - **Add ampicillin 2 g IV q4h** if **> 50 / immunocompromised / pregnant / alcoholic** (Listeria).
> - **CSF bacterial**: neutrophils > 1000, **glucose CSF:serum < 0.4**, high protein, ↑opening pressure.
> - **Thailand**: always consider **TB** (subacute, basal, ↓glucose lymphocytic) and **cryptococcal** (HIV) meningitis.
> - Meningococcus → droplet isolation 24 h + **contact chemoprophylaxis** (ciprofloxacin/rifampicin/ceftriaxone); notifiable.

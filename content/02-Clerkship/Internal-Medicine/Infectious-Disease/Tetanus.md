# Tetanus

A neurological disease caused by the toxin (**tetanospasmin**) of *Clostridium tetani*, producing **uncontrolled muscle rigidity and spasms** and, in severe cases, life-threatening **autonomic instability.** It is entirely vaccine-preventable, so it clusters in the **unvaccinated/incompletely vaccinated** (elderly, migrant workers, neonates of unvaccinated mothers) — a real consideration in parts of Thailand.

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

Spores from soil/rusty objects/wounds germinate in anaerobic (devitalised, contaminated, deep-puncture) tissue → **tetanospasmin** travels retrogradely up motor axons to the spinal cord/brainstem and **blocks release of inhibitory neurotransmitters (GABA, glycine)** from Renshaw/inhibitory interneurons → **unopposed motor neuron firing** → sustained rigidity and paroxysmal spasms; disinhibition of the autonomic nervous system → **sympathetic overactivity.** Toxin binding is irreversible — recovery requires new nerve terminals (weeks), so antitoxin only neutralises unbound toxin (prevent progression, not reverse).

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Incubation ~3–21 days (shorter = more severe). **Generalised tetanus:** **trismus (lockjaw)** and **risus sardonicus** (facial muscle spasm), neck/back rigidity → **opisthotonus**, board-like abdomen, and **reflex generalised spasms** triggered by stimuli (light, noise, touch) — painful, and causing **laryngospasm/apnoea, fractures, and rhabdomyolysis.** **Autonomic dysfunction** (severe): labile hypertension/tachycardia alternating with hypotension/bradycardia, arrhythmias, sweating — a major cause of death. Consciousness is preserved. Localised and cephalic (cranial-nerve) forms occur; **neonatal tetanus** (poor feeding, rigidity, spasms in a neonate of an unvaccinated mother) is severe.

## 🩻 Investigations

**Tetanus is a clinical diagnosis** — do not wait for tests. The **spatula test** (touching the posterior pharynx provokes reflex masseter spasm/biting rather than a gag) supports it. Wound culture is insensitive. Check CK (rhabdomyolysis), and monitor for autonomic instability (continuous cardiac monitoring). Exclude mimics: dystonic drug reaction, strychnine, hypocalcaemic tetany, dental abscess, [[Neurotoxic Snakebite|envenoming]], meningitis.

## 💊 Management (ICU, quiet dark environment)

**1. Neutralise unbound toxin — antitoxin.** **Human tetanus immunoglobulin (TIG) — a single IM dose (commonly ~3000–6000 units; protocols vary)** as soon as possible (equine antitoxin where TIG unavailable, after test dose). Some centres give part intrathecally. TIG does not reverse bound toxin.

**2. Eliminate the source.** **Wound debridement** (remove devitalised/contaminated tissue) and **antibiotics — metronidazole 500 mg IV q6–8h (preferred over penicillin, which is a GABA antagonist and may worsen spasms).**

**3. Control muscle spasms + rigidity.** **Benzodiazepines (diazepam or midazolam) — the mainstay**, titrated (often high doses/infusion) to control spasms; **magnesium sulfate infusion** reduces spasms and autonomic instability; add other agents (baclofen, propofol) as needed. **Minimise stimulation** (dark, quiet room). Severe/refractory spasms or laryngospasm → **intubation/ventilation with neuromuscular blockade** and sedation.

**4. Airway & autonomic care.** Early airway protection (laryngospasm risk); manage **autonomic storm** (magnesium, short-acting agents — labetalol/esmolol; avoid abrupt swings), and support ventilation for a prolonged ICU course. Nutrition, VTE prophylaxis, pressure care.

**5. Vaccinate — the disease does NOT confer immunity.** Give a **full tetanus toxoid vaccination series** during recovery (plus wound-appropriate prophylaxis), and educate. Prevention: **tetanus-prone wound management** — toxoid ± TIG based on immunisation status and wound type.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

Human TIG, tetanus toxoid, metronidazole, benzodiazepines, and magnesium are available in Thai hospitals; Thailand's childhood immunisation coverage is high, so tetanus is now uncommon but **still occurs in incompletely-vaccinated adults/elderly and via agricultural/soil-contaminated wounds** — hence robust **wound prophylaxis (toxoid ± TIG by wound type and vaccine history)** in the ER is the key preventive skill. Neonatal tetanus has been largely eliminated but requires maternal vaccination awareness.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Autonomic instability and laryngospasm are the killers** — ICU, airway protection, magnesium/benzodiazepines.
> - **Metronidazole preferred over penicillin** (penicillin is a GABA antagonist → may worsen spasms).
> - **Antitoxin neutralises only unbound toxin** — give early; it does not reverse established disease.
> - **The disease does NOT confer immunity — vaccinate** during recovery.
> - **Minimise stimulation** (dark, quiet room) — light/noise/touch trigger spasms.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Toxin (tetanospasmin) blocks **GABA/glycine** → trismus, risus sardonicus, opisthotonus, reflex spasms, autonomic storm; **consciousness preserved**.
> - **Clinical diagnosis** (spatula test); treat now.
> - **TIG (~3000–6000 U IM) + metronidazole + wound debridement**; **benzodiazepines ± magnesium** for spasms; quiet dark room; intubate/paralyse if severe.
> - **Autonomic instability = major killer** → magnesium, short-acting agents.
> - **Vaccinate** (no natural immunity) + wound-based prophylaxis.

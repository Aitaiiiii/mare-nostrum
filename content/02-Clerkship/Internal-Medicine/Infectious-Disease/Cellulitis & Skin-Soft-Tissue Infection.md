# Cellulitis & Skin-Soft-Tissue Infection (SSTI)

Infection of the skin and underlying soft tissue, from superficial erysipelas to limb- and life-threatening necrotizing fasciitis. Two forks drive everything: **purulent vs non-purulent** (which organism, which antibiotic) and **severity** (site of care, spectrum) — with an overriding duty to **detect necrotizing infection early**, because it is a surgical emergency that antibiotics alone will not save.

## 🧬 Microbiology & the purulent/non-purulent fork

| Type | Usual organism | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-purulent** cellulitis / erysipelas | **β-haemolytic streptococci** (esp. group A) | β-lactam covers; MRSA cover **not** needed unless features suggest it |
| **Purulent** (abscess, furuncle, purulent cellulitis) | **_S. aureus_, incl. community MRSA** | **incision & drainage is primary**; add anti-staph/MRSA antibiotic by severity |
| **Diabetic foot / polymicrobial** | mixed aerobes + anaerobes, GNB | broad cover; assess for osteomyelitis |
| **Necrotizing** | group A strep, *Clostridium*, polymicrobial, *Vibrio vulnificus* (seawater/liver disease), *Aeromonas* (fresh water) | **surgical emergency** |

Erysipelas is a distinct, superficial (upper dermis/lymphatics) strep infection: **sharply demarcated, raised, fiery-red plaque**, often facial, with fever — versus cellulitis (deeper dermis/subcutis, ill-defined borders).

## 🩺 Presentation & severity

Spreading erythema, warmth, swelling, tenderness ± fever; regional lymphadenopathy and lymphangitic streaking; a portal of entry (tinea pedis, wound, ulcer, oedema). Usually **unilateral** — bilateral lower-leg "cellulitis" is more often stasis dermatitis/lipodermatosclerosis (a common misdiagnosis). Severity tiers (IDSA):
- **Mild** — local signs, no systemic features → oral, outpatient.
- **Moderate** — systemic signs (fever) → IV antibiotics.
- **Severe** — SIRS/sepsis, immunocompromise, failed oral therapy, or **signs of a deeper/necrotizing process** → IV broad-spectrum + surgical assessment.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance — necrotizing fasciitis
> **Pain out of proportion to visible skin findings**, rapid progression (hours), **systemic toxicity/shock**, tense oedema beyond the erythema, skin bullae/necrosis/dusky discolouration, **crepitus** (gas), or anaesthesia over the area. **This is a surgical emergency — call surgery for immediate exploration/debridement; do NOT delay for imaging or the LRINEC score.** The LRINEC score and CT/MRI may support the diagnosis but a high clinical suspicion overrides a low score.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Clinical diagnosis** for uncomplicated cellulitis — no routine bloods/imaging.
- **When moderate/severe or necrotizing suspected:** CBC, CRP, electrolytes, creatinine, glucose, CK (↑ in necrotizing myonecrosis), lactate, blood cultures; **culture any drained pus/abscess** (guides MRSA therapy).
- **Imaging:** US to find a drainable abscess; CT/MRI for deep/necrotizing extent or osteomyelitis — **but imaging must not delay surgery when necrotizing infection is clinically likely.**
- **LRINEC** (CRP, WBC, Hb, Na, creatinine, glucose) as an adjunct only.

## 🗂️ Differential diagnosis

Stasis dermatitis (bilateral, chronic, no fever), DVT ([[VTE (DVT & Pulmonary Embolism)]]), gout/septic arthritis, contact dermatitis, erythema migrans, and — critically — **necrotizing fasciitis masquerading as "bad cellulitis".**

## 💊 Management

**1. Non-purulent cellulitis/erysipelas (strep-directed):**
- **Mild → oral:** penicillin V, amoxicillin, cephalexin, or dicloxacillin.
- **Moderate → IV:** **cefazolin**, ceftriaxone, or **ampicillin** / penicillin G. MRSA cover only if risk factors.
- Elevate the limb, treat the portal of entry (antifungal for tinea pedis), and mark the erythema border to track progression.

**2. Purulent SSTI / abscess (staph-directed, MRSA-aware):**
- **Incision & drainage is the primary treatment** for an abscess.
- **Add antibiotics by severity:** mild often needs I&D alone; moderate/severe add **MRSA cover — co-trimoxazole or doxycycline (oral)**, or **vancomycin / clindamycin / linezolid (IV)**.

**3. Severe / necrotizing / diabetic-foot:**
- **Broad empiric IV: vancomycin (or linezolid) + piperacillin-tazobactam (or a carbapenem)** — covering MRSA, Gram-negatives, and anaerobes.
- **Necrotizing fasciitis → immediate surgical debridement** (the definitive therapy) + broad antibiotics **+ clindamycin** (toxin/protein-synthesis suppression) ± IVIG for streptococcal toxic shock; ICU support.
- **Duration:** uncomplicated cellulitis **5–6 days**, extend only if slow to respond; necrotizing infection until debridement is complete and the patient stabilises.

**4. Recurrence prevention:** treat chronic oedema/lymphoedema and tinea; consider prophylactic penicillin for frequent recurrences.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

All agents are on the Thai NLEM (penicillins, cefazolin/cephalexin, ceftriaxone, cloxacillin/dicloxacillin, clindamycin, co-trimoxazole, doxycycline, vancomycin, piperacillin-tazobactam, carbapenems). Thai-relevant necrotizing pathogens include ***Vibrio vulnificus*** (seawater exposure, raw seafood, cirrhosis → fulminant necrotizing infection needing doxycycline + ceftriaxone/ceftazidime and surgery) and ***Aeromonas*** (freshwater/paddy-field injuries); **melioidosis** can present as skin/soft-tissue abscess. Diabetic foot infection is a very common Thai ward SSTI requiring vascular + osteomyelitis assessment.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Purulent → S. aureus/MRSA → I&D first** ± MRSA drug (co-trimoxazole/doxycycline PO; vancomycin IV). **Non-purulent → strep → β-lactam** (cefazolin/penicillin).
> - **Necrotizing fasciitis = surgical emergency**: pain out of proportion, systemic toxicity, rapid progression, crepitus/bullae → **debride now, don't wait for imaging/LRINEC**; add **clindamycin**.
> - Bilateral lower-leg "cellulitis" is usually **stasis dermatitis** — reconsider.
> - Cellulitis duration **5–6 days**; treat the portal of entry (tinea) and oedema.
> - Thailand: seawater/cirrhosis → ***Vibrio vulnificus***; freshwater injury → *Aeromonas*.

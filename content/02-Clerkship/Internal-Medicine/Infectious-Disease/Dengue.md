# Dengue & Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever

A mosquito-borne (*Aedes aegypti*) flavivirus infection endemic across Thailand and the commonest cause of an acute febrile illness with thrombocytopenia in the Thai ER. The disease is defined not by the fever but by **plasma leakage** during the **critical phase**, which begins paradoxically as the fever falls. The clinical task is to **recognise the phase, monitor for warning signs and a rising haematocrit, and give just enough isotonic fluid** — under- and over-resuscitation both kill.

## 🧬 Virology & Pathophysiology

Four serotypes (DENV 1–4); infection gives lifelong serotype-specific immunity but only transient cross-protection. **Secondary infection with a different serotype carries the highest risk of severe dengue** through **antibody-dependent enhancement** (non-neutralising antibodies increase viral uptake) and a cytokine surge that transiently increases capillary permeability → **plasma leakage** into pleural and peritoneal spaces (haemoconcentration, effusions, hypovolaemic shock). Thrombocytopenia and coagulopathy arise from marrow suppression, immune platelet destruction, and consumption.

## 🩺 The three phases (this is the whole disease)

| Phase | Timing | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Febrile** | days 1–3 | high fever, headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia/arthralgia ("break-bone"), flushing, rash; **positive tourniquet test**; falling platelets & WBC |
| **Critical** | **days ~3–7, at/just after defervescence** | **plasma leakage over 24–48 h** → rising Hct, effusions/ascites, warning signs, shock (DSS), bleeding, organ impairment |
| **Recovery** | after ~48 h of leakage | reabsorption of extravasated fluid → Hct falls, diuresis, **"convalescent" confluent rash with islands of sparing**; **risk of fluid overload/pulmonary oedema if IV fluids not stopped** |

> [!note] 💎 Pearls
> - **Defervescence is the danger point, not the fever** — the patient looks "better" as leakage begins. Watch hardest on days 3–7.
> - **A rising haematocrit with a falling platelet count and a narrowing pulse pressure** = plasma leakage/impending shock, even before hypotension.
> - **"Islands of white in a sea of red"** convalescent rash = recovery phase → stop IV fluids to avoid overload.

## WHO classification & warning signs

- **Dengue without warning signs** — fever + 2 of: nausea/vomiting, rash, aches, positive tourniquet, leukopenia, any warning sign absent.
- **Dengue WITH warning signs** — **abdominal pain/tenderness · persistent vomiting · clinical fluid accumulation (effusion/ascites) · mucosal bleeding · lethargy/restlessness · hepatomegaly > 2 cm · rising Hct with rapidly falling platelets.** → admit and observe closely.
- **Severe dengue** — any of: **severe plasma leakage** (shock [DSS] or respiratory distress from fluid accumulation), **severe bleeding**, or **severe organ impairment** (AST/ALT ≥ 1000, impaired consciousness, myocarditis/heart failure).

The older **WHO 1997 DHF grading** is still used in Thai practice: **DHF I** (fever + leakage evidence + positive tourniquet), **II** (+ spontaneous bleeding), **III** (circulatory failure — narrow pulse pressure ≤ 20 mmHg, cold clammy) and **IV** (profound shock, unrecordable BP) = **dengue shock syndrome (DSS)**. DHF requires **thrombocytopenia (≤ 100,000) + evidence of plasma leakage** (Hct rise ≥ 20%, effusion/ascites, or hypoalbuminaemia) — the leakage is what separates DHF from dengue fever.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **CBC serially** — the workhorse. Track **haematocrit** (baseline then q6–12h in critical phase; a ≥ 20% rise = leakage) and **platelets** (nadir in critical phase); leukopenia early.
- **Dengue diagnostics:** **NS1 antigen** (positive days 1–5, early), **dengue IgM** (from ~day 4–5), IgG (rising in secondary infection). PCR where available.
- **Tourniquet test** (inflate cuff to midway between SBP/DBP for 5 min; ≥ 10–20 petechiae per inch² = positive) — supportive early.
- AST/ALT (often raised; ≥ 1000 = severe), albumin, coagulation if bleeding, electrolytes, glucose; CXR/US for effusions/ascites (right lateral decubitus CXR is sensitive).
- **Exclude the mimics** endemic in Thailand: leptospirosis, malaria (blood film), scrub typhus, chikungunya, typhoid, melioidosis, and (early) sepsis.

## 💊 Management — supportive, phase- and Hct-guided

There is no antiviral; **management is fluid and monitoring** matched to the phase.

**Febrile phase / dengue without warning signs (outpatient possible):**
- **Oral fluids**; **paracetamol** for fever (max ~2.5–3 g/day per weight). **Strictly avoid NSAIDs and aspirin** (bleeding, gastritis) and **avoid IM injections** (haematoma).
- Return-precaution education; daily CBC + review during days 3–7 (critical window).

**Dengue with warning signs / DHF I–II → admit, isotonic crystalloid:**
- **Isotonic crystalloid (0.9% NaCl or Ringer's lactate)**, start ~**5–7 mL/kg/hr** and **titrate to Hct, urine output (≥ 0.5 mL/kg/hr), and vitals**, stepping *down* as the patient stabilises (typically over 24–48 h). **Give the minimum fluid to maintain perfusion** — the leak is time-limited.

**Severe dengue / DSS (DHF III–IV) → resuscitate:**
- **Compensated shock (DHF III):** isotonic crystalloid **bolus 10–20 mL/kg over ~1 h**, reassess, titrate down as Hct falls and perfusion returns.
- **Hypotensive shock (DHF IV):** rapid crystalloid **20 mL/kg bolus**; if no response and Hct remains high, escalate to a **colloid**; **if Hct falls with continued shock → occult haemorrhage → transfuse blood**, not more crystalloid.
- **Platelet transfusion is NOT given for a low count alone** — only for significant/active bleeding or before an invasive procedure; prophylactic platelets do not help and add risk.

**Recovery phase — STOP the IV fluids.** As leaked fluid returns to the circulation, the Hct falls and the patient auto-diureses; continued IV fluids cause **pulmonary oedema/effusions**. Watch for fluid overload; support until platelets recover.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

Dengue is hyperendemic in Thailand with seasonal (rainy-season) surges; NS1 and IgM/IgG rapid tests, serial CBC, and isotonic crystalloid are universally available, and management follows the **Thai/WHO SEARO dengue guidelines** (the DHF I–IV grading remains in routine Thai use alongside the WHO 2009 categories). The **CYD-TDV (Dengvaxia)** vaccine is restricted to the seropositive; the newer **TAK-003 (Qdenga)** is available in parts of Thailand. Co-circulating chikungunya and Zika share the vector; leptospirosis and scrub typhus are the key look-alikes that (unlike dengue) **do** need antibiotics (doxycycline) — see [[Tropical Infectious Diseases]].

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **NO NSAIDs, NO aspirin, NO IM injections** in suspected dengue (bleeding risk).
> - **Defervescence = critical phase** — the patient who "improves" as the fever breaks may be leaking; monitor Hct + warning signs on days 3–7.
> - **Warning signs (abdo pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleed, lethargy, effusion/ascites, rising Hct + falling platelets)** → admit.
> - **Hct falling during ongoing shock = internal bleeding → transfuse blood**, do not just give more crystalloid.
> - **Recovery phase → STOP IV fluids** (overload/pulmonary oedema); **don't transfuse platelets for count alone.**

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Three phases: **febrile (1–3) → critical/leakage at defervescence (3–7) → recovery (stop fluids)**.
> - **Monitor Hct + platelets serially**; **Hct ↑ ≥ 20% = plasma leakage**; DHF = thrombocytopenia ≤ 100k **+** leakage.
> - Fluids = **isotonic crystalloid titrated to Hct/UOP/vitals**; DSS bolus 10–20 mL/kg; colloid/blood if refractory with falling Hct.
> - **Paracetamol only — no NSAID/aspirin/IM**; **platelets only for bleeding**, not count.
> - Dx: **NS1 early (d1–5), IgM from d4–5**; exclude lepto/malaria/scrub typhus.

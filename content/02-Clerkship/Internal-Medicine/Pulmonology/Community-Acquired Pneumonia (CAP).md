# Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

Acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside hospital, defined by a new infiltrate on imaging plus a compatible acute febrile respiratory illness. This page is adult CAP; hospital-acquired/ventilator-associated pneumonia (HAP/VAP) and aspiration pneumonia are managed on the broader [[Pneumonia]] order. The two decisions that drive outcome are **severity-based site of care** (home vs ward vs ICU) and **timely, correctly-targeted empiric antibiotics**; in Thailand a third decision is whether to **cover melioidosis**.

## 🧬 Microbiology & Pathophysiology

Organisms reach the alveoli by micro-aspiration of colonising flora, inhalation of aerosol, or haematogenous spread; disease follows when the inoculum or virulence overwhelms mucociliary and innate defences, producing the alveolar exudate seen as consolidation. Pathogen distribution shapes empiric therapy:

| Group | Organisms | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| Typical bacterial | *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (most common overall), *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella*, *S. aureus* (post-influenza) | abrupt fever, rigors, productive cough, lobar consolidation |
| Atypical | *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*, *Legionella* | gradual onset, dry cough, extrapulmonary features (headache, diarrhoea, ↑LFT, hyponatraemia for Legionella) |
| Viral | Influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV | seasonality, bilateral interstitial pattern; bacterial co-infection possible |
| Aspiration/anaerobic | oral anaerobes, Gram-negatives | impaired consciousness, dysphagia, alcohol, dependent-segment infiltrate |
| Gram-negative / MDR | *Klebsiella*, *Pseudomonas*, MRSA | structural lung disease, recent antibiotics/hospitalisation, prior isolation |
| **Thailand-specific** | **_Burkholderia pseudomallei_ (melioidosis)**, *Klebsiella*, TB, scrub typhus/leptospirosis (AFI mimics), avian influenza | **diabetes + NE Thailand + severe CAP/sepsis**; consider TB in subacute/cavitary |

> [!note] 💎 Pearls
> - **Post-influenza pneumonia** = think *S. aureus* (incl. MRSA) and *S. pneumoniae* — a biphasic course (flu, brief recovery, then worsening).
> - **Legionella** — high fever, relative bradycardia, diarrhoea, confusion, **hyponatraemia**, ↑LFT, failure to respond to β-lactam; diagnose with **urine antigen** (detects serogroup 1).
> - **Melioidosis** is the Thai twist: a diabetic from the north-east with severe CAP/sepsis, multi-lobar or cavitary disease — empiric **ceftazidime or a carbapenem** covers it; standard CAP β-lactams do not.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Cough (± purulent sputum), fever/chills, dyspnoea, pleuritic chest pain; exam shows tachypnoea, focal crackles, bronchial breath sounds, dullness and increased tactile fremitus over consolidation, or a pleural rub. **Elderly and immunocompromised patients may lack fever and present only with confusion, falls, tachypnoea, or decompensated comorbidity** — a low threshold for imaging is essential. Tachypnoea (RR ≥ 30), hypoxaemia (SpO₂ < 92%), hypotension, and altered mentation are the bedside markers of severity.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Chest imaging — required to confirm.** CXR (PA + lateral) shows the infiltrate and complications (effusion, cavitation, multilobar). **Lung ultrasound is an acceptable alternative to CXR where expertise exists (2025 ATS update)** and outperforms CXR for effusion. CT if slow response, suspected empyema/abscess, or diagnostic doubt.
- **CBC, BUN/creatinine, electrolytes, LFT, glucose** — severity (urea for CURB-65) and comorbidity; hyponatraemia/↑LFT hint Legionella.
- **Procalcitonin** — supports bacterial vs viral and guides duration/de-escalation; **do not use it to withhold initial antibiotics** in clear CAP.
- **Microbiology for severe/inpatient CAP:** blood cultures ×2 before antibiotics, sputum Gram stain + culture (good sample: > 25 PMN, < 10 epithelial/LPF), **urine pneumococcal and Legionella antigens**, respiratory viral PCR (influenza, SARS-CoV-2). In Thailand add **blood/sputum culture for melioidosis** and consider TB workup (AFB/Xpert) when subacute or cavitary. HIV testing where risk warrants.
- **ABG/VBG** if SpO₂ < 92% or RR > 30.

## Severity assessment & site of care

Two complementary tools decide **where** the patient is treated; the ATS/IDSA severe-CAP criteria decide **ICU**.

- **CURB-65** (1 point each): **C**onfusion · **U**rea > 7 mmol/L (BUN > 19 mg/dL) · **R**espiratory rate ≥ 30 · **B**P (SBP < 90 or DBP ≤ 60) · age ≥ **65**. Score **0–1 → outpatient**, **2 → admit (short-stay/ward)**, **≥ 3 → severe, consider ICU**. CRB-65 (no urea) is the clinic/ED version.
- **PSI/PORT** (five risk classes) is better validated for identifying low-risk patients safe for home (classes I–II) but is cumbersome.
- **ATS/IDSA severe CAP = 1 major OR ≥ 3 minor criteria:**
  - **Major:** mechanical ventilation; septic shock needing vasopressors.
  - **Minor:** RR ≥ 30 · PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 250 · multilobar infiltrates · confusion · **uraemia (BUN ≥ 20 mg/dL)** · leukopenia (< 4000) · thrombocytopenia (< 100k) · hypothermia (< 36 °C) · hypotension requiring aggressive fluids.

Scores support but do not replace clinical judgement (hypoxaemia, decompensated comorbidity, or social factors can justify admission at CURB-65 0–1).

## 🗂️ Differential diagnosis

Congestive heart failure/pulmonary oedema ([[ADHF]]), [[VTE (DVT & Pulmonary Embolism)\|pulmonary embolism]] with infarction, aspiration pneumonitis (chemical, not infective), pulmonary TB, lung malignancy with post-obstructive pneumonia, [[COPD Exacerbation]], organising pneumonia and other ILD, and — in Thailand — the acute febrile illnesses (dengue, leptospirosis, scrub typhus, melioidosis) that overlap with CAP. **A non-resolving "pneumonia" in an older smoker demands a follow-up CXR at 6 weeks to exclude an obstructing tumour.**

## 💊 Management

**Give empiric antibiotics promptly** (goal within 4 h of diagnosis; within 1 h if septic shock), after cultures where indicated. Regimens below follow ATS/IDSA 2019 (empiric choices unchanged in the 2025 update) with the Thai NLEM/RCPT flavour; add melioidosis cover where the risk applies.

**Outpatient (CURB-65 0–1, no comorbidity):**
- **Amoxicillin 1 g PO tid**, or **doxycycline 100 mg PO bid**, or a macrolide (azithromycin) only where local pneumococcal macrolide resistance is low.
- **With comorbidity** (chronic heart/lung/liver/renal disease, diabetes, alcohol, asplenia): **amoxicillin-clavulanate or a cephalosporin + a macrolide**, or **respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy** (levofloxacin 750 mg PO OD / moxifloxacin 400 mg PO OD).

**Inpatient, non-severe (ward):**
- **β-lactam (ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV OD, or ampicillin-sulbactam) + a macrolide (azithromycin 500 mg IV/PO OD)**, OR **respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy (levofloxacin 750 mg IV OD)**.

**Inpatient, severe (ICU):**
- **β-lactam (ceftriaxone/cefotaxime) + either a macrolide (azithromycin) or a respiratory fluoroquinolone** — do not use fluoroquinolone monotherapy alone in the ICU. (β-lactam + macrolide is favoured for its mortality signal in severe CAP.)
- **Add MRSA cover** (vancomycin or linezolid) **only** with locally-validated risk factors (prior MRSA isolation, post-influenza necrotising pneumonia).
- **Add antipseudomonal cover** (piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h, cefepime 2 g IV q8h, or meropenem) **only** with risk factors (structural lung disease/bronchiectasis, prior *Pseudomonas*, recent broad-spectrum antibiotics).
- **Thailand:** in a diabetic/immunocompromised patient with severe CAP from an endemic area, **cover melioidosis** — **ceftazidime 2 g IV q8h or meropenem 1 g IV q8h** in the acute (intensive) phase, then oral eradication (co-trimoxazole) for months once confirmed.

**Antiviral:** **oseltamivir 75 mg PO bid × 5 days** if influenza is suspected/confirmed, ideally within 48 h (still give in severe/hospitalised disease beyond 48 h).

**Corticosteroid for severe CAP:** the **2025 ATS update suggests systemic corticosteroids** in adult inpatients with severe CAP, driven by **CAPE COD (2023)**: **hydrocortisone 200 mg/day IV** (continuous infusion or 50 mg q6h) for days 1–4, then taper to a total of 8–14 days by response, started within ~24 h. Do **not** give steroids in non-severe CAP, in likely influenza, or where a fungal/viral cause makes them hazardous.

**Duration & de-escalation:** **de-escalate to targeted therapy on culture data** and **switch IV → PO once clinically stable, afebrile, and tolerating oral (usually 48–72 h).** Total course is short: **outpatient and non-severe inpatient CAP reaching stability need only 3 to < 5 days**; **severe CAP ≥ 5 days**; longer (7–14 days) for Legionella, complications, or specific organisms (much longer for melioidosis). Procalcitonin can support stopping. Follow-up CXR at 6 weeks in smokers/older adults to confirm resolution and exclude malignancy.

## 📚 Landmark evidence

- **CAPE COD (2023, NEJM)** — in severe CAP, IV hydrocortisone reduced 28-day mortality and the need for intubation/vasopressors → basis of the 2025 steroid recommendation.
- **ATS/IDSA CAP 2019** — three-tier framework (outpatient/inpatient non-severe/severe), abandonment of the "HCAP" category, MRSA/*Pseudomonas* cover only by risk.
- **2025 ATS update** — shorter durations (min 3 days, < 5 if stable), lung ultrasound as an imaging alternative, steroids for severe CAP.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & Thai CPG

All first-line agents are on the Thai NLEM (amoxicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, doxycycline, azithromycin, ceftriaxone, levofloxacin, piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, meropenem, vancomycin, oseltamivir). Thai practice follows the ATS/IDSA severity-and-site framework and the RCPT antimicrobial guidance, with one important local addition: **melioidosis must be considered in the empiric plan for severe CAP/sepsis in diabetics and residents of endemic (especially north-eastern) Thailand**, where ceftazidime/carbapenem replaces the standard β-lactam. TB is a constant differential and confounds "non-resolving pneumonia". Fluoroquinolone use is tempered by its activity against TB (can mask/partially treat it and delay diagnosis) and by resistance stewardship.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Severe CAP (1 major or ≥ 3 minor criteria) or septic shock** → ICU, cultures + antibiotics within 1 h, consider hydrocortisone (see [[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)]]).
> - **Do not miss melioidosis** in a diabetic with severe CAP from endemic Thailand — standard ceftriaxone does not cover *B. pseudomallei*.
> - **Non-resolving pneumonia** (no improvement at 48–72 h, or a persistent infiltrate in a smoker) → reassess for empyema/abscess, TB, or obstructing malignancy — repeat imaging/CT, follow-up CXR at 6 weeks.
> - **Do not add MRSA or antipseudomonal cover reflexively** — only with validated risk factors; unnecessary broad-spectrum therapy drives resistance.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **CURB-65**: 0–1 home · 2 admit · ≥ 3 ICU-consider. Confusion / Urea > 7 / RR ≥ 30 / BP low / age ≥ 65.
> - Empiric: outpatient **amoxicillin/doxycycline**; ward **β-lactam + macrolide** or **levofloxacin**; ICU **β-lactam + macrolide** (never FQ alone), MRSA/*Pseudomonas* only by risk.
> - **Thailand: cover melioidosis (ceftazidime/meropenem)** in severe CAP + diabetes/endemic area.
> - **Severe CAP → hydrocortisone 200 mg/day IV × ~4 days then taper (CAPE COD)**; **oseltamivir** if influenza.
> - Duration is **short**: stable → 3–< 5 days; severe ≥ 5 days. IV→PO at 48–72 h. Follow-up CXR at 6 wk in smokers.

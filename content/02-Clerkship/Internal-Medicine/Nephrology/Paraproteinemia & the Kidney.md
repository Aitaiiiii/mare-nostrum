# Paraproteinemia & the Kidney (Monoclonal Gammopathy Kidney Disease / MGRS)

This is the **Nephrology** view of paraproteinemia — how a monoclonal immunoglobulin or its light/heavy chain injures the kidney, how the lesions are classified, and how they are diagnosed and treated. The haematologic diseases that generate the paraprotein live on their own pages: [[Multiple Myeloma (MM)]], [[MGUS, Smoldering MM & MGRS]], and [[POEMS Syndrome & AL Amyloidosis]]; the systemic amyloid picture is on [[POEMS Syndrome & AL Amyloidosis]] and [[Cardiac Amyloidosis (AL-ATTR)]]. Frame every case around one question the whole field turns on: **is the clone big enough to be a cancer (myeloma-defining), or is it a small clone whose only crime is a nephrotoxic protein (MGRS)?** — because that decides both the workup and who treats the patient.

## 🧬 Overview

Paraproteinemia is any disease that produces a **monoclonal immunoglobulin** (M-protein) or its fragments. Plasma cells and B cells normally make more light chain than heavy chain, and more κ than λ; the free light chains that spill over are small enough to be filtered and are the usual nephrotoxin. A **monoclonal** gammopathy (one clone, one identical Ig — a spike) is the concern here and points to a clonal disorder; a **polyclonal** gammopathy (many clones, from infection/autoimmunity) does not cause these lesions. The clonal sources that damage the kidney span plasma-cell disorders (multiple myeloma ~60% of paraprotein-related kidney disease, Waldenström, smouldering MM, AL amyloidosis, MGRS) and B-cell disorders (MBL, CLL, low-grade NHL, MGRS).

The mechanism is nephrotoxicity of the monoclonal protein, in one of three ways: **direct deposition** of the Ig/light chain in a renal compartment, **complement activation** (C3 glomerulopathy, some TMA), or **precipitation/aggregation** (cast nephropathy, cryoglobulin, crystals). Where the protein lands sets the syndrome — glomerulus → proteinuria/nephrotic or nephritic; tubule → tubular dysfunction and AKI/CKD; vessel → hypertension and TMA.

## 🧭 Two buckets: myeloma-defining renal disease vs MGRS

Both start from a monoclonal clone, but they are managed by different teams and thresholds.

- **Myeloma-defining renal disease** — the clone already meets **tumour-burden or CRAB/myeloma-defining criteria**, so the renal lesion is a myeloma-defining event (MDE). The archetype is **light-chain cast nephropathy**, which is by definition a feature of overt myeloma, **not** MGRS.
- **MGRS (Monoclonal Gammopathy of Renal Significance)** — an **IKMG** concept: a kidney lesion caused by a monoclonal Ig **AND** an underlying B-cell/plasma-cell clone that does **not** meet the criteria for a specific overt malignancy needing treatment. The clone is small (MGUS/smouldering-MM/smouldering-WM/MBL/low-grade CLL/NHL range) but the protein is nephrotoxic. IKMG has progressively **expanded** MGRS to include all such low-grade clones producing a kidney-damaging M-protein. MGRS matters because it is "malignant behaviour from a benign-looking clone": poor renal outcome (a large fraction reach ESKD), high recurrence after transplant, and it responds to clone-directed therapy that a "MGUS — just watch" label would have withheld.

**CRAB / myeloma-defining criteria** (why a clone counts as myeloma): **C**alcium >11 mg/dL (or >1 above ULN), **R**enal insufficiency (SCr >2 mg/dL or CrCl <40 mL/min from the myeloma), **A**naemia (Hb <10 or >2 below ULN), **B**one lytic lesions/pathologic fracture — see [[Multiple Myeloma (MM)]] for the full IMWG SLiM-CRAB set.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

The monoclonal protein presents as one of four renal syndromes, and the pattern narrows the lesion before the biopsy comes back:

| Syndrome | Lesions that cause it |
|---|---|
| **Nephrotic** (heavy proteinuria) | AL amyloidosis (glomerular), MIDD, immunotactoid glomerulopathy, PGNMID |
| **Nephritic** (haematuria, HT, ↓eGFR) | Cryoglobulinemic GN, PGNMID, C3 glomerulopathy, immunotactoid |
| **AKI / AKD** | **Light-chain cast nephropathy** (the big one — a myeloma-defining lesion, not MGRS), TMA, MIDD |
| **Tubular / CKD** | Light-chain proximal tubulopathy (± Fanconi), crystal-storing histiocytosis, amyloid (vascular/tubulointerstitial), TMA |

Two clinical fingerprints are worth committing to memory, contrasting the two commonest patterns:

> [!note] 💎 Pearls — presentation (Thai mnemonics kept verbatim)
> - **Cast nephropathy — "ไตวายมากกก albuminuria น้อย"** — severe kidney failure with *little albuminuria*: the filtered protein is overflow free light chain, so the urine dipstick (albumin) badly under-reads the 24-h protein. A big dipstick–vs–24-h gap in an older patient with unexplained AKI is myeloma until proven otherwise.
> - **AL amyloidosis — "ไต ไม่ค่อยวาย Proteinuria เยอะมากกก"** — kidney function relatively preserved but *massive* (albuminuric, nephrotic-range) proteinuria; look for the systemic tells — macroglossia, periorbital purpura, shoulder-pad sign, autonomic neuropathy, HFpEF/restrictive cardiomyopathy.
> - **Clues that turn "AKI + anaemia" into myeloma:** hypercalcaemia with a *high* phosphate in renal failure, anaemia out of proportion to the renal impairment, bone pain with lytic lesions but a **normal ALP**, and a **low anion gap** (IgG paraprotein) — plus SFLC κ:λ far off with a level >500 mg/L.

## 🩻 Investigations & diagnosis

Screen for the paraprotein, then biopsy to name the lesion — you cannot tell MGRS subtypes apart clinically.

- **SPEP + UPEP** (serum/urine protein electrophoresis) — screen; detects a monoclonal free light chain roughly when SFLC ≥500 mg/L.
- **Immunofixation electrophoresis (IFE)** — more sensitive, detects the monoclonal band down to SFLC ≈150 mg/L, and types it (which heavy/light chain).
- **Serum free light chain (SFLC) assay — the most sensitive**, and quantitative. Interpret the **κ:λ ratio**, not the absolute: normal renal function **0.26–1.65**, but in AKI/CKD the reference widens to **0.37–3.17** because both chains are retained (do not overcall a mildly high ratio in a failing kidney). An involved:uninvolved ratio far outside range, plus a rising involved chain, is the clonal signal and the way response is tracked.
- **Kidney biopsy is essential** to distinguish the MGRS lesions — light microscopy (pattern + Congo red), immunofluorescence (monotypic light-chain restriction, or its absence), and electron microscopy (deposit architecture and fibril/microtubule size). A haematologic workup (marrow, flow, imaging) then sizes the clone to sort myeloma-defining from MGRS.

## 🗂️ Multiple myeloma and the kidney

Myeloma injures the kidney at every level; only some are direct paraprotein lesions.

- **Pre-renal** — hypercalcaemia (afferent vasoconstriction, nephrogenic DI), volume depletion, and nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, diuretics, contrast).
- **Renal (paraprotein lesions)** — **light-chain cast nephropathy 40–63%** (a myeloma-defining lesion), **light-chain proximal tubulopathy ~40%** (κ ~95%, ± Fanconi), **AL amyloidosis 7–30%**, **MIDD ~20%**, **cryoglobulinemia <1%**; plus ATN, TLS/acute uric-acid nephropathy, and FSGS (pamidronate). Direct plasma-cell infiltration is rare.
- **Post-renal** — intraluminal stones (Ca/uric acid), extraluminal plasmacytoma.
- **From the paraprotein load itself** — pseudohyponatraemia and a spurious non-anion-gap picture from high globulin, and **hyperviscosity** (IgM/Waldenström, IgA myeloma).

**Light-chain cast nephropathy** (myeloma kidney) is the one to know cold: filtered free light chain binds **uromodulin (Tamm–Horsfall protein)** in the **distal** tubule to form obstructing casts. LM shows fractured, eosinophilic, PAS-pale casts with a **giant-cell reaction** and interstitial inflammation; IF shows κ or λ restriction. It presents as AKI (~50%) with minimal albuminuria (overflow proteinuria), often precipitated by volume depletion, NSAIDs, hypercalcaemia, or contrast — so the first treatment is to remove precipitants and rehydrate, in parallel with clone-directed therapy.

## 🔬 The MGRS lesions (kidney-biopsy classification)

MGRS lesions are sorted by whether the deposits are **organized** (fibrils, microtubules, or crystals — usually IF-negative for a single monoclonal Ig on routine stains, needing EM/special work) or **non-organized** (granular, monotypic on IF). A clean recall hook for the *organized* ones, in ascending EM fibril/microtubule size:

> [!tip] 🎯 Fibril-size mnemonic — **A-F-C-I → 10-20-30-40 nm**
> **A**myloid ~8–12 nm · **F**ibrillary GN ~10–30 (median ~20) · **C**ryoglobulinemic ~20–35 (median ~30) · **I**mmunotactoid ~20–60 (median ~40). Amyloid is the only Congo-red-positive one.

| Lesion | Category | IF | EM (arrangement, size) | Congo red | Clinical | Clone-directed Rx |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AL amyloidosis** | Organized, fibrillar | λ-restriction (λ:κ ≈3:1; ~94% LC) | random non-branching fibrils **8–12 nm** | **+** (apple-green birefringence) | nephrotic 60–70%, AKI low; systemic (heart/liver/nerve) | anti-plasma-cell (± ASCT); see [[POEMS Syndrome & AL Amyloidosis]] |
| **Fibrillary GN** | Organized, fibrillar | *polyclonal* IgG4 (smudgy), C3, κ&λ; **DNAJB9 +** | random non-branching fibrils **10–30 nm** | − | GN (HT, ↓eGFR, proteinuria) | treat by LM pattern; often not clonal |
| **Immunotactoid glomerulopathy** | Organized, microtubular | monotypic **IgG1**, κ>λ | parallel-stacked hollow microtubules **20–60 nm** | − | nephrotic/GN, low C3/C4; a/w CLL/lymphoma | treat the lymphoproliferative clone |
| **Cryoglobulinemic GN (type I, II)** | Organized, microtubular | IgMκ (type I) or polyclonal IgG + monoclonal IgMκ (type II) | curvilinear parallel microtubules **20–35 nm**, capillary cryo-plugs | − | nephritic, **cryocrit ≥2%**, ↓C4>C3; a/w HCV/B-cell clone | rituximab + steroid ± PLEX |
| **Light-chain proximal tubulopathy (± Fanconi)** | Organized, crystalline/inclusion | κ (crystalline ~95%) in proximal tubular cells | rhomboid crystals in PCT | − | proximal RTA ± Fanconi, slow CKD | clone-directed |
| **Crystal-storing histiocytosis** | Organized, crystalline | LC crystals in histiocytes + PCT | rhomboid crystals in histiocytes | − | tubulointerstitial; extrarenal (spleen, LN, cornea) | clone-directed |
| **MIDD** (LCDD 70% / HCDD 20% / LHCDD 10%; κ ~75%) | Non-organized | **linear** LC (κ>λ) along GBM/TBM/vessels | granular "gunpowder/punctate" deposits | − | nodular glomerulosclerosis, HT 50–80%, proteinuria 1.8–2.4 g/d (NS 20–40%), AKI ~56% | bortezomib-based ± ASCT |
| **PGNMID** | Non-organized | monotypic **IgG3κ > IgG1κ**, granular in glomeruli | discrete granular electron-dense deposits (mesangial/subendo/subepi) | − | nephritic > nephrotic, CKD | rituximab or bortezomib/cyclophosphamide-based |

C3 glomerulopathy and TMA can also be driven by a monoclonal Ig **without** Ig deposits on IF ("MGRS with no monoclonal Ig deposit") — the paraprotein acts through complement, so screen for a clone in unexplained C3 GN or TMA in an older adult.

## 💊 Management

The principle is uniform: **clone-directed therapy to shut off the pathogenic protein**, matched to the cell of origin (plasma-cell vs B-cell), plus supportive nephrology. Renal pathology is required first, because the treatment follows the clone, not the creatinine.

- **Myeloma cast nephropathy / MM-renal (IMWG 2023).** Start a **bortezomib-based** backbone early with high-dose dexamethasone and rapid volume/precipitant correction; the earlier the free light chain falls, the better the renal recovery. **Daratumumab** now moves renal outcomes further — early Dara-containing regimens (Dara-VCd/Isa-VCd in fit patients, Dara-Vd in frail) give renal-response rates around 76% vs ~45% with VCd historically. Renal-dose the cytotoxics (**melphalan, lenalidomide**); bortezomib and daratumumab need no renal adjustment.
- **Extracorporeal free-light-chain removal is adjunctive and unsettled.** **HCO-HD / extended HD** and plasma exchange lower circulating FLC, but the RCTs disagree: **EuLITE (2019)** was negative (dialysis-independence 56% HCO vs 51% high-flux, with more infections in the HCO arm), while **MYRE** found higher renal recovery with intensive HCO-HD on a bortezomib backbone. Use it as an add-on to effective chemotherapy in biopsy-proven cast nephropathy with dialysis-dependent AKI, not as a substitute for treating the clone.
- **Hyperviscosity** (symptomatic, or as Rituximab prophylaxis in IgM disease) → **therapeutic plasma exchange** (ASFA 2023 Category I for symptomatic hyperviscosity). Rough paraprotein thresholds that flag risk: **IgM >4 g/dL, IgG >6 g/dL, IgA >6 g/dL**.
- **MGRS** — treat clone-directed even though tumour criteria are not met, because renal outcome and transplant recurrence are driven by the protein; the goal is **clearance of the monoclonal gammopathy** (a deep haematologic response). Regimen follows the clone: plasma-cell clone → bortezomib/daratumumab-based ± ASCT; B-cell/CLL clone → rituximab-based.
- **Kidney transplantation** — defer until the clone is controlled, because recurrence is high: **PGNMID and MIDD recur in ~80–90%**, PGNMID within weeks–6 months and MIDD (and AL) over years; immunotactoid, fibrillary GN, and cryoglobulinemic GN also recur readily.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Unexplained AKI/CKD + anaemia + hypercalcaemia in an adult >50 → screen for a paraprotein (SPEP/UPEP + SFLC) before blaming ATN or "pre-renal".** Missing cast nephropathy costs nephrons fast.
> - **A normal urine albumin/dipstick does not exclude myeloma kidney** — cast nephropathy is overflow free-light-chain proteinuria; order SFLC and UPEP, not just albuminuria.
> - **Do not label a nephrotoxic small clone "MGUS, observe"** — if there is a monoclonal-Ig kidney lesion, it is MGRS and needs clone-directed treatment, not watchful waiting.
> - **Biopsy before treating** — the MGRS subtypes look alike clinically and are treated differently; and renal-dose melphalan/lenalidomide to avoid toxicity.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Every case: **myeloma-defining renal disease (cast nephropathy) vs MGRS** (nephrotoxic clone *not* meeting tumour criteria) — different thresholds, same clone-directed logic.
> - Workup — **SPEP/UPEP + IFE + SFLC ratio** (κ:λ 0.26–1.65 normal; 0.37–3.17 in AKI/CKD) then **kidney biopsy** (LM/IF/EM); SFLC is the most sensitive and tracks response.
> - Cast nephropathy — filtered LC + **uromodulin** casts in the **distal** tubule; AKI with minimal albuminuria; bortezomib-based ± daratumumab early + remove precipitants; FLC-removal (HCO-HD/PLEX) adjunctive and unproven (EuLITE − / MYRE +).
> - Fibril sizes — **A-F-C-I 10-20-30-40 nm**; only amyloid is Congo-red positive.
> - MGRS transplant recurrence is high (**PGNMID/MIDD ~80–90%**) — control the clone first.

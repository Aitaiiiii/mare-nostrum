# Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

An abrupt (hours–days) decline in glomerular filtration causing retention of nitrogenous waste and dysregulation of fluid, electrolytes, and acid-base. AKI is a **syndrome, not a diagnosis** — the work is to **stage it, find and reverse the cause, and manage the life-threatening consequences** (hyperkalaemia, acidosis, volume overload, uraemia) while protecting the kidney from further injury.

## Definition & KDIGO staging

**AKI = any one of:** ↑ serum creatinine **≥ 0.3 mg/dL within 48 h**, OR ↑ creatinine **≥ 1.5× baseline within 7 days**, OR **urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 6 h.**

| Stage | Creatinine | Urine output |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1.5–1.9× baseline, or ↑ ≥ 0.3 mg/dL | < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 h |
| **2** | 2.0–2.9× baseline | < 0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥ 12 h |
| **3** | ≥ 3× baseline, or ≥ 4.0 mg/dL, or **RRT started** | < 0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥ 24 h, or anuria ≥ 12 h |

## 🧬 Aetiology — the pre-/intrinsic/post-renal framework

| Category | Mechanism | Examples | Clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-renal** (commonest) | ↓ renal perfusion, tubules intact | hypovolaemia, haemorrhage, sepsis, heart failure, cirrhosis (hepatorenal), NSAIDs/ACEi/ARB altering glomerular haemodynamics | FeNa < 1%, urine Na < 20, bland sediment, responds to volume |
| **Intrinsic** | parenchymal injury | **ATN** (ischaemia, sepsis, contrast, rhabdomyolysis, nephrotoxins), AIN (drugs — PPI, β-lactams, NSAIDs), glomerulonephritis, vascular (TMA) | FeNa > 2%, muddy-brown casts (ATN), WBC/eosinophils + rash (AIN), dysmorphic RBC/casts (GN) |
| **Post-renal** | obstruction | BPH, stones, pelvic malignancy, blocked catheter, retroperitoneal fibrosis | anuria/fluctuating output, hydronephrosis on US — **always exclude, it is reversible** |

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Serum creatinine + urea trend, electrolytes (K⁺!), venous gas/HCO₃, calcium/phosphate, CBC.**
- **Urinalysis + microscopy** — the cheapest, most informative test: **muddy-brown granular casts (ATN)**, RBC casts/dysmorphic RBC (GN), WBC/eosinophils (AIN), bland (pre-/post-renal).
- **Urine electrolytes / FeNa** (FeUrea if on diuretics) to separate pre-renal from ATN.
- **Renal ultrasound** to exclude **obstruction** (and assess size/echogenicity for chronicity) — mandatory in unexplained AKI.
- **Fluid/volume assessment**; medication review for nephrotoxins; CK + urine myoglobin if rhabdomyolysis suspected; immunology (ANCA, anti-GBM, complement) if GN suspected.

## 💊 Management

**1. Treat the cause and restore perfusion.** **Isotonic crystalloid** to a euvolaemic target for hypovolaemic/pre-renal AKI (avoid over-resuscitation and starch colloids); **relieve obstruction** (catheter, nephrostomy/stent) for post-renal; treat sepsis, stop the offending drug for AIN, and give disease-specific therapy for GN/TMA.

**2. Stop and avoid nephrotoxins.** Hold **NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, aminoglycosides, contrast (or minimise), and other nephrotoxins**; **renally dose-adjust all drugs**; avoid iodinated contrast where feasible.

**3. Manage the dangerous complications.**
- **Hyperkalaemia** — the acute killer: ECG, calcium gluconate (membrane stabilisation), insulin-dextrose + salbutamol (shift), and removal (diuretic, binder, or dialysis) — see [[Dyskalemia (Hypo- & Hyperkalemia)]].
- **Metabolic acidosis** — bicarbonate for severe acidaemia; treat the cause ([[Metabolic Acidosis]]).
- **Volume overload/pulmonary oedema** — loop diuretic if responsive; dialysis if refractory. (Diuretics do not prevent or treat AKI itself — use only for volume.)

**4. Renal replacement therapy — the "AEIOU" indications (refractory to medical therapy):**
- **A**cidosis — severe metabolic acidosis not medically correctable
- **E**lectrolytes — refractory **hyperkalaemia**
- **I**ntoxications — dialysable toxins (e.g. salicylate, lithium, toxic alcohols)
- **O**verload — refractory volume overload / pulmonary oedema
- **U**raemia — pericarditis, encephalopathy, or symptomatic uraemia

There is **no benefit to "early" prophylactic dialysis** in the absence of these indications (AKIKI/STARRT-AKI) — dialyse for a complication, not a number.

**5. Supportive:** nutrition, avoid hypotension, monitor daily weight and strict I/O, and arrange nephrology follow-up (AKI predicts future CKD → [[Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)]]).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

All supportive drugs (crystalloids, calcium gluconate, insulin, salbutamol, sodium bicarbonate, loop diuretics, potassium binders) and both intermittent haemodialysis and CRRT are available at Thai hospitals. Thai-relevant AKI causes to keep in mind: **leptospirosis** (fever + AKI + jaundice + myalgia), **snake envenomation** (haemotoxic → AKI, rhabdomyolysis), **severe dengue**, **malaria (blackwater fever)**, and **traditional/herbal nephrotoxins** — the febrile Thai patient with AKI needs an infection screen, not just a pre-renal label.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Hyperkalaemia with ECG changes** = immediate treatment (calcium → shift → remove); it kills before the creatinine does.
> - **Always exclude obstruction** with an ultrasound — post-renal AKI is fully reversible if relieved early.
> - **Stop nephrotoxins** (NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, aminoglycosides, contrast) and renally dose every drug.
> - **Don't dialyse by number** — dialyse for a refractory AEIOU indication; early prophylactic RRT gives no benefit.
> - Febrile Thai patient with AKI → screen for **leptospirosis, dengue, malaria, snakebite**.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **AKI = ↑Cr ≥ 0.3 in 48 h / ≥ 1.5× in 7 d / UOP < 0.5 mL/kg/h ≥ 6 h**; KDIGO stages 1–3.
> - Classify **pre-renal (FeNa < 1%) / intrinsic (ATN — muddy-brown casts) / post-renal (hydronephrosis)**.
> - Fix perfusion, **relieve obstruction**, **stop nephrotoxins**, treat **hyperkalaemia/acidosis/overload**.
> - **Dialyse for AEIOU** (Acidosis, Electrolytes, Intoxication, Overload, Uraemia); no benefit to early prophylactic RRT.
> - Urinalysis + FeNa + renal US are the high-yield first tests.

# Febrile Neutropenia

Fever in a patient with a low neutrophil count — an **oncologic emergency**, because a neutropenic patient cannot mount a normal inflammatory response and a trivial-looking infection can progress to overwhelming sepsis within hours. The rule is fixed: **draw cultures and give broad-spectrum antipseudomonal antibiotics within 1 hour — do not wait for the source, the imaging, or the full septic work-up.**

## Definition

- **Fever:** a single oral temperature **≥ 38.3 °C**, or **≥ 38.0 °C sustained over 1 hour.**
- **Neutropenia:** **absolute neutrophil count (ANC) < 500 cells/µL**, or **< 1000 with a predicted fall to < 500.** Risk and severity rise with **depth (ANC < 100) and duration (> 7 days)** of neutropenia.

## 🧬 Context & Pathophysiology

Usually follows **cytotoxic chemotherapy** (nadir ~7–14 days post-cycle) or marrow failure/infiltration. Loss of neutrophils removes the primary defence; the gut (mucositis), skin/lines (central venous catheters), and lungs are the common portals. **Gram-negative bacteria (incl. _Pseudomonas_) and Gram-positive organisms** predominate; with prolonged neutropenia, **invasive fungal infection (Candida, Aspergillus)** and resistant organisms emerge. **Signs of infection are blunted** — often only fever, so localizing signs (and pus) may be absent.

## 🩺 Assessment & risk stratification

Rapid focused history/exam for a source (mouth/mucositis, perianal — **no digital rectal exam/suppositories**, skin/catheter sites, chest, abdomen, CNS) and **haemodynamic assessment (sepsis/shock).** Stratify risk to decide site of care and route:
- **MASCC score:** **≥ 21 = low risk** (may be eligible for oral/outpatient therapy in selected, reliable patients); **≤ 20 = high risk** (admit, IV). High-risk features: profound (< 100)/prolonged (> 7 d) neutropenia, comorbidity, haemodynamic instability, mucositis, organ dysfunction, uncontrolled cancer.

## 🩻 Investigations (in parallel with, never before, antibiotics)

**Blood cultures (peripheral + each lumen of any central line) BEFORE antibiotics**, CBC + differential (ANC), U&E/LFT/CRP/lactate, urinalysis + culture, **CXR**, and cultures from any focal site; consider procalcitonin, viral swabs, and (if prolonged/persistent) fungal markers (galactomannan/β-D-glucan) and CT chest/sinuses. Do not delay the first antibiotic dose to complete these.

## 💊 Management

**1. Empiric antibiotics WITHIN 1 HOUR — after cultures.** **Monotherapy with an antipseudomonal β-lactam:** **cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, or a carbapenem (meropenem/imipenem)** for high-risk patients. Adjust to local antibiogram and prior colonisation (ESBL/resistant organisms → carbapenem).

**2. When to broaden:**
- **Add vancomycin (or linezolid) only for specific indications:** haemodynamic instability/severe sepsis, suspected catheter-related or skin/soft-tissue infection, pneumonia, known MRSA colonisation, or severe mucositis — **not routinely** (de-escalate at 48–72 h if no Gram-positive isolate).
- **Add antifungal cover** for **persistent/recurrent fever after ~4–7 days** of broad-spectrum antibiotics in prolonged neutropenia (empiric echinocandin or mould-active azole per protocol), guided by fungal markers/imaging.
- **Escalate/broaden Gram-negative cover** for clinical deterioration or resistant-organism risk.

**3. Low-risk pathway.** Selected **MASCC ≥ 21** patients with support and reliable follow-up may receive **oral fluoroquinolone + amoxicillin-clavulanate** (not if on fluoroquinolone prophylaxis) as outpatient/step-down therapy after initial assessment.

**4. Source control & support.** Remove/treat an infected catheter as indicated, treat mucositis, resuscitate sepsis ([[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)]]), and consider **G-CSF** in selected high-risk/deteriorating patients (not routine for uncomplicated FN). Continue antibiotics until afebrile and neutrophil recovery (or a defined course for an identified infection).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, carbapenems, vancomycin, antifungals, and G-CSF are available at Thai cancer/haematology centres; the **Thai Society of Hematology febrile-neutropenia guidance (2567/2024)** is in the reference library. The **"door-to-antibiotic within 1 hour"** target and local-antibiogram-guided empiric choice (with attention to high Thai ESBL/resistant-Gram-negative rates) are the key deliverables.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Antibiotics within 1 hour** after cultures — do NOT wait for a source or full work-up; neutropenic sepsis kills fast.
> - **Blunted signs** — fever may be the only clue; a benign-looking neutropenic patient can be septic.
> - **No digital rectal exam / rectal suppositories / rectal temps** (bacterial translocation risk).
> - **Vancomycin and antifungals are NOT routine** — add on specific indications; de-escalate.
> - **MASCC ≤ 20 / instability / profound-prolonged neutropenia = high risk** → admit, IV, broaden as needed.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **FN = temp ≥ 38.3 (or ≥ 38.0 × 1 h) + ANC < 500** (or < 1000 falling) → emergency.
> - **Cultures then empiric antipseudomonal β-lactam (cefepime / pip-tazo / carbapenem) WITHIN 1 h.**
> - **MASCC ≥ 21 = low risk** (possible oral FQ + amox-clav/outpatient); **≤ 20 = high risk** (IV, admit).
> - **Add vancomycin** only for instability/line/skin/pneumonia/MRSA; **antifungal** for persistent fever > 4–7 d.
> - No rectal exams/suppositories; treat mucositis/line source; G-CSF selectively.

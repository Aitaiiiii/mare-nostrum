# TTP & HUS (Thrombotic Microangiopathy)

The thrombotic microangiopathies (TMA) share **microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA) + thrombocytopenia + microvascular thrombosis causing organ injury.** The one you cannot miss is **TTP** — untreated mortality > 90%, but with prompt **plasma exchange** it falls dramatically — so a TMA picture demands you **think TTP and treat it emergently**, and **never transfuse platelets.**

## 🧬 Mechanism — the key distinction

| | **TTP** | **HUS** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **severe ADAMTS13 deficiency (< 10%)** — usually autoantibody (acquired), rarely congenital | **Shiga-toxin** (STEC O157, post-diarrhoeal — "typical") or **complement dysregulation** ("atypical" aHUS) |
| Pathology | uncleaved ultra-large vWF multimers → platelet-rich microthrombi | endothelial injury (toxin/complement) → microthrombi |
| Dominant organ | **neurological** (CNS) | **renal (AKI)** |
| Treatment | **plasma exchange + steroids (± caplacizumab)** | STEC-HUS supportive; aHUS → **eculizumab** |

ADAMTS13 normally cleaves von Willebrand factor multimers; its severe deficiency lets ultra-large multimers accumulate → spontaneous platelet aggregation in the microvasculature → consumption thrombocytopenia and shear-induced red-cell fragmentation.

## 🩺 Presentation

The classic **TTP pentad — MAHA, thrombocytopenia, fever, neurological signs, renal impairment — is present in < 10%**; suspect TTP with just **MAHA + thrombocytopenia and no other explanation.** Neurological features (headache, confusion, focal deficits, seizures, fluctuating) dominate TTP; **AKI + bloody diarrhoea** points to STEC-HUS (children especially). Bleeding is usually mild despite the low platelets.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **CBC + blood film: schistocytes (fragmented RBCs), thrombocytopenia**; reticulocytosis.
- **Haemolysis markers: ↑LDH, ↑indirect bilirubin, ↓haptoglobin, DAT (Coombs) negative.**
- **Renal function, urinalysis; coagulation (PT/aPTT/fibrinogen NORMAL — distinguishes from DIC).**
- **ADAMTS13 activity + inhibitor/antibody** (send before plasma exchange; **< 10% confirms TTP**) — but **do not wait for the result to start treatment.**
- **PLASMIC score** to estimate pre-test probability of severe ADAMTS13 deficiency and guide urgent plasma exchange. Stool for STEC/Shiga toxin if diarrhoeal; complement studies for suspected aHUS; pregnancy test; exclude DIC, malignant hypertension, HELLP, sepsis.

## 💊 Management

**1. TTP — plasma exchange is the emergency treatment.** **Start therapeutic plasma exchange (PEX) urgently** (daily) on clinical suspicion / high PLASMIC score — it removes antibody + ultra-large vWF and replaces ADAMTS13. If PEX is not immediately available, give **plasma infusion** as a bridge. Add **corticosteroids** (immune suppression) and, where available, **caplacizumab** (anti-vWF nanobody — faster platelet recovery, fewer exacerbations). **Rituximab** for refractory/relapsing acquired TTP. Continue PEX until platelets normalise (> 150) and haemolysis resolves.

**2. NEVER give platelet transfusions in TTP** (except catastrophic/life-threatening bleeding or before a critical procedure) — added platelets fuel microvascular thrombosis and can worsen organ injury. This is a defining rule.

**3. HUS.** **STEC-HUS ("typical", post-diarrhoeal): supportive** — fluids/renal support/dialysis; **avoid antibiotics and antimotility agents in STEC** (increase toxin release/HUS risk). **Atypical HUS (complement): eculizumab/ravulizumab** (complement C5 inhibition) — and vaccinate/prophylax against meningococcus before eculizumab. Plasma exchange is less effective in HUS than TTP.

**4. Supportive.** Folate, transfuse **red cells** as needed (RBC transfusion is fine; platelets are not), manage AKI, and treat the underlying trigger; involve haematology early. Relapse surveillance for acquired TTP.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Therapeutic plasma exchange, corticosteroids, and rituximab are available at Thai tertiary/haematology centres; **caplacizumab and eculizumab access/cost may be limited** — plasma exchange + steroids (± rituximab) remain the backbone, with plasma infusion as a bridge where PEX is being arranged. Recognising TTP early and getting the patient to a centre that can perform PEX is the key deliverable.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **MAHA + thrombocytopenia unexplained = TTP until excluded** → urgent plasma exchange, don't wait for ADAMTS13.
> - **NEVER transfuse platelets in TTP** (fuels thrombosis) — except life-threatening bleeding.
> - **Normal PT/aPTT/fibrinogen** distinguishes TMA from DIC; DAT-negative haemolysis + schistocytes.
> - **STEC-HUS: avoid antibiotics/antimotility agents** (↑HUS risk); aHUS → eculizumab (meningococcal cover first).
> - Full pentad is rare — treat on MAHA + thrombocytopenia; involve haematology immediately.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - TMA = **MAHA (schistocytes) + thrombocytopenia + organ injury**; normal coagulation (not DIC), Coombs-negative.
> - **TTP = ADAMTS13 < 10%** (neuro-dominant) → **urgent plasma exchange + steroids ± caplacizumab**; **PLASMIC score**; send ADAMTS13 but treat first.

> - **HUS**: STEC (renal, post-diarrhoeal) = supportive, **avoid antibiotics**; aHUS = **eculizumab**.

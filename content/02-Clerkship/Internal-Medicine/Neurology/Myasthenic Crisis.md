# Myasthenic Crisis

Life-threatening **respiratory failure** in myasthenia gravis (MG), from weakness of the diaphragm/bulbar muscles requiring ventilatory support. As in [[Guillain-Barre Syndrome|GBS]], the disease is treatable and reversible — the danger is under-recognising impending respiratory failure, so the management is **anticipating and securing the airway early** plus rapid immunotherapy and removing the precipitant.

## 🧬 Pathophysiology & precipitants

MG = autoantibodies against the **postsynaptic acetylcholine receptor** (or MuSK/LRP4) at the neuromuscular junction → fatigable weakness. A **crisis** is precipitated in ~40% by:
- **Infection** (commonest), aspiration, surgery/anaesthesia, pregnancy/childbirth, tapering immunotherapy.
- **Drugs that worsen MG:** **aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides**, **β-blockers**, **magnesium (IV)**, neuromuscular blockers, procainamide, quinine, telavancin, and **high-dose corticosteroids can transiently worsen weakness** in the first ~1–2 weeks.
- Distinguish from **cholinergic crisis** (excess anticholinesterase → muscarinic overactivity: sweating, salivation, miosis, fasciculations) — rare now, but hold pyridostigmine if unclear.

## 🩺 Presentation

Worsening **bulbar (dysphagia, dysarthria, weak cough, jaw/neck weakness) and respiratory (dyspnoea, orthopnoea, tachypnoea, paradoxical breathing, weak cough)** weakness, often with generalised fatigable weakness and ptosis/diplopia. **Hypoxaemia and hypercapnia are LATE** — do not wait for them.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Serial forced vital capacity (FVC) and negative inspiratory force (NIF/MIP) — the key monitoring** (bedside single-breath count as a surrogate). **Trends matter more than an absolute number**; a falling FVC or FVC < ~15–20 mL/kg, NIF weaker than about −20 to −30 cmH₂O, or bulbar failure/secretions → intubate.
- Assess the precipitant: **septic screen, CXR, cultures**; ABG (late), electrolytes (**avoid IV magnesium**), medication review. Anti-AChR/MuSK antibodies and diagnostics if new MG.

## 💊 Management

**1. Airway/respiratory — intubate EARLY on trends, not on gases.** Monitor FVC/NIF closely; **elective intubation** for declining respiratory mechanics, bulbar failure, or secretion-clearance failure — a crash airway is dangerous. Avoid depolarising/prolonged neuromuscular blockers where possible; support ventilation.

**2. Immunotherapy — IVIG or plasma exchange (fast-acting, equally effective; PLEX may act slightly faster).**
- **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days** (total 2 g/kg), OR **plasma exchange ~5 sessions (1–1.5 plasma volumes) every other day.**
- **Corticosteroids** are usually started (for longer-term control) but **can transiently worsen weakness in the first ~1–2 weeks** — start under cover of IVIG/PLEX and ventilatory support in a crisis.

**3. Stop the precipitant + avoid aggravating drugs.** Treat infection; **avoid aminoglycosides/fluoroquinolones/macrolides, β-blockers, IV magnesium, and other MG-worsening drugs.** Manage pyridostigmine carefully (secretions can worsen ventilation; often held while intubated, restarted on recovery). Distinguish/treat cholinergic crisis if relevant.

**4. Supportive.** VTE prophylaxis, nutrition (aspiration precautions/NG), physiotherapy, and thymoma assessment/thymectomy planning after recovery. Neurology + ICU throughout.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

IVIG, plasma exchange, pyridostigmine, corticosteroids, and ICU/ventilatory support are available at Thai tertiary centres; the **Thai neuromuscular immunotherapy guidance** is in the reference library. The key deliverables are early airway control (FVC/NIF monitoring) and avoiding the common MG-worsening drugs (aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, magnesium) that are otherwise freely used.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Intubate EARLY on FVC/NIF trends + bulbar failure — hypoxaemia/hypercapnia are LATE.**
> - **Avoid MG-worsening drugs**: aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, β-blockers, **IV magnesium**, neuromuscular blockers.
> - **Corticosteroids can transiently worsen weakness** for 1–2 weeks — start under IVIG/PLEX + ventilatory cover.
> - **Find/treat the precipitant** (infection commonest).
> - Distinguish **cholinergic crisis** (muscarinic excess) — hold pyridostigmine if unclear.

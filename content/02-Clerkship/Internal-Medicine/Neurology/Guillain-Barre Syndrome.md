# Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)

An acute immune-mediated polyradiculoneuropathy causing **rapidly progressive, ascending, symmetric flaccid paralysis with areflexia.** The disease itself is treatable and often reversible, but it kills through **respiratory failure and autonomic instability** — so the emergency management is **anticipating ventilatory failure (serial FVC) and monitoring autonomic dysfunction**, alongside immunotherapy.

## 🧬 Pathophysiology & variants

A post-infectious autoimmune attack (molecular mimicry) on peripheral nerves, typically **1–3 weeks after** a respiratory or GI infection — classically ***Campylobacter jejuni*** (associated with the axonal AMAN variant), also CMV, EBV, *Mycoplasma*, influenza, Zika, and (historically) some vaccines. Subtypes: **AIDP** (demyelinating, commonest in the West), **AMAN/AMSAN** (axonal), and **Miller Fisher syndrome** (ophthalmoplegia + ataxia + areflexia, anti-GQ1b antibody).

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Progressive, symmetric, ascending weakness starting in the legs** with **early loss of reflexes (areflexia)**, often with distal paraesthesiae/neuropathic pain; progression over **hours to ~2 weeks (nadir by 4 weeks)**. Danger features:
- **Respiratory failure** — bulbar weakness, weak cough, breathlessness, rising RR, paradoxical breathing.
- **Autonomic instability** — labile BP, arrhythmias (tachy/brady, asystole), ileus, urinary retention — a major cause of death.
- Bulbar/facial weakness (aspiration risk); Miller Fisher's ophthalmoplegia/ataxia.

> [!note] 💎 Pearls
> - **Areflexia early in the course** is a key discriminator; sensory symptoms may exceed sensory signs.
> - **Autonomic swings can cause sudden fatal arrhythmia** — continuous cardiac monitoring in progressive/severe disease.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Serial forced vital capacity (FVC)** — the single most important monitoring test (see the 20/30/40 rule below); bedside single-breath count as a surrogate.
- **Lumbar puncture:** **albuminocytological dissociation** (raised CSF protein with normal cell count) — often absent in the first week, so a normal early LP does not exclude GBS.
- **Nerve conduction studies** (demyelinating vs axonal) support the diagnosis. Antiganglioside antibodies (anti-GQ1b for Miller Fisher) in selected cases.
- ECG/telemetry (autonomic), electrolytes, and exclude mimics (hypokalaemia, tick paralysis, [[Neurotoxic Snakebite|neurotoxic envenoming]], botulism, [[Myasthenic Crisis|myasthenia]], transverse myelitis, hypokalaemic periodic paralysis).

## 💊 Management

**1. Airway/respiratory surveillance — the priority.** **Monitor FVC serially.** The **"20/30/40 rule"** flags likely need for intubation: **FVC < 20 mL/kg, maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP) < 30 cmH₂O, or maximal expiratory pressure (MEP) < 40 cmH₂O** — but this is a guide, not an absolute trigger; **intubate on the clinical trajectory (rapid progression, bulbar weakness, fatigue, aspiration)**, electively rather than as a crash. Avoid suxamethonium (hyperkalaemia risk).

**2. Immunotherapy — IVIG or plasma exchange (equally effective; do NOT combine).**
- **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days** (total 2 g/kg) — usually preferred (easier, better tolerated), best started within 2 weeks of onset.
- **Plasma exchange (PLEX): ~200–250 mL/kg over 5 sessions**, effective within 4 weeks of onset.
- **Corticosteroids are NOT effective in GBS and should not be used.**
- Treat patients who cannot walk unaided or who are progressing; Miller Fisher is often self-limiting but treated if deteriorating.

**3. Autonomic & supportive care.** Continuous cardiac monitoring (treat arrhythmias; **be cautious with drugs for labile BP** — exaggerated responses), **VTE prophylaxis**, neuropathic pain control, bowel/bladder care, physiotherapy, nutrition, and psychological support. ICU for respiratory/autonomic compromise.

**4. Prognosis.** Most recover substantially over weeks–months; a minority have residual deficits, and mortality (~3–7%) is mainly from respiratory/autonomic complications — hence the emphasis on monitoring.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

**IVIG and plasma exchange are both available** at Thai tertiary centres and neuro-ICUs; ventilatory and autonomic monitoring are the core deliverables. The **Thai neuromuscular-immunotherapy guidance** is in the reference library. In the Thai setting, actively exclude **hypokalaemic periodic paralysis (common), neurotoxic snakebite, and organophosphate/botulism** as acute-weakness mimics before anchoring on GBS.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Monitor FVC serially and intubate early on trajectory** (20/30/40 rule as a guide) — do not wait for arrest; a "comfortable" patient can crash.
> - **Autonomic instability can cause sudden fatal arrhythmia** — continuous cardiac monitoring; use vasoactive drugs cautiously.
> - **Steroids do NOT work in GBS** — use IVIG or PLEX (not both).
> - A **normal early LP/NCS does not exclude GBS** — treat on the clinical picture.
> - Exclude mimics: hypokalaemia/periodic paralysis, snakebite, botulism, myasthenia, myelitis.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Ascending symmetric flaccid weakness + early areflexia**, ~1–3 wk post-infection (*Campylobacter*); **albuminocytological dissociation**.
> - **Serial FVC**; **20/30/40 rule** (FVC < 20 mL/kg, MIP < 30, MEP < 40) → intubate (guide, not absolute); intubate on trajectory.
> - **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 d OR PLEX ~200–250 mL/kg × 5** — equal, not combined; **NO steroids**.
> - **Autonomic instability** = major killer → cardiac monitoring, cautious vasoactive use; VTE prophylaxis.
> - Miller Fisher = ophthalmoplegia + ataxia + areflexia (anti-GQ1b).

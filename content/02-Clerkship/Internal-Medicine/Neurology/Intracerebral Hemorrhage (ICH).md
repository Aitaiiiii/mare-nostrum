# Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH)

Non-traumatic bleeding into the brain parenchyma — the deadliest stroke subtype, where early deterioration is driven by **haematoma expansion** in the first hours. The ER bundle (2022 AHA/ASA) is a small number of things done fast: **smooth blood-pressure control, immediate reversal of any anticoagulant, ICP management, and timely neurosurgical assessment.**

## 🧬 Aetiology & Pathophysiology

Primary injury is mechanical disruption by the expanding clot; secondary injury follows from **haematoma expansion** (first 3–6 h, worse with anticoagulation/high BP), **perihaematomal oedema**, raised ICP, and intraventricular extension with hydrocephalus.

| Cause | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Hypertensive (chronic small-vessel)** | **deep** — basal ganglia (putamen), thalamus, pons, cerebellum (Charcot–Bouchard microaneurysms) |
| **Cerebral amyloid angiopathy** | **lobar**, elderly, recurrent |
| **Anticoagulant / coagulopathy** | any location, larger, expands |
| Vascular malformation / aneurysm | younger, lobar/atypical |
| Tumour, haemorrhagic transformation of infarct, venous thrombosis, drugs (sympathomimetics) | context-specific |

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Sudden focal deficit with **headache, vomiting, and depressed consciousness more prominent than in ischaemic stroke**, often with markedly raised BP. Deficit maps to location (putaminal — hemiparesis/gaze deviation; thalamic — sensory + eye signs; cerebellar — ataxia, vomiting, ↓GCS from brainstem compression; pontine — coma, pinpoint pupils). **Clinically indistinguishable from ischaemic stroke — urgent CT decides.**

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Non-contrast CT head immediately** — confirms and localises the bleed, measures volume (ABC/2), shows IVH/hydrocephalus/midline shift. A **"spot sign" on CT angiography** predicts expansion; CTA/MRI to find an underlying lesion (malformation, aneurysm, tumour) in atypical/lobar/young patients.
- **Coagulation screen, INR, platelets, CBC, anti-Xa if on a DOAC**, glucose, renal/liver function, ECG/troponin.
- **ICH Score** (GCS, age ≥ 80, volume ≥ 30 mL, IVH, infratentorial origin) for prognosis — a communication tool, **not** a reason to withdraw care early.

## 💊 Management

**1. Blood pressure — early, smooth, sustained lowering.** For mild-to-moderate ICH presenting with **SBP 150–220**, acute lowering to a **target around 140 mmHg (keep it in ~130–150) is reasonable**; **avoid dropping SBP < 130** (associated with harm) and **avoid large fluctuations** — smooth, sustained control (IV titratable agent: nicardipine/labetalol) limits haematoma expansion. Treat as part of a bundle, not in isolation.

**2. Reverse anticoagulation IMMEDIATELY — the single most time-critical action.** Stop the anticoagulant and reverse as fast as the diagnosis allows:
- **Warfarin/VKA (INR ≥ 2):** **4-factor prothrombin complex concentrate (4F-PCC)** in preference to FFP + **IV vitamin K** — PCC corrects the INR faster and limits expansion.
- **Dabigatran:** **idarucizumab**.
- **Factor Xa inhibitors (rivaroxaban/apixaban/edoxaban):** **andexanet alfa** where available, otherwise **4F-PCC**.
- **Heparin:** protamine. **Antiplatelet-associated ICH: routine platelet transfusion is NOT recommended** (harm signal), except before neurosurgery.

**3. Neurosurgical management.** Emergency referral for: **cerebellar haemorrhage > ~3 cm (or with brainstem compression/hydrocephalus) → evacuate** (life-saving); **hydrocephalus → external ventricular drain**; selected supratentorial clots (minimally-invasive evacuation is an evolving option). Decompression for herniation.

**4. ICP & neuroprotective care.** Head up 30°, normoxia/normocapnia, treat fever and hyperglycaemia, seizure treatment (do not give prophylactic anticonvulsants routinely), reverse coagulopathy, and manage in a **stroke unit/neuro-ICU**. **VTE prophylaxis with intermittent pneumatic compression from admission**; pharmacological prophylaxis only after bleeding has stopped (usually 24–48 h).

**5. Reversal-then-resumption:** decide if/when to restart anticoagulation later (weigh the original indication vs re-bleed risk, especially in lobar/amyloid ICH where resumption is riskier).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

IV antihypertensives (nicardipine, labetalol, nitroprusside), vitamin K, and **4F-PCC** are available at Thai hospitals; **idarucizumab is available**, while **andexanet alfa access is limited** — so **4F-PCC is the practical reversal for Xa-inhibitor ICH in most Thai settings**. Neurosurgery and neuro-ICU care are at regional/tertiary centres. Hypertensive deep ICH is the dominant Thai pattern given the community hypertension burden.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Reverse anticoagulation immediately** — 4F-PCC (VKA/Xa) / idarucizumab (dabigatran); every minute of delay expands the clot.
> - **Lower BP smoothly to ~140; do NOT drop SBP < 130** and avoid wide swings.
> - **Cerebellar haemorrhage > 3 cm or with brainstem compression/hydrocephalus → emergency evacuation** — do not "wait and watch".
> - **Do NOT routinely transfuse platelets** for antiplatelet-associated ICH (PATCH trial harm) except pre-surgery.
> - Deteriorating GCS → urgent repeat CT (expansion/hydrocephalus/herniation).

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **CT first** — ICH is clinically indistinguishable from ischaemic stroke.
> - **BP: aim SBP ~140 (130–150), avoid < 130, smooth control** (nicardipine/labetalol).
> - **Reverse anticoagulant now:** VKA → **4F-PCC + vit K**; dabigatran → **idarucizumab**; Xa → **andexanet/4F-PCC**.
> - **Cerebellar bleed > 3 cm / brainstem compression / hydrocephalus → neurosurgery**; EVD for hydrocephalus.
> - **No routine platelet transfusion** for antiplatelet ICH; IPC for VTE prophylaxis first.

# Acute Liver Failure (ALF)

Rapid loss of hepatic function — **coagulopathy (INR ≥ 1.5) and hepatic encephalopathy developing within ~26 weeks in a patient WITHOUT pre-existing chronic liver disease.** It is distinct from decompensated cirrhosis, and it is a transplant emergency: the two things that determine survival are **cerebral oedema** (the leading cause of death) and **timely transfer for transplantation.**

## 🧬 Causes & Pathophysiology

Massive hepatocyte necrosis/apoptosis → loss of synthetic (coagulation, albumin, glucose), metabolic (ammonia clearance), and immune functions → **encephalopathy + cerebral oedema (ammonia/glutamine-driven astrocyte swelling), coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia, lactic acidosis, AKI (hepatorenal/ATN), and a SIRS/immunoparetic state prone to sepsis.** Causes:

- **Drug-induced — paracetamol** (commonest in many countries; a treatable cause → [[Paracetamol Overdose]]), idiosyncratic drug reactions (anti-TB drugs, antiepileptics, herbal/traditional remedies).
- **Viral hepatitis** (**hepatitis A, B, E** — important in Thailand; E especially dangerous in pregnancy), other viruses.
- **Others:** autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson's disease (young, haemolysis, low ALP), Budd-Chiari, ischaemic ("shock liver"), pregnancy-related (AFLP, HELLP), amanita mushroom, heat stroke.

## 🩺 Presentation

Prodrome of malaise/nausea/jaundice, then **encephalopathy (grade I–IV)**, coagulopathy/bruising, RUQ pain, and features of the complications: hypoglycaemia, **cerebral oedema (worsening encephalopathy, raised ICP → hypertension/bradycardia, abnormal pupils, decerebrate posturing)**, AKI, infection/sepsis, and cardiovascular instability. Grade of encephalopathy tracks cerebral-oedema risk (high in grade III–IV).

## 🩻 Investigations

- **INR/PT (the key marker of severity — do NOT reflexively "correct" it), LFTs, bilirubin, ammonia, glucose (frequent — hypoglycaemia), lactate, U&E/creatinine, ABG, CBC, phosphate.**
- **Aetiology work-up:** **paracetamol level**, viral serology (**anti-HAV IgM, HBsAg/anti-HBc IgM, anti-HEV, HSV**), autoimmune markers, **caeruloplasmin/copper (Wilson's)**, pregnancy test, toxicology, and imaging (US/Doppler — Budd-Chiari).
- Cultures (low threshold — sepsis common); CT head if focal signs/ICP concern.

## 💊 Management (ICU + early transplant-centre contact)

**1. N-acetylcysteine (NAC) — for all-cause ALF.** Give NAC not only for paracetamol but for **non-paracetamol ALF** too (improves transplant-free survival in early-grade encephalopathy). Start early.

**2. Cerebral oedema — the leading killer.** Nurse **head-up 30°, avoid stimulation, treat fever and hyponatraemia**; **grade III–IV encephalopathy → intubate + ICU.** For raised ICP: **hypertonic saline** (target Na ~145–155, keep < 160) or **mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg bolus**; **hyperventilation to PaCO₂ 25–30 mmHg** for acute surges; consider ICP monitoring in selected transplant candidates. Aim to prevent herniation while bridging to transplant/recovery.

**3. Do NOT routinely correct the coagulopathy.** **Only give FFP/factors for active bleeding or before an invasive procedure** — the INR is the key prognostic marker and blindly correcting it removes that signal and adds volume/TRALI risk. Give **vitamin K**; use a PPI for stress-ulcer prophylaxis.

**4. Supportive care + treat the cause.** **Frequent glucose (dextrose infusion for hypoglycaemia)**, correct electrolytes/phosphate, haemodynamic support (vasopressors), **renal support (CRRT — also helps ammonia/volume)**, low threshold for **empiric antibiotics/antifungals** (sepsis surveillance), and **lactulose is of limited/uncertain benefit in ALF** (unlike cirrhotic HE) — do not over-rely on it. **Aciclovir** for HSV; specific therapy (antivirals, chelation, steroids for autoimmune, delivery for pregnancy-related).

**5. Transplant referral — early, by prognosis.** **Contact a transplant centre early** and apply the **King's College Criteria** for poor prognosis / transplant listing:
- **Paracetamol ALF:** arterial **pH < 7.30** (after resuscitation), **OR** all three of **INR > 6.5 (PT > 100 s) + creatinine > 300 µmol/L (3.4 mg/dL) + grade III–IV encephalopathy**; (lactate and phosphate are adjuncts).
- **Non-paracetamol ALF:** **INR > 6.5 alone**, OR any three of: age < 10 or > 40, unfavourable aetiology (non-A non-B hepatitis, drug/idiosyncratic), jaundice-to-encephalopathy > 7 days, INR > 3.5, bilirubin > 300 µmol/L.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

NAC, dextrose, hypertonic saline/mannitol, CRRT, and ICU care are available; **liver transplantation is at major Thai transplant centres**, so early transfer is essential. Thai-relevant causes: **paracetamol overdose, viral hepatitis (A/B/E), anti-TB drug hepatotoxicity, and herbal/traditional-remedy DILI** — a careful drug/herbal history and viral serology are high-yield.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Cerebral oedema is the leading killer** — grade III–IV encephalopathy → intubate/ICU, treat raised ICP (hypertonic saline/mannitol/hyperventilation).
> - **Do NOT correct the coagulopathy** unless bleeding/pre-procedure — INR is the prognostic marker.
> - **Give NAC for ALL-cause ALF**, not just paracetamol.
> - **Refer to a transplant centre EARLY** (King's College criteria) — don't wait for the full criteria to be met.
> - **Frequent glucose** (hypoglycaemia) and **low threshold for sepsis** work-up/empiric antimicrobials.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **ALF = coagulopathy (INR ≥ 1.5) + encephalopathy, NO pre-existing cirrhosis.**
> - **NAC for all causes**; **cerebral oedema** management (hypertonic saline/mannitol, hyperventilation) — the main killer.

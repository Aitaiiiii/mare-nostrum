# Acute Mesenteric Ischemia (AMI)

Sudden interruption of intestinal blood flow causing bowel ischaemia and, if untreated, transmural infarction, perforation, and death. It is a **diagnosis of suspicion** — the classic teaching, **"pain out of proportion to physical findings,"** must trigger CT angiography and vascular referral before peritonitis develops, because once the bowel is dead, mortality is very high.

## 🧬 The four types (each changes treatment)

| Type | Mechanism | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| **SMA embolism** (~50%) | embolus lodges in SMA (spares the first jejunal branches) | **AF**, recent MI, valve disease — sudden severe pain |
| **SMA thrombosis** (~15–25%) | acute-on-chronic atherosclerotic occlusion at the origin | prior post-prandial pain/"intestinal angina", weight loss, diffuse atherosclerosis |
| **Non-occlusive mesenteric ischaemia (NOMI)** | splanchnic vasoconstriction, low flow | critically ill, shock, vasopressors, dialysis, heart failure — insidious |
| **Mesenteric venous thrombosis** | SMV thrombosis → congestion | hypercoagulable states, portal hypertension, younger patients — subacute |

Ischaemia → mucosal barrier failure → bacterial translocation, lactic acidosis, third-spacing, and infarction; the venous type causes bowel oedema/congestion rather than pale infarct.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Severe, poorly-localised abdominal pain that is dramatically out of proportion to a relatively benign early abdominal exam** — the hallmark. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and later **GI bleeding**. As transmural infarction develops, the abdomen becomes distended, tender, with **peritonism, guarding, and shock — a late, ominous stage.** NOMI in an ICU patient may present only as unexplained acidosis or sepsis.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Multiphasic (arterial + portal-venous) CT angiography is the diagnostic gold standard** (sensitivity ~96–100%) — arterial cut-off/filling defect, bowel-wall changes, **pneumatosis intestinalis or portal-venous gas** (late/necrosis), mesenteric oedema (venous). Order it early; do not wait.
- **Lactate** rises (a late marker of established necrosis — a normal lactate does not exclude early AMI); metabolic acidosis; leukocytosis, haemoconcentration. D-dimer is sensitive but non-specific.
- Plain films are insensitive (used to exclude free air). ECG/echo for embolic source; thrombophilia work-up for venous AMI.

## 💊 Management

**1. Resuscitate + heparinise + cover + call surgery — immediately.** Aggressive **IV fluid** resuscitation (large third-space losses), **IV heparin** anticoagulation, **broad-spectrum antibiotics** (Gram-negative + anaerobic cover for translocation), NG decompression, analgesia, correct acidosis, and **urgent surgical + vascular/IR referral.**

**2. Revascularise and assess bowel viability by type.**
- **SMA embolism → surgical embolectomy** (or endovascular aspiration/thrombolysis) + laparotomy to assess/resect non-viable bowel.
- **SMA thrombosis → revascularisation** (endovascular stenting or surgical bypass) + resection of infarcted bowel.
- **NOMI → treat the cause of low flow** (optimise cardiac output, stop/limit vasopressors), **intra-arterial vasodilator (papaverine)**, and resect necrotic bowel; revascularisation is not the primary fix.
- **Venous (MVT) → anticoagulation is the mainstay** (heparin then long-term), bowel resection only for infarction; thrombolysis/thrombectomy in selected cases.

**3. Damage-control & second look.** Resect frankly necrotic bowel; a **planned "second-look" laparotomy** at 24–48 h reassesses marginal bowel to preserve length. Support the septic/reperfusion physiology in ICU (see [[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)]]).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Heparin, broad-spectrum antibiotics, CT angiography, and both open and endovascular revascularisation are available at Thai tertiary centres; peripheral hospitals resuscitate, heparinise, cover, and transfer urgently. AF and atherosclerosis (embolic/thrombotic) dominate; consider hypercoagulable causes (including malignancy) for venous AMI.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Pain out of proportion to exam = AMI until proven otherwise** → CT angiography now, don't observe.
> - **A normal lactate does NOT exclude early AMI** — it rises only with established necrosis.
> - **Peritonism/shock = transmural infarction (late)** → emergency laparotomy.
> - **NOMI** in a shocked/ICU patient → suspect on unexplained acidosis; treat low-flow state, don't just image once.
> - Heparinise + broad antibiotics + surgery/vascular referral early — every hour of delay costs bowel and life.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Pain out of proportion + a risk factor (AF, atherosclerosis, low-flow, hypercoagulable)** → **CTA gold standard**.
> - Four types: **SMA embolism (AF) · SMA thrombosis (chronic) · NOMI (low flow) · venous (hypercoagulable)**.
> - Immediate: **fluids + heparin + broad antibiotics + urgent surgery/vascular**; embolectomy/revascularisation + resect dead bowel; **second-look laparotomy**.
> - **Venous AMI → anticoagulation is primary**; **NOMI → fix perfusion + papaverine**.
> - **Lactate/peritonism are late** — act on suspicion, not confirmation.

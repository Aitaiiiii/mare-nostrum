# Approach — Acute Abdomen

"Acute abdomen" = the acute onset of abdominal pain that may need urgent surgery. The task is not an instant diagnosis but a **safe, structured triage: resuscitate, decide sick-vs-not-sick, localise the likely organ, and — above all — exclude the time-critical catastrophes** that kill within hours, routing to the specific disease pages.

## 🩺 Resuscitate + assess severity first

ABCs; IV access, fluids for hypovolaemia/sepsis; analgesia (**giving opioid analgesia does NOT mask the diagnosis or delay surgery**). Flag the **unstable / peritonitic patient** (rigid abdomen, guarding, rebound, absent bowel sounds, shock) → they need **urgent surgical referral and resuscitation in parallel with work-up**, not a leisurely investigation.

| Region | Common | Must-not-miss |
|---|---|---|
| **RUQ** | biliary colic, [[Acute Cholecystitis\|cholecystitis]], [[Acute Cholangitis\|cholangitis]], hepatitis | see [[Approach - RUQ Pain in Adults]]; ascending cholangitis (sepsis) |
| **Epigastric** | [[Peptic Ulcer Disease & Perforation\|PUD]], [[Acute Pancreatitis\|pancreatitis]], gastritis | **perforated ulcer, inferior MI, ruptured AAA** |
| **RIF / LIF** | [[Acute Appendicitis\|appendicitis]], [[Acute Diverticulitis\|diverticulitis]] | appendicitis, [[Ectopic Pregnancy\|ectopic]], [[Ovarian Torsion\|torsion]] |
| **Central/diffuse** | gastroenteritis, early appendicitis, [[Bowel Obstruction (SBO & LBO)\|obstruction]] | **[[Acute Mesenteric Ischemia\|mesenteric ischaemia]], ruptured AAA, perforation** |
| **Loin/flank** | [[Urolithiasis (Renal Colic)\|renal colic]], [[Urinary Tract Infection & Pyelonephritis\|pyelonephritis]] | **ruptured AAA** (mimics colic in the elderly) |
| **Suprapubic** | [[Urinary Tract Infection & Pyelonephritis\|UTI]], [[Acute Urinary Retention\|retention]], gynae | ectopic, PID |

## 🚨 The catastrophes to exclude in every acute abdomen

- **Perforated viscus** ([[Peptic Ulcer Disease & Perforation]]) — sudden severe pain, peritonism, **free air**.
- **Acute mesenteric ischaemia** ([[Acute Mesenteric Ischemia]]) — **pain out of proportion**, AF/atherosclerosis, lactate.
- **Ruptured/leaking abdominal aortic aneurysm** — older patient, pain + hypotension ± pulsatile mass; can mimic renal colic — **never anchor on "colic" in an older first presentation.**
- **Ectopic pregnancy** ([[Ectopic Pregnancy]]) — **β-hCG in EVERY woman of reproductive age.**
- **Bowel obstruction with strangulation** ([[Bowel Obstruction (SBO & LBO)]]), **acute pancreatitis** (severe), **ascending cholangitis** (sepsis), and **testicular/ovarian torsion**.
- Extra-abdominal mimics: **inferior MI, DKA, lower-lobe pneumonia, adrenal crisis.**

## 🩻 Investigations

**β-hCG (all women), lactate, CBC, U&E, LFTs, amylase/lipase, glucose, venous gas, group & save, urinalysis**; **ECG + troponin** (epigastric pain — exclude MI). Imaging: **erect CXR** (free air), **ultrasound** (biliary, gynae, aorta, free fluid, first-line in pregnancy/young), and **CT abdomen/pelvis** — the workhorse for undifferentiated or serious acute abdomen (obstruction, ischaemia, perforation, appendicitis, AAA). Don't let imaging delay theatre in the unstable peritonitic patient.

## 💊 Disposition

- **Peritonitis / haemodynamic instability / suspected catastrophe → resuscitate + urgent surgical referral (± straight to theatre)**; broad-spectrum antibiotics for suspected perforation/sepsis; NG + catheter as needed.
- **Stable, localised** → targeted work-up and the disease-specific pathway (many are non-operative — e.g. uncomplicated diverticulitis, renal colic).
- **Undifferentiated but stable** → observe with serial examination + repeat bloods/imaging; a **normal early assessment does not exclude an evolving surgical abdomen** — re-examine.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Ultrasound, CT, laboratory, and surgical services are available; the deliverables anywhere are resuscitation, β-hCG, lactate, an erect CXR/US, and early recognition/transfer of the catastrophes. Consider Thai-relevant causes (typhoid perforation, amoebic liver abscess, right-sided diverticulitis, melioidosis, dengue with abdominal pain).

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **β-hCG in EVERY reproductive-age woman** (ectopic).
> - **Peritonitis/instability → resuscitate + urgent surgery** — don't delay for imaging.
> - **Pain out of proportion = mesenteric ischaemia**; **older "renal colic" first presentation = exclude ruptured AAA.**
> - **Analgesia does NOT mask the diagnosis** — give it.
> - **Exclude extra-abdominal mimics** (inferior MI, DKA, lower-lobe pneumonia); a normal early exam can evolve — re-examine.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Resuscitate + **sick-vs-not-sick**; peritonitis/instability → urgent surgery (imaging must not delay theatre).
> - **Localise by region**, but always exclude the catastrophes: **perforation, mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured AAA, ectopic, strangulated obstruction, cholangitis**.
> - **β-hCG (all women), lactate, ECG/troponin (epigastric), erect CXR (free air), US, CT** (workhorse).
> - Give analgesia; consider **inferior MI / DKA / pneumonia** mimics.
> - Undifferentiated + stable → **serial exam** (evolving abdomen).

# Acute Pancreatitis

Acute inflammation of the pancreas from premature intra-acinar trypsinogen activation → autodigestion, a local and systemic inflammatory response, and (in severe disease) organ failure. Most cases are mild and self-limited; the clinical work is to **identify the aetiology (treatable — gallstones), resuscitate correctly, feed early, and predict/monitor for the minority who become severe.** The 2024 ACG guideline reversed the old dogma of "early aggressive" fluids toward a **moderately aggressive** strategy.

## 🧬 Aetiology & Pathophysiology

Trypsin activation overwhelms protective mechanisms → acinar necrosis, cytokine release (SIRS), microcirculatory failure, and a capillary-leak state that drives hypovolaemia and, when severe, ARDS, AKI, and shock. Causes (mnemonic **I GET SMASHED**), but two dominate:

| Cause | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Gallstones** (~40–70%) | commonest; obstruction at the ampulla; ↑ALT (> 3× ULN) points here; needs cholecystectomy |
| **Alcohol** (~30%) | binge or chronic; second commonest |
| **Hypertriglyceridaemia** | **TG > 1000 mg/dL**; can cause spuriously normal amylase; treat with insulin ± apheresis |
| **Hypercalcaemia** | check calcium |
| **ERCP** (post-procedure) | rectal indometacin prophylaxis reduces it |
| **Drugs** | azathioprine, valproate, GLP-1 agonists, thiazides, oestrogen, DDI |
| Others | trauma, autoimmune (IgG4), infection (mumps), scorpion sting, idiopathic |

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Constant, severe **epigastric pain radiating to the back**, relieved by leaning forward, with nausea and vomiting. Exam: epigastric tenderness, ileus, low-grade fever, tachycardia; severe disease shows SIRS, hypotension, and rarely **Cullen's (periumbilical) or Grey-Turner's (flank) ecchymoses** (retroperitoneal haemorrhage — late, ominous). Dyspnoea suggests effusion/ARDS.

## 🩻 Investigations & diagnosis

**Diagnosis needs 2 of 3:** (1) characteristic abdominal pain; (2) **serum lipase or amylase ≥ 3× the upper limit of normal** (lipase is more sensitive/specific and stays elevated longer — prefer it); (3) characteristic imaging (CT/MRI/US). If pain + enzymes ≥ 3× ULN are both present, **CT is not needed to diagnose**.

- **US abdomen in every patient** to look for gallstones/biliary dilatation (identifies the treatable cause).
- **LFTs** (ALT > 3× ULN suggests gallstone aetiology), **calcium, triglycerides**, CBC, BUN/creatinine, glucose, electrolytes, CRP.
- **CT with contrast is NOT for early diagnosis** — reserve for uncertainty, or at **72 h to > 4 days** to assess necrosis/complications when not improving (early CT underestimates necrosis).
- **Enzyme levels do not correlate with severity** — do not trend lipase to gauge progress.

**Severity — revised Atlanta classification:**
- **Mild** — no organ failure, no local/systemic complications.
- **Moderately severe** — **transient organ failure (resolves ≤ 48 h)** and/or local complications (peripancreatic collection, pseudocyst, walled-off necrosis).
- **Severe** — **persistent organ failure (> 48 h)**, single or multi-organ.

Early predictors of severity: SIRS persisting > 48 h, **BUN ≥ 20 mg/dL or a rising BUN**, haemoconcentration (Hct), age, comorbidity, and CRP > 150 at 48 h. Scores (BISAP, APACHE-II, Ranson) supplement but do not replace serial clinical assessment. **A rising BUN at 24 h is the single best simple marker of inadequate resuscitation and worse outcome.**

## 🗂️ Differential diagnosis

Perforated peptic ulcer, acute cholecystitis/[[Acute Cholangitis|cholangitis]], mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured/leaking AAA, inferior MI, DKA (can raise amylase), bowel obstruction, ectopic pregnancy — the acute abdomen mimics.

## 💊 Management

**1. Fluid resuscitation — moderately aggressive, with Lactated Ringer's (2024 ACG / WATERFALL).**
- **Lactated Ringer's is preferred to normal saline** (less hyperchloraemic acidosis; possible anti-inflammatory benefit).
- **Moderately aggressive: ~1.5 mL/kg/hr**, with a **10 mL/kg bolus only if hypovolaemic/hypotensive.** The WATERFALL trial showed that **early *aggressive* fluids caused more fluid overload without benefit** — so the goal is euvolaemia, not maximal volume.
- **Reassess within 6 h and over the next 24–48 h** to targets: HR < 120, MAP 65–85, UOP > 0.5 mL/kg/hr, and a **falling BUN and Hct** — titrate down once these are met.

**2. Analgesia** — opioids (there is no evidence morphine worsens outcomes); antiemetics.

**3. Early enteral feeding — do NOT keep NPO.** Start **oral low-fat solid food within 24–48 h as tolerated** in mild AP (no need to wait for pain/enzymes to normalise or to "step up" from clear liquids). In moderately severe/severe AP unable to eat, use **early enteral (naso-gastric or naso-jejunal) nutrition**, which reduces infectious complications versus parenteral. Reserve TPN for enteral failure.

**4. Aetiology-directed therapy.**
- **Gallstone AP with concurrent acute cholangitis or persistent biliary obstruction → urgent ERCP within 24 h.** **Uncomplicated gallstone AP does NOT need early ERCP.**
- **Mild gallstone AP → same-admission (index) cholecystectomy** to prevent recurrence.
- **Hypertriglyceridaemic AP** → insulin infusion ± apheresis; **hypercalcaemia** → treat the cause.

**5. Antibiotics — NOT prophylactic.** **Do not give prophylactic antibiotics** even in severe/necrotising AP. Reserve for a proven or strongly suspected **infected necrosis** (clinical deterioration at 7–10 days, gas in a collection) — use carbapenem/agents that penetrate necrosis, aiming to **delay any drainage beyond 4 weeks (step-up: percutaneous/endoscopic before surgery)**; some infected collections resolve on antibiotics alone.

**6. ICU** for persistent organ failure, and manage complications (ARDS, AKI, abdominal compartment syndrome, pseudocyst, walled-off necrosis).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

All agents are on the Thai NLEM (Lactated Ringer's/Acetar, opioids, insulin, carbapenems). ERCP and cross-sectional imaging are available at Thai regional/tertiary centres; index-admission laparoscopic cholecystectomy for mild gallstone AP is standard. No Thai-specific pancreatitis CPG is on disk; management here follows ACG 2024 / revised Atlanta, consistent with Thai GI practice.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Do NOT flog with aggressive fluids** — WATERFALL showed harm (fluid overload, ARDS); target euvolaemia with moderately aggressive LR and titrate to a **falling BUN**.
> - **Do NOT give prophylactic antibiotics** in severe/necrotising AP — only for infected necrosis.
> - **Do NOT keep the patient NPO** — early low-fat oral (or enteral) feeding is protective.
> - **Concurrent cholangitis / biliary obstruction → urgent ERCP within 24 h**; don't delay in a septic obstructed patient.
> - Persistent organ failure > 48 h = **severe AP** → ICU.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Dx = 2 of 3**: pain · **lipase ≥ 3× ULN** · imaging. Enzyme level ≠ severity.
> - **Fluids: Lactated Ringer's, moderately aggressive ~1.5 mL/kg/hr**, bolus 10 mL/kg only if hypovolaemic; titrate to **falling BUN/Hct, UOP > 0.5** (WATERFALL/ACG 2024).
> - **Feed early** (low-fat solids 24–48 h); **no prophylactic antibiotics**.
> - **Gallstone AP**: US everyone · **ERCP < 24 h only if cholangitis/obstruction** · **index-admission cholecystectomy** for mild.
> - Severity (revised Atlanta): organ failure ≤ 48 h = moderately severe; **> 48 h = severe**.

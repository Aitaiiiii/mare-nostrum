# Acute Diverticulitis

Inflammation/infection of a colonic diverticulum, classically the sigmoid colon — the "left-sided appendicitis". The modern shift is that **most uncomplicated cases need little or no antibiotics and can be managed as outpatients**, while the ER task is to **use CT to separate uncomplicated from complicated disease** (abscess, perforation, obstruction, fistula), which changes management entirely.

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

Diverticula (mucosal herniations at points of vascular penetration) form with raised intraluminal pressure/low-fibre diet and ageing. Obstruction of a diverticulum (faecolith) → microperforation, localised inflammation/infection → **uncomplicated diverticulitis**; progression → **abscess, free perforation (purulent/faeculent peritonitis), obstruction, or fistula (colovesical → pneumaturia)**. Right-sided diverticulitis is more common in East Asian populations (a Thai-relevant point) and mimics appendicitis.

## 🩺 Presentation

**Left-lower-quadrant pain (constant), fever, altered bowel habit, nausea**, with LLQ tenderness ± a mass; peritonism/systemic toxicity signals complicated disease. Right-sided diverticulitis (more common in Asians) presents like appendicitis. Ask about prior episodes.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast is the investigation of choice** — confirms diverticulitis, **stages it (uncomplicated vs abscess/perforation/obstruction; Hinchey classification for perforated disease)**, and excludes mimics.
- Bloods: CBC, **CRP** (correlates with severity), U&E, lactate; urinalysis (fistula/UTI). Avoid colonoscopy in the acute phase (perforation risk); **do a colonoscopy at 6 weeks** after a complicated first episode to exclude malignancy.

## 💊 Management

**1. Uncomplicated diverticulitis — often no/selective antibiotics, frequently outpatient.** In immunocompetent, systemically-well patients, **antibiotics can be omitted or given orally** (equivalent outcomes — DINAMO and other RCTs), with **oral intake as tolerated and outpatient management** for those who can tolerate fluids and have adequate support. Analgesia (avoid NSAIDs/opioids where possible), oral antibiotics (e.g. amoxicillin-clavulanate, or ciprofloxacin + metronidazole) when used, and follow-up. Admit if unable to tolerate oral, significant comorbidity/immunosuppression, or failure to improve.

**2. Complicated diverticulitis — image-guided or surgical.**
- **Abscess:** small (< ~3–4 cm) → IV antibiotics; larger → **image-guided percutaneous drainage** + IV antibiotics.
- **Free perforation with purulent/faeculent peritonitis (Hinchey III–IV):** **emergency surgery** — traditionally **Hartmann's procedure** (resection + end colostomy); laparoscopic lavage or primary anastomosis in selected cases.
- **Obstruction/fistula:** surgery (often elective after optimisation).

**3. After recovery.** Colonoscopy at ~6 weeks (exclude malignancy) after complicated/first episodes; dietary fibre; discuss elective resection for recurrent/complicated disease case-by-case (no longer purely by episode count).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

CT, image-guided drainage, and colorectal surgery are available at Thai hospitals. **Right-sided diverticulitis is relatively more common in Thai/Asian patients** and mimics appendicitis — CT distinguishes them and spares an unnecessary appendicectomy. Antibiotic-sparing outpatient management of uncomplicated disease is applicable where follow-up is reliable.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **CT to separate uncomplicated from complicated** disease — it changes everything.
> - **Peritonitis/free perforation (Hinchey III–IV) → emergency surgery**; abscess → drainage.
> - **Uncomplicated disease often needs no/oral antibiotics** and outpatient care — avoid over-treating.
> - **Colonoscopy at ~6 weeks** after complicated/first episode (exclude malignancy) — not acutely.
> - Consider **right-sided diverticulitis** (Asian) mimicking appendicitis.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **LLQ pain + fever** ("left-sided appendicitis"); **CT is the test** (stages uncomplicated vs complicated; Hinchey).
> - **Uncomplicated → no/oral antibiotics + outpatient** (DINAMO) if well and tolerating oral.
> - **Abscess → percutaneous drainage**; **perforation/peritonitis (Hinchey III–IV) → emergency surgery (Hartmann's)**.
> - **Colonoscopy at 6 weeks** (malignancy); avoid acute colonoscopy.
> - Right-sided diverticulitis common in Asians — mimics appendicitis.

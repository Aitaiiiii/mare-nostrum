# Peptic Ulcer Disease & Perforation

Mucosal ulceration of the stomach/duodenum from an imbalance between aggressive factors (acid, pepsin, *H. pylori*, NSAIDs) and mucosal defence. This page centres on the **surgical emergency — perforation** — alongside the disease and its other complications (bleeding → [[UGIB (Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding)]], obstruction). A perforated ulcer is a classic acute abdomen where **delay to surgery costs lives.**

## 🧬 Aetiology & Pathophysiology

The two dominant causes: ***H. pylori*** (breaks mucosal defence, drives duodenal > gastric ulcers) and **NSAIDs/aspirin** (inhibit protective prostaglandins). Others: physiological stress (ICU stress ulcers), Zollinger-Ellison (gastrinoma), smoking, steroids-with-NSAID. **Complications:** bleeding (commonest), **perforation** (full-thickness → chemical then bacterial peritonitis), penetration, and gastric outlet obstruction. A gastric ulcer can be malignant — biopsy/recheck healing.

## 🩺 Presentation

**Uncomplicated PUD:** epigastric pain (duodenal — relieved by food, nocturnal; gastric — worse with food, weight loss), dyspepsia, nausea. **Perforation:** **sudden, severe, generalised abdominal pain**, a rigid "board-like" abdomen with guarding/rebound (peritonitis), shallow breathing, tachycardia, and progression to **septic shock**; bowel sounds absent; the patient lies still. **Alarm features** (weight loss, dysphagia, anaemia, vomiting, GI bleed, age > 50/60 with new dyspepsia) mandate endoscopy to exclude malignancy.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Perforation:** **erect CXR / abdominal film — free air under the diaphragm (pneumoperitoneum)** in ~70–80%; **CT abdomen** is more sensitive (free air, fluid, site). Bloods: CBC, U&E, **lactate**, amylase (can be mildly raised), coagulation, group & save, venous gas; septic screen.
- **Uncomplicated PUD:** **upper GI endoscopy** (diagnose, biopsy gastric ulcers for malignancy/*H. pylori*), **_H. pylori_ testing** (urea breath test/stool antigen/biopsy — see [[H. pylori Infection & Eradication]]).

## 💊 Management

**1. Perforated peptic ulcer — a surgical emergency.** **Resuscitate (IV fluids, correct physiology), NG decompression, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (Gram-negative + anaerobe cover), IV PPI, and analgesia — then urgent surgery.** Standard operation: **laparoscopic or open omental (Graham) patch repair + peritoneal lavage**; large/malignant/complex perforations may need resection. **Non-operative management** (NG, antibiotics, PPI) is only for highly selected, stable, contained perforations with no peritonitis. **Risk-stratify with the Boey score** (shock on admission, major medical illness, symptoms > 24 h) / ASA / PULP — higher scores predict mortality. **Test for _H. pylori_ and eradicate + stop NSAIDs** to prevent recurrence.

**2. Uncomplicated PUD.** **PPI** to heal the ulcer; **eradicate _H. pylori_** if positive; **stop NSAIDs/aspirin** (or co-prescribe a PPI if unavoidable); lifestyle. **Confirm healing of a gastric ulcer** (repeat endoscopy) to exclude malignancy. Bleeding ulcers → [[UGIB (Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding)]] (endoscopic haemostasis + IV PPI).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

PPIs, antibiotics, *H. pylori* eradication regimens, endoscopy, and laparoscopic/open surgery are available. NSAID use (often OTC, and in traditional/compounded "ยาชุด") and *H. pylori* are common Thai drivers of PUD and its complications; eradication + NSAID cessation are the key preventive steps. Perforated peptic ulcer is a frequent Thai acute-surgical admission.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Sudden severe generalised abdominal pain + peritonism + free air = perforation → resuscitate + IV antibiotics/PPI + urgent surgery** (don't delay).
> - **Sepsis drives mortality** — resuscitate aggressively; higher Boey/ASA score = higher risk.
> - **Biopsy/recheck a gastric ulcer** (malignancy) and **alarm features → endoscopy**.
> - **Test and eradicate _H. pylori_ and stop NSAIDs** to prevent recurrence/re-perforation.
> - Bleeding ulcer → endoscopic haemostasis + IV PPI (see UGIB).

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - PUD = *H. pylori* + NSAIDs; complications = **bleeding, perforation, obstruction**.
> - **Perforation: free air under diaphragm (CXR) / CT → resuscitate + broad IV antibiotics + PPI + urgent omental patch repair.**
> - **Boey/ASA/PULP** risk scores; sepsis = main cause of death.
> - **Eradicate H. pylori + stop NSAIDs**; recheck gastric ulcer healing (malignancy).
> - Alarm features (weight loss, dysphagia, anaemia, bleeding, age > 50) → endoscopy.

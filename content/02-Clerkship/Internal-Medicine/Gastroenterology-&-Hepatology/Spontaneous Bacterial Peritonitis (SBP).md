# Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)

Infection of ascitic fluid **without a surgical source**, a common and lethal complication of cirrhotic ascites. The single most important habit is to **perform a diagnostic paracentesis on every cirrhotic with ascites who is admitted or who deteriorates** — SBP is frequently silent, and untreated it causes hepatorenal syndrome, encephalopathy, and death.

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

Portal hypertension + gut oedema → **bacterial translocation** from the gut lumen into mesenteric nodes and ascitic fluid, with impaired opsonic/immune defence in cirrhotic ascites (low ascitic protein). Usually **monomicrobial and Gram-negative (_E. coli_, _Klebsiella_)** or streptococcal; polymicrobial/anaerobic or very high ascitic amylase suggests **secondary** (surgical) peritonitis instead. SBP triggers systemic inflammation and renal vasoconstriction → **hepatorenal syndrome (HRS)**.

## 🩺 Presentation

Often subtle: **fever, abdominal pain/tenderness, worsening ascites**, but frequently presents only as **new/worsening hepatic encephalopathy, deterioration, hypotension, ileus, or renal impairment** in a cirrhotic — hence the low threshold to tap. Some are asymptomatic.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Diagnostic paracentesis is mandatory** — send ascitic fluid for **cell count + differential, culture (inoculate blood-culture bottles at the bedside), albumin/protein, glucose, LDH.**
- **Diagnosis: ascitic polymorphonuclear (PMN) count ≥ 250 cells/mm³** (regardless of culture result — cultures are often negative). Treat on the PMN count, don't wait for culture.
- Distinguish **secondary peritonitis** (suspect if PMN very high + multiple organisms, or ≥ 2 of: ascitic protein > 1 g/dL, glucose < 50 mg/dL, LDH > upper serum limit → imaging/surgery). "Bacterascites" = positive culture but PMN < 250 (treat if symptomatic).
- Bloods: CBC, renal function (baseline for HRS), LFTs, coagulation, blood cultures.

## 💊 Management

**1. Empiric antibiotics immediately (after the tap).** **A third-generation cephalosporin — cefotaxime 2 g IV q8h (or ceftriaxone)** is first-line; broaden (piperacillin-tazobactam/carbapenem) for nosocomial/healthcare-associated SBP or where local resistance/ESBL is high. Typical course **5–7 days**; repeat the tap at 48 h if not improving (expect a ≥ 25% fall in PMN).

**2. IV albumin — to prevent hepatorenal syndrome.** **Albumin 1.5 g/kg on day 1 and 1 g/kg on day 3** reduces the incidence of HRS and mortality — give it especially when creatinine > 1 mg/dL, BUN > 30, or bilirubin > 4. This is a defining, evidence-based part of SBP treatment, not optional.

**3. Withhold nephrotoxins and non-selective β-blockers in the acute phase** (NSBBs can worsen haemodynamics in advanced/SBP settings), manage encephalopathy ([[Hepatic Encephalopathy]]), and treat the cirrhosis complications.

**4. Secondary prophylaxis (long-term).** After an SBP episode, give **long-term oral prophylaxis — norfloxacin (or ciprofloxacin/co-trimoxazole)** — as bridge to transplant. **Primary prophylaxis** in high-risk patients (low ascitic protein < 1.5 g/dL with renal/liver dysfunction, or during acute variceal bleeding — a 7-day course). Refer for transplant assessment (SBP marks advanced disease).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Cefotaxime/ceftriaxone, albumin, and norfloxacin/ciprofloxacin are on the Thai NLEM; ascitic-fluid analysis is routine. Given high Thai Gram-negative/ESBL resistance, tailor empiric cover to the local antibiogram (and broaden for nosocomial SBP). Cirrhosis from **alcohol, hepatitis B/C, and NAFLD** is common in Thailand, so SBP is a frequent ward emergency — the **"tap every decompensating cirrhotic"** rule is the key deliverable.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Tap EVERY cirrhotic with ascites who is admitted or deteriorating** — SBP is often silent (may present only as encephalopathy/renal decline).
> - **Treat on ascitic PMN ≥ 250** — don't wait for culture (often negative).
> - **Give IV albumin (1.5 g/kg day 1, 1 g/kg day 3)** — it prevents hepatorenal syndrome and saves lives.
> - **Consider secondary peritonitis** (surgical) if polymicrobial/very high PMN or the 2-of-3 exudate criteria → imaging/surgery.
> - Start **secondary prophylaxis** after an episode; refer for transplant.

# Caustic (Corrosive) Ingestion

Swallowing a strong acid or alkali (household cleaners, drain/toilet cleaner, bleach, battery fluid, descalers) causing chemical burns of the aerodigestive tract. Management is defined as much by **what NOT to do** (no emesis, lavage, charcoal, or "neutralisation") as by what to do — **protect the airway and endoscope early to stratify injury.**

## 🧬 Mechanism — acid vs alkali

- **Alkali (pH > 12; drain cleaner, lye)** → **liquefaction necrosis** → deep, penetrating injury, especially the **oesophagus** → higher perforation and stricture risk.
- **Acid (pH < 2; toilet/descaler, battery acid)** → **coagulation necrosis** (an eschar that can limit depth) → tends to injure the **stomach** more; systemic absorption can cause metabolic acidosis/haemolysis.
Both can cause airway (epiglottic/supraglottic) burns, oesophageal/gastric necrosis, perforation (mediastinitis/peritonitis), and — weeks later — **strictures** and (long-term) oesophageal carcinoma risk.

## 🩺 Presentation

Oral/retrosternal/epigastric pain, **odynophagia/dysphagia, drooling, hoarseness/stridor** (airway involvement), vomiting (± haematemesis), and, with perforation, chest/abdominal pain, subcutaneous emphysema, peritonism, and shock. **Absence of visible oral burns does NOT exclude significant oesophageal/gastric injury.** Assess intent/volume (deliberate ingestions tend to be larger/worse).

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Upper GI endoscopy at 12–48 hours** (up to ~96 h) — the key test to **grade injury (Zargar 0–4)** and guide management/prognosis. **Contraindicated** with suspected perforation, or severe supraglottic/hypopharyngeal oedema, or a haemodynamically unstable patient.
- **CT chest/abdomen** (contrast) increasingly used to assess transmural necrosis/perforation and need for surgery, especially in severe/unstable cases.
- CXR (free air/mediastinal air), bloods (CBC, gas/lactate, renal/liver, coagulation, group & save), and — deliberate overdose — **paracetamol/salicylate** co-ingestion.

## 💊 Management

**1. Airway first.** Early assessment; **early intubation (ideally endoscopic/fibreoptic)** for stridor/airway oedema — swelling progresses; a blind or delayed attempt is dangerous.

**2. Do NOT — the classic "don'ts":**
- **No induced vomiting** (re-exposes the oesophagus).
- **No gastric lavage / NG blind insertion** (perforation/re-injury).
- **No activated charcoal** (doesn't bind, obscures endoscopy).
- **No neutralising agents / no oral dilution with acid or base** (exothermic reaction worsens injury). (Immediate small sips of water/milk very early are debated — do not force fluids.)

**3. Supportive + stratified care.** NPO, IV fluids, analgesia, PPI; treat shock. Manage by endoscopic grade: **low grade (0–2a)** → supportive, early feeding, good prognosis; **high grade (2b–3)** → higher stricture/perforation risk, ICU, cautious nutrition (NJ/parenteral), surveillance; **grade 3b/4 or transmural necrosis/perforation → emergency surgery** (oesophagogastrectomy) — a surgical emergency with high mortality. **Steroids are not routinely recommended** (no clear stricture-prevention benefit; may mask infection).

**4. Follow-up.** Monitor for **strictures** (dysphagia over weeks → endoscopic dilatation), and counsel on the **long-term oesophageal cancer risk**. Psychiatric assessment for deliberate ingestion; poison-centre advice; public-health/child-safety measures for accidental (paediatric) exposures.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

Endoscopy, CT, PPI, and surgical services are available at Thai hospitals; the **Ramathibodi Poison Center (1367)** advises. Accessible household corrosives (drain cleaners, toilet acids, bleach) cause both accidental paediatric and deliberate adult ingestions in Thailand — the deliverables are airway protection, the "don'ts", and timely endoscopy/transfer.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Secure the airway early** (stridor/oedema → early fibreoptic intubation).
> - **Do NOT induce vomiting, lavage, give charcoal, or neutralise** — all worsen injury.
> - **Absence of oral burns does not exclude serious oesophageal/gastric injury.**
> - **Suspected perforation (chest/abdo pain, free air, sepsis) → CT + emergency surgery**; endoscopy contraindicated then.
> - **Endoscope at 12–48 h** to grade; steroids not routine; watch for later strictures + cancer risk.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Alkali = liquefaction (deep, oesophagus); acid = coagulation (stomach).**
> - **Airway first**; **NO emesis / lavage / charcoal / neutralisation.**
> - **Upper endoscopy at 12–48 h → Zargar grade** guides management (contraindicated if perforation/unstable/severe supraglottic oedema).
> - **Grade 3b/4 or perforation → emergency surgery**; low grade → supportive + early feeding.
> - Follow for **strictures** (dilatation) and long-term **oesophageal cancer**; psych review if deliberate.

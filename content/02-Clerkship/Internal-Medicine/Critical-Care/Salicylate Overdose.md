# Salicylate (Aspirin) Overdose

A deceptively dangerous poisoning: the classic teaching is that **the sicker the patient looks, the more urgent dialysis is — and that intubating a salicylate-poisoned patient can kill them.** Toxicity uncouples oxidative phosphorylation and stimulates the respiratory centre, producing a characteristic **mixed acid–base picture** and a narrow therapeutic path.

## 🧬 Mechanism & acid–base

Salicylate **directly stimulates the medullary respiratory centre → respiratory alkalosis**, and **uncouples oxidative phosphorylation + inhibits Krebs enzymes → a high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis** (lactate, ketones), hyperthermia (heat from uncoupling), and increased tissue glucose use (CNS neuroglycopenia despite a normal serum glucose). **Acidaemia is dangerous** because it shifts salicylate to its non-ionised form, which crosses into the CNS — so **anything that lowers pH (hypoventilation/intubation) can precipitate catastrophic CNS toxicity.**

## 🩺 Presentation

**Tinnitus/deafness, nausea/vomiting, hyperventilation (tachypnoea/hyperpnoea), diaphoresis, hyperthermia**, and in severe cases agitation → confusion → coma, seizures, non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, and cardiovascular collapse. **Classic ABG: a mixed respiratory alkalosis + high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis** (a normal or high pH early, becoming acidaemic and ominous late — especially in children/late presentation). Chronic salicylism (elderly, therapeutic misadventure) is easily missed and often sicker at a lower level.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Serum salicylate level — serial (every 2 h until falling)**, because levels can rise with ongoing absorption (enteric-coated/bezoar); a single level can mislead.
- **ABG/VBG + anion gap**, electrolytes (**K⁺**), **glucose**, renal function, lactate, coagulation, and **paracetamol level** (common co-ingestant).
- Note: the Done nomogram is unreliable — treat by the clinical picture + serial levels, not a single number.

## 💊 Management

**1. Resuscitate + glucose + potassium.** IV fluids for volume depletion; **give dextrose even if serum glucose is normal** (CNS neuroglycopenia), especially with altered mentation; **replete potassium aggressively** — you cannot alkalinise the urine while hypokalaemic (the kidney secretes H⁺ to retain K⁺).

**2. Alkalinisation — serum and urine.** **Sodium bicarbonate** (bolus then infusion, e.g. bicarbonate added to dextrose water) to **alkalinise the serum (keeps salicylate out of the CNS) and the urine (target urine pH ~7.5–8, which increases renal clearance ~10–20-fold)**. Monitor K⁺ and urine pH closely.

**3. Activated charcoal** if early/ongoing absorption (and consider repeat doses for enteric-coated ingestions), airway protected.

**4. Haemodialysis — for severe toxicity.** Indications: **serum level very high (commonly > 90 mg/dL acute, or > 60 mg/dL chronic/symptomatic)**, altered mental status/seizures, pulmonary or cerebral oedema, renal failure, severe acidaemia refractory to bicarbonate, or clinical deterioration. **Call nephrology early** — there are intrinsic delays to starting dialysis.

**5. AVOID intubation if at all possible.** A salicylate patient hyperventilates to protect their pH; **sedation/intubation drops minute ventilation → acidaemia → CNS salicylate shift → arrest.** If intubation is unavoidable, **match their high minute ventilation, give bicarbonate around the procedure, and use ketamine/awake technique** — this is a high-risk manoeuvre best done by seniors with dialysis ready.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Aspirin and salicylate-containing products (including some traditional/topical preparations and oil of wintergreen) are accessible in Thailand; sodium bicarbonate, dextrose, potassium, and haemodialysis are available. Recognise chronic salicylism in unwell elderly patients on aspirin, and always check a **paracetamol** level in intentional overdose.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **AVOID intubation** unless unavoidable — hypoventilation/acidaemia can be rapidly fatal; if you must, match minute ventilation + bicarbonate + dialysis ready.
> - **Acidaemia is dangerous** — keep the serum alkalotic; don't let pH fall.
> - **Repeat serum levels** (ongoing absorption; enteric-coated) — one level can under-call it.
> - **Replete K⁺** (can't alkalinise urine while hypokalaemic); **give glucose even if serum glucose is normal** (neuroglycopenia).
> - **Dialyse early** for high level / AMS / pulmonary or cerebral oedema / renal failure / refractory acidosis.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Tinnitus + hyperventilation + hyperthermia + mixed respiratory alkalosis & high-AG metabolic acidosis.**
> - **Serial levels**; treat by picture, not a single number (Done nomogram unreliable).
> - **Fluids + K⁺ + glucose + sodium bicarbonate** (serum & urine alkalinisation, urine pH ~8).
> - **Haemodialysis** for level > ~90 (acute)/> 60 (chronic), AMS, pulmonary/cerebral oedema, renal failure, refractory acidosis.
> - **Avoid intubation** (fatal acidaemia); check **paracetamol** co-ingestion.

# Heat Stroke

A failure of thermoregulation producing **core temperature > 40 °C with central nervous system dysfunction** and a systemic inflammatory response — the most severe heat illness and a true emergency. The single determinant of survival is **how fast the patient is cooled**: the treatment is not a drug, it is **rapid cooling to a target**, and every minute above ~40 °C adds organ damage.

## 🧬 Classification & Pathophysiology

Heat gain overwhelms heat loss → cellular thermal injury, endothelial damage, a cytokine surge resembling sepsis, and a coagulation cascade toward DIC, culminating in multi-organ dysfunction. Two forms:

| | **Exertional** | **Classic (non-exertional)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | young, fit, athletes/soldiers/labourers in heat | elderly, chronically ill, infants; heat waves |
| Onset | rapid, during exertion | over days |
| Sweating | often **still sweating** | usually **anhidrotic (dry, hot skin)** |
| Extras | rhabdomyolysis, lactic acidosis, hyperkalaemia common | comorbidity, drugs impairing thermoregulation |

**Heat illness is a spectrum:** heat cramps → heat exhaustion (core < 40 °C, CNS intact, salvageable with rest/fluids) → **heat stroke (> 40 °C + CNS dysfunction)** — the CNS change is what defines heat stroke.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Hyperthermia (> 40 °C, measure CORE — rectal) + altered mental status** (confusion, agitation, ataxia, seizures, coma), with tachycardia, hypotension, tachypnoea, and hot skin (dry in classic, may be sweaty in exertional). Complications define severity: **rhabdomyolysis → AKI**, hepatic injury, **DIC/bleeding**, ARDS, and cardiovascular collapse.

## 🩻 Investigations (do NOT delay cooling for them)

**Core (rectal) temperature** continuously. Bloods: CBC, coagulation/DIC screen, electrolytes (**K⁺**), BUN/creatinine, glucose, **CK** (rhabdomyolysis), liver enzymes (often markedly ↑ at 24–48 h), ABG/lactate, and **exclude mimics** — sepsis, [[Thyroid Storm]], anticholinergic/sympathomimetic toxidromes, neuroleptic malignant / serotonin syndrome, and CNS infection (a low threshold for LP if meningitis is possible). Capillary glucose (exclude hypoglycaemia as a cause of altered mentation).

## 💊 Management — cooling IS the treatment

**1. Cool immediately and fast — target < 39 °C within 30 minutes.**
- **Cold-water (ice-water) immersion is the fastest and gold-standard method** for exertional heat stroke (cools ~1 °C per 5 min); if immersion is unavailable, use **evaporative cooling** (spray tepid water + fans), ice packs to neck/axillae/groin, cooling blankets, and cold IV fluids.
- **"Cool first, transport second"** where aggressive cooling is available on-site — do not delay cooling to move the patient.
- **Stop cooling at a core (rectal) temperature of ~39 °C** to avoid overshoot hypothermia.
- **Antipyretics (paracetamol, NSAIDs) do NOT work** — the set-point is normal; they are ineffective and may add hepatic/renal/coagulation harm. **Dantrolene is not effective** in heat stroke (unlike malignant hyperthermia).

**2. Airway, breathing, circulation.** Protect the airway (intubate if coma/seizing), high-flow O₂, and **isotonic crystalloid** for hypotension (guided — heat stroke shock is partly distributive; avoid over-resuscitation). Benzodiazepines control shivering (which generates heat) and seizures.

**3. Treat the complications.** **Rhabdomyolysis** → fluids + monitor CK/K⁺, protect renal function ([[Acute Kidney Injury]]); **hyperkalaemia** → treat; **DIC** → supportive/product replacement; monitor for hepatic failure and ARDS. ICU admission for organ support.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

No specific drug is needed — cooling equipment (immersion, fans, ice, cooling blankets, cold IV fluids) and ICU support are the requirements, all available. **Thailand's hot season (esp. Songkran/April) drives classic heat stroke** in the elderly and outdoor workers, and exertional heat stroke in military recruits/athletes — a recognised Thai public-health concern with annual advisories. Alcohol, dehydration, and drugs impairing sweating raise the risk.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Cooling is the emergency** — cool to < 39 °C within 30 min; "cool first, transport second".
> - **Antipyretics and dantrolene do NOT work** — do not rely on paracetamol/NSAIDs.
> - **Measure CORE (rectal) temperature** and stop cooling at ~39 °C (avoid overshoot).
> - **Anticipate rhabdomyolysis/AKI, DIC, and hepatic failure** — check CK, coagulation, K⁺, LFTs.
> - Exclude mimics: sepsis, thyroid storm, toxidromes, NMS/serotonin syndrome, CNS infection.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Heat stroke = core > 40 °C + CNS dysfunction**; exertional (sweaty, young) vs classic (dry, elderly).
> - **Cool fast to < 39 °C within 30 min — cold-water immersion is fastest** (~1 °C/5 min); cool first, transport second.
> - **Antipyretics/dantrolene ineffective**; benzodiazepine for shivering/seizures.
> - Watch **rhabdomyolysis → AKI, DIC, hepatic failure, hyperkalaemia** → ICU.
> - Thai hot-season disease (Songkran/April) — elderly + outdoor workers + recruits.

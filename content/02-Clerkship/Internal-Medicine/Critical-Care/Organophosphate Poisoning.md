# Organophosphate & Carbamate Poisoning

Poisoning by acetylcholinesterase (AChE)-inhibiting insecticides — a leading cause of self-poisoning in agricultural Thailand and a true resuscitation emergency. The clinical picture is a **cholinergic crisis**; treatment is **atropine titrated to effect + an oxime (pralidoxime) + aggressive airway/ventilatory support**, with the trap being **the intermediate syndrome** (delayed respiratory failure 1–4 days later).

## 🧬 Mechanism

Organophosphates phosphorylate and irreversibly inactivate AChE (carbamates do so reversibly, so they are shorter-lived and oximes are usually unnecessary), causing **acetylcholine accumulation** at muscarinic, nicotinic, and CNS synapses. Over hours the OP-AChE bond **"ages"** (loses an alkyl group) and becomes permanent — which is why **oximes must be given early, before ageing**. Toxicity ranges by agent lipophilicity and dose.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation — three receptor groups

| Receptor | Effects | Mnemonic |
|---|---|---|
| **Muscarinic** | miosis, bronchorrhoea/bronchospasm, bradycardia, salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, emesis, sweating | **SLUDGE / DUMBELS** |
| **Nicotinic** | fasciculations, muscle weakness, paralysis, tachycardia, hypertension, mydriasis (mixed picture) | "days of the week" (MTWtF) |
| **CNS** | agitation, confusion, seizures, coma, central respiratory depression | |

**The killers are respiratory:** bronchorrhoea + bronchospasm ("the patient drowns in secretions"), respiratory-muscle weakness, and central apnoea. A **garlic/solvent odour** and a history of pesticide contact/ingestion support the diagnosis.

> [!note] 💎 Pearls
> - **Bradycardia + miosis + copious secretions** distinguishes the cholinergic toxidrome from anticholinergic (dry, mydriatic, tachycardic).
> - **Intermediate syndrome** (24–96 h after resolution of the acute crisis): proximal muscle, neck-flexor, cranial-nerve and **respiratory-muscle weakness → respiratory failure** — anticipate and monitor; it is not a relapse of the acute crisis and does not respond to more oxime.
> - **OP-induced delayed polyneuropathy (OPIDN)** appears 2–3 weeks later (distal sensorimotor).

## 🩻 Investigations

Clinical diagnosis — **do not delay antidotes for tests.** Supportive: **red-cell (or plasma/butyrylcholinesterase) cholinesterase activity** (low, confirms exposure, correlates loosely with severity); ABG, electrolytes, glucose, ECG (QTc, bradyarrhythmia), CK, paracetamol/co-ingestant screen. Continuous SpO₂ and cardiac monitoring.

## 💊 Management

**1. Resuscitation + staff protection.** **Airway, high-flow O₂, suction secretions**; intubate for respiratory failure/secretions/coma (**avoid suxamethonium — prolonged block via cholinesterase inhibition**; use a non-depolarising agent). Staff wear **PPE**; remove the patient's clothes and **wash the skin** (dermal absorption continues) — decontaminate before/alongside treatment. Consider activated charcoal only if a recent large ingestion and airway protected.

**2. Atropine — titrate to atropinisation (the priority antidote).** **Give 2–5 mg IV (child 0.05 mg/kg) and DOUBLE the dose every 5 minutes until "atropinised"** — the endpoints are **clear chest on auscultation (dried secretions), heart rate > 80, systolic BP > 80, and no bronchorrhoea/bronchospasm** (pupil size and dry mouth are less reliable targets). Then start a **maintenance infusion at ~10–20% of the total loading dose per hour**, titrated. Large cumulative doses (tens–hundreds of mg) are often needed — do not under-dose.

**3. Pralidoxime (2-PAM) / oxime — reactivates AChE (nicotinic + muscarinic).** Give to **all symptomatic patients needing atropine, as early as possible** (before ageing; within ~48 h). **Loading dose pralidoxime ~30 mg/kg (commonly 1–2 g) IV over 20–30 min, then a continuous infusion ~8 mg/kg/hr** (WHO). It targets the nicotinic (muscle-weakness/respiratory) features that atropine cannot. (Evidence of mortality benefit is debated, but it remains standard.) **Carbamate poisoning usually does not need an oxime.**

**4. Benzodiazepines** for seizures/agitation (diazepam/lorazepam) — also neuroprotective.

**5. Anticipate and support the intermediate syndrome** — serial respiratory assessment (vital capacity, single-breath count), ICU, and mechanical ventilation for 1–3 weeks if it develops. Continue atropine/oxime as guided; wean atropine slowly.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

**Atropine, pralidoxime, and diazepam are on the Thai NLEM and stocked in Thai EDs** — OP self-poisoning is common in rural/agricultural Thailand, so familiarity with high-dose atropine titration is essential. Glyphosate and paraquat (a different, non-cholinergic, highly lethal herbicide with no antidote — pulmonary fibrosis) are separate agricultural poisonings to distinguish. Thai poison-information support: **Ramathibodi Poison Center (1367)**.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Respiratory failure from bronchorrhoea/weakness/central apnoea is the killer** — secure the airway and give **enough atropine** (double q5min to a dry chest).
> - **Do NOT under-dose atropine** — titrate to atropinisation endpoints (clear chest, HR > 80, SBP > 80), not to a fixed dose.
> - **Give the oxime early** (before ageing) to any patient needing atropine.
> - **Avoid suxamethonium** (prolonged paralysis); protect staff with **PPE + decontamination** (ongoing dermal absorption).
> - **Anticipate the intermediate syndrome** (day 1–4 respiratory failure) — do not discharge early after the acute crisis resolves.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Cholinergic crisis = **SLUDGE/DUMBELS + nicotinic weakness + CNS**; **bradycardia + miosis + secretions**.
> - **Atropine 2–5 mg IV, DOUBLE q5min to atropinisation** (clear chest, HR > 80, SBP > 80), then infusion 10–20%/hr.
> - **Pralidoxime load ~30 mg/kg (1–2 g) IV then ~8 mg/kg/hr** — early, before ageing; not needed for carbamates.
> - **Airway + suction + O₂**, avoid suxamethonium, PPE + skin decontamination, benzodiazepine for seizures.
> - **Intermediate syndrome day 1–4 → respiratory failure** — anticipate, monitor, ventilate.

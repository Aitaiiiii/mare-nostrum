# Serotonin Syndrome & Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome

Two life-threatening **drug-induced hyperthermic syndromes** that overlap (altered mentation, autonomic instability, hyperthermia, rigidity) but have opposite neuromuscular signatures and different antidotes. The ER task is to **recognise a drug-induced hyperthermic toxidrome, stop the culprit, cool aggressively, and distinguish the two** by onset and neuromuscular findings.

## 🧬 Mechanism & the key contrast

| | **Serotonin syndrome (SS)** | **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **excess serotonergic activity** — SSRIs/SNRIs, MAOIs, tramadol/meperidine, linezolid, triptans, ondansetron, methylene blue, St John's wort (often a **new drug/interaction**) | **dopamine (D2) blockade** — antipsychotics, metoclopramide; or **withdrawal of dopaminergic drugs** (Parkinson's meds) |
| **Onset** | **rapid — within ~24 h** of starting/increasing a drug | **slow — over days** |
| Neuromuscular | **neuromuscular EXCITABILITY: clonus (esp. lower-limb/ocular), hyperreflexia, myoclonus, tremor** — greater in the legs | **"lead-pipe" rigidity, bradyreflexia** (sluggish) |
| Other | GI (diarrhoea), shivering, mydriasis, agitation | very high **CK** (rhabdomyolysis), leukocytosis, diaphoresis |
| Resolution | usually resolves in **~24 h** after stopping the drug | prolonged (**days–weeks**) |

Both share **hyperthermia (can be extreme), autonomic instability, and altered mental status**, and both can cause rhabdomyolysis, DIC, AKI, and death from hyperthermia.

## 🩺 Diagnosis

- **Serotonin syndrome — Hunter criteria** (in the context of a serotonergic agent): **spontaneous clonus**, OR inducible clonus + agitation/diaphoresis, OR ocular clonus + agitation/diaphoresis, OR tremor + hyperreflexia, OR hypertonia + temperature > 38 °C + ocular/inducible clonus. **Clonus and hyperreflexia are the discriminators.**
- **NMS** — a dopamine antagonist (or dopaminergic withdrawal) + the tetrad **hyperthermia, "lead-pipe" rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability**, with **markedly raised CK**.
- Exclude/consider mimics: [[Heat Stroke|heat stroke]], anticholinergic toxidrome, malignant hyperthermia (anaesthetic trigger), CNS infection ([[Bacterial Meningitis]]), thyroid storm, [[Status Epilepticus]], sepsis.

## 🩻 Investigations

Temperature (core), CK (rhabdomyolysis — very high in NMS), electrolytes, renal/liver function, venous gas/lactate, coagulation (DIC), CBC, glucose, and a drug history/timeline (the diagnosis is clinical + medication-based). Septic and CNS work-up as needed to exclude mimics.

## 💊 Management (both: stop the drug + cool + benzodiazepines + support)

**1. Stop the offending agent** (all serotonergic drugs in SS; the neuroleptic in NMS, or restart dopaminergic therapy if withdrawal-induced) and admit to a monitored/ICU setting.

**2. Aggressive cooling + benzodiazepines + supportive care (for both).** **External cooling** (and for severe hyperthermia, active measures); **benzodiazepines** to control agitation, rigidity-driven heat generation, and autonomic hyperactivity; **IV fluids** and treat **rhabdomyolysis/AKI, hyperkalaemia, and DIC.** **For extreme hyperthermia with rigidity, consider sedation, paralysis (non-depolarising), and intubation** to abolish heat production — a definitive step when temperature is dangerously high. Antipyretics do not work (the set-point is normal).

**3. Serotonin syndrome — specific:** **cyproheptadine** (a 5-HT antagonist), oral/NG, for moderate–severe cases not responding to supportive care. **Avoid antipsychotics with intrinsic serotonergic/anti-dopaminergic effects** as a "treatment," and **do NOT use bromocriptine (a serotonin agonist — worsens SS).**

**4. NMS — specific:** **dantrolene** (reduces muscle heat production) for severe rigidity/hyperthermia, and/or **bromocriptine/amantadine** (dopaminergic) — the opposite of SS. **Avoid further dopamine antagonists.**

**5. Undifferentiated overlap:** start supportive care + **cyproheptadine** first (trial); if no response in 24–48 h, SS is less likely → consider **dantrolene/bromocriptine** for NMS. Avoid the agents contraindicated in each (bromocriptine in SS, dopamine blockers/chlorpromazine in NMS).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Benzodiazepines, cooling measures, dantrolene, bromocriptine/amantadine, and cyproheptadine are available in Thailand (dantrolene stock may be limited to centres — check locally). Both syndromes are drug-induced and increasingly seen with **widespread SSRI/antipsychotic use and tramadol** (a serotonergic analgesic common in Thai practice), so a careful medication timeline is the diagnostic key.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Stop the offending drug immediately**; cool aggressively — **hyperthermia is the killer** (antipyretics don't work).
> - **Distinguish by onset + neuromuscular signs**: SS = rapid, **clonus/hyperreflexia**; NMS = slow, **lead-pipe rigidity/very high CK**.
> - **Bromocriptine worsens serotonin syndrome**; **do not give more dopamine antagonists in NMS**.
> - **Extreme hyperthermia + rigidity → sedate/paralyse/intubate** to stop heat production.
> - Watch **rhabdomyolysis/AKI, hyperkalaemia, DIC**; consider **tramadol/linezolid/MAOI interactions** for SS.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **SS**: serotonergic drug, **onset < 24 h**, **clonus + hyperreflexia** (legs), Hunter criteria → **cyproheptadine**.
> - **NMS**: dopamine blocker, **onset days**, **lead-pipe rigidity + bradyreflexia + very high CK** → **dantrolene ± bromocriptine**.
> - **Both**: stop drug + aggressive cooling + **benzodiazepines** + fluids; severe → sedate/paralyse/intubate.
> - **Bromocriptine contraindicated in SS**; more dopamine blockers contraindicated in NMS.
> - Antipyretics ineffective; exclude heat stroke/malignant hyperthermia/CNS infection/thyroid storm.

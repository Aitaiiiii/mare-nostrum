# Beta-Blocker & Calcium-Channel-Blocker Overdose

Overdose of these cardiovascular drugs causes **bradycardia and refractory hypotension/cardiogenic shock** and is a leading cause of cardiovascular-drug death. Management is a **stepwise escalation** through standard resuscitation, specific antidotes (calcium, glucagon), **high-dose insulin euglycaemic therapy (HIET)**, vasopressors, and — for refractory shock — lipid emulsion and ECMO.

## 🧬 Mechanism & how they differ

- **Beta-blockers** → β1 antagonism → ↓heart rate, ↓contractility, ↓cAMP; **propranolol** adds **sodium-channel blockade (wide QRS, seizures)** and lipophilic CNS toxicity; **sotalol** adds **QT prolongation/torsades**.
- **Calcium-channel blockers** → block L-type Ca channels: **non-dihydropyridines (verapamil, diltiazem)** → bradycardia + ↓contractility (most lethal); **dihydropyridines (amlodipine)** → vasodilatory shock (reflex tachycardia early). CCB toxicity impairs insulin release → **hyperglycaemia** (a useful clue) and shifts the myocardium to carbohydrate dependence (rationale for HIET).

## 🩺 Presentation

**Bradycardia + hypotension**, with conduction blocks, cardiogenic/vasodilatory shock, altered mental status, and (BB, esp. propranolol) seizures. **Hyperglycaemia points to CCB; relative euglycaemia/hypoglycaemia to BB.** Onset can be delayed with **sustained-release preparations** (a well-looking patient can crash hours later).

## 🩻 Investigations

ECG (bradycardia, blocks, QRS/QT), continuous monitoring; **glucose** (hyperglycaemia = CCB), electrolytes (K⁺), venous gas/lactate, renal function, **paracetamol + salicylate** and coingestant screen; consider drug levels only where relevant (digoxin if unclear).

## 💊 Management (stepwise, escalate early in severe toxicity)

**1. Resuscitation + first-line.** IV crystalloid for hypotension; **atropine** for symptomatic bradycardia (often ineffective in significant toxicity). Activated charcoal if early/airway protected; **whole-bowel irrigation** for sustained-release ingestions.

**2. Specific antidotes.**
- **Calcium** (calcium gluconate or chloride, repeated/infusion) — especially CCB; overcomes channel blockade.
- **Glucagon** (bolus then infusion) — especially BB; increases cAMP independently of β-receptors (causes vomiting — protect airway).

**3. High-dose insulin euglycaemic therapy (HIET) — a key inotrope in severe toxicity.** **Insulin 1 U/kg IV bolus, then 1–10 U/kg/h infusion** (titrate up; higher rates used), with **dextrose to maintain euglycaemia** and **potassium repletion**. **Monitor glucose every ~20 min initially then hourly, and monitor K⁺.** HIET improves myocardial glucose uptake/contractility and takes ~15–60 min to work — **start early, don't wait for arrest.**

**4. Vasopressors + refractory therapies.** **Noradrenaline/adrenaline** for shock (HIET should be combined with a vasopressor, not used alone). Refractory: **IV lipid emulsion** (esp. lipophilic BB like propranolol), **methylene blue** (vasoplegia), **transvenous/transcutaneous pacing** (limited benefit), and **VA-ECMO** for refractory cardiogenic shock/arrest — the definitive rescue with good outcomes reported. **Sotalol torsades → magnesium**; propranolol wide-QRS → sodium bicarbonate.

**5. Disposition.** Prolonged monitored/ICU observation, especially for **sustained-release** ingestions; psychiatric assessment (deliberate).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Atropine, calcium, glucagon (may be limited/expensive in some Thai hospitals), insulin/dextrose, vasopressors, lipid emulsion, and pacing are available; ECMO at tertiary centres. Amlodipine, verapamil/diltiazem, and various β-blockers are widely prescribed for hypertension/arrhythmia in Thailand and are common deliberate-overdose agents. Where glucagon is scarce, **HIET + calcium + vasopressors** carry the resuscitation.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Sustained-release preparations delay toxicity** — a stable patient can crash; monitor long, whole-bowel irrigation.
> - **Start HIET early** in severe toxicity (don't wait for arrest); combine with a vasopressor, not alone.
> - **HIET → hypoglycaemia + hypokalaemia** — glucose q20min then hourly, replace K⁺.
> - **Refractory shock → lipid emulsion / VA-ECMO** (bridge to drug clearance — good recovery).
> - Hyperglycaemia = think **CCB**; propranolol → wide QRS/seizures; sotalol → torsades.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Bradycardia + hypotension**; **hyperglycaemia → CCB**, euglycaemia → BB; SR preparations = delayed crash.
> - Escalate: **fluids/atropine → calcium + glucagon → HIET (1 U/kg bolus then 1–10 U/kg/h + dextrose + K⁺) + vasopressors → lipid emulsion/ECMO**.
> - **Start HIET early**; monitor glucose q20min + K⁺.
> - Propranolol → **bicarbonate** (wide QRS); sotalol → **magnesium** (torsades).
> - Whole-bowel irrigation for SR; prolonged monitoring; psych review.

# Electrical Injury

Injury from the passage of electrical current through the body, ranging from a minor shock to cardiac arrest and massive deep-tissue destruction. The defining principle is that **the visible skin injury vastly underestimates the internal damage** — current follows the path of least resistance through vessels, nerves, and muscle, so the dangers are **cardiac (arrhythmia/arrest)** and **deep muscle necrosis with rhabdomyolysis and compartment syndrome.**

## 🧬 Determinants & Pathophysiology

Severity depends on **voltage (high ≥ 1000 V vs low), current type (AC vs DC — AC causes tetanic muscle contraction that can "lock on" and worsen exposure and cause VF), current path (hand-to-hand or transthoracic = cardiac/respiratory), resistance, and duration.** Tissue injury is thermal (deep muscle heats most) → **rhabdomyolysis → myoglobinuric AKI**; current across the heart → **VF/asystole/arrhythmia**; across the thorax → respiratory-muscle tetany/arrest. **Lightning** is a massive brief DC exposure (flashover) → cardiac arrest (often asystole with spontaneous cardiac recovery but persistent respiratory arrest → prolonged apnoea → secondary hypoxic arrest), tympanic rupture, keraunoparalysis, and cataracts.

## 🩺 Presentation

Entry/exit skin burns (small, deceptive), deep tissue/limb injury, **cardiac arrest or arrhythmia**, altered consciousness/neurological deficits (often transient), **rhabdomyolysis** (dark urine, swollen tender muscles), and **associated blunt trauma/fractures** (tetanic contraction or a fall/being thrown — including **cervical-spine injury**). Cataracts and neurological sequelae may be delayed.

## 🩻 Investigations

**ECG + continuous cardiac monitoring**, **CK + urine myoglobin** (rhabdomyolysis), U&E/creatinine, **potassium**, venous gas, CBC, coagulation; troponin if cardiac concern; trauma survey/imaging (c-spine, fractures) per mechanism. Assess limbs for **compartment syndrome** (pain, tense, distal ischaemia).

## 💊 Management

**1. Scene safety + resuscitation.** Ensure the power source is off before contact. **Cardiac arrest → standard [[CPR - ACLS|ACLS]]** (prolonged resuscitation is worthwhile, especially in lightning — "reverse triage": treat the apparently-dead first, as they may have a recoverable respiratory arrest). Treat as **trauma** (c-spine/ATLS) given the injury mechanism.

**2. Cardiac monitoring.** **Continuous ECG monitoring** for arrhythmia; admit those with **any ECG abnormality, arrest/LOC, chest pain/arrhythmia, high-voltage exposure, or transthoracic current path.** A low-voltage exposure with a **normal ECG and no symptoms/LOC** can often be discharged after observation.

**3. Rhabdomyolysis & fluids.** **Aggressive IV fluids titrated to a higher urine output** (aim ~1–1.5 mL/kg/h, higher if pigmenturia) to protect the kidney; monitor **CK, potassium, and renal function** (see [[Rhabdomyolysis]]). Do not under-resuscitate — **the internal muscle injury (and thus fluid needs) far exceeds the visible burn** (standard burn/Parkland estimates underestimate electrical injury).

**4. Deep injury & surgery.** **Compartment syndrome → fasciotomy**; extensive necrosis → debridement/possible amputation; escharotomy for circumferential burns. Wound care, **tetanus prophylaxis**, analgesia; ophthalmology (cataracts) and neurology follow-up for sequelae.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Cardiac monitoring, IV fluids, dialysis, and surgery are available; electrical injuries occur with **occupational/agricultural exposure, unsafe wiring, and flooding**, and **lightning strikes** are a recognised Thai outdoor/rural hazard (farm workers). The deliverables: scene safety, ACLS, cardiac monitoring, aggressive fluids for rhabdomyolysis, and vigilance for compartment syndrome and occult trauma.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Visible skin burns underestimate deep injury** — resuscitate for the internal muscle damage (fluids > burn-formula estimate).
> - **Continuous cardiac monitoring**; admit ECG abnormality/arrest/LOC/high-voltage/transthoracic path.
> - **Rhabdomyolysis → aggressive fluids to a higher urine output**; watch **hyperkalaemia** and **compartment syndrome (fasciotomy)**.
> - **Treat as trauma** (c-spine, fractures from tetany/fall).
> - **Lightning: prolonged CPR + "reverse triage"** (recoverable respiratory arrest); expect apnoea outlasting the arrhythmia.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Injury = **current path + voltage + AC/DC + duration**; AC "lock-on"/VF; transthoracic → cardiac/respiratory arrest.
> - **ECG + cardiac monitoring**; admit if abnormal/arrest/LOC/high-voltage/transthoracic; low-voltage + normal ECG/asymptomatic → discharge after observation.
> - **Rhabdomyolysis: aggressive fluids to UOP ~1–1.5 mL/kg/h**; monitor CK/K⁺/renal; **fasciotomy** for compartment syndrome.
> - **Deep injury ≫ skin burn** — under-resuscitation is the trap; treat as trauma.
> - **Lightning → prolonged CPR, reverse triage**; late cataracts/neuro sequelae.

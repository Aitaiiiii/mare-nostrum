# Toxic Alcohol Poisoning (Methanol & Ethylene Glycol)

Ingestion of **methanol** (windshield fluid, illicit/adulterated liquor, solvents) or **ethylene glycol** (antifreeze/coolant) — the parent alcohols are relatively harmless, but their **metabolites cause a devastating high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis with end-organ injury.** The antidote works by **blocking that metabolism**, so it must be given early, on suspicion.

## 🧬 Mechanism — it's the metabolites

Both are metabolised by **alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH)**:
- **Methanol → formaldehyde → formic acid** → **retinal toxicity (blindness), basal ganglia injury (putaminal necrosis/parkinsonism)**, and acidosis.
- **Ethylene glycol → glycolic acid (acidosis) → oxalic acid → calcium oxalate crystals → AKI** and hypocalcaemia (tetany).
Because toxicity is metabolite-driven, there is an early **latent period** (parent alcohol only → raised **osmolar gap**, minimal acidosis) followed by a late toxic phase (metabolites → **high anion-gap acidosis**, falling osmolar gap). Ethanol competes for ADH (co-ingested ethanol can delay toxicity).

## 🩺 Presentation

Early: apparent inebriation without the smell of ethanol. Later (hours–day):
- **Methanol:** headache, **visual disturbance/blurring/blindness ("snowstorm"), dilated poorly-reactive pupils**, severe acidosis, coma.
- **Ethylene glycol:** intoxication → **cardiopulmonary (tachypnoea, shock)** → **renal (flank pain, AKI, oxalate crystalluria, hypocalcaemia/tetany)** phases.
Both cause tachypnoea (Kussmaul), altered mental status, and shock in severe poisoning.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Anion gap** (high, from the acid metabolites) and **osmolar gap** (high early from the parent alcohol; **measured − calculated osmolality**); a normal osmolar gap late does not exclude toxicity.
- ABG/VBG, electrolytes, **calcium** (EG), renal function, glucose, lactate (glycolate can falsely raise some lactate assays), **ethanol level** (co-ingested), and **paracetamol/salicylate**.
- **Urine:** calcium oxalate crystals (EG); Wood's-lamp fluorescence of antifreeze is unreliable. Specific methanol/ethylene glycol levels where available (often send-out).

## 💊 Management

**1. Block alcohol dehydrogenase — the antidote — on suspicion.**
- **Fomepizole (preferred): 15 mg/kg IV loading, then 10 mg/kg IV every 12 h × 4 doses, then 15 mg/kg q12h** (auto-induction after 48 h); **continue until the toxic-alcohol level < 20 mg/dL and acidosis resolves.** Easy, safe, no monitoring of levels/sedation.
- **Ethanol** is the alternative where fomepizole is unavailable (target blood ethanol ~100–150 mg/dL) — cheaper but needs infusion, level monitoring, and causes intoxication/hypoglycaemia.
- **Indications to start:** documented ingestion with osmolar gap, or **high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis + raised osmolar gap**, or a strong history + acidosis.

**2. Haemodialysis — removes the toxin and corrects acidosis.** Indications: **severe metabolic acidosis, end-organ injury (visual symptoms/methanol; AKI/ethylene glycol), very high levels, or clinical deterioration** despite ADH blockade. Dialysis dramatically shortens the toxin half-life; **continue fomepizole during and adjust dosing around dialysis.**

**3. Cofactor/adjunct therapy + supportive.** **Sodium bicarbonate** for severe acidaemia (also enhances excretion/reduces tissue penetration). Cofactors to divert metabolism to non-toxic products: **folinic/folic acid for methanol; thiamine and pyridoxine for ethylene glycol.** Treat **hypocalcaemia** in EG only if symptomatic (calcium can worsen oxalate deposition). Ophthalmology for methanol visual toxicity.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

**Outbreaks of methanol poisoning from illicit/adulterated liquor ("เหล้าเถื่อน") occur in Thailand** — cluster presentations of acidosis + visual loss should raise immediate suspicion and prompt poison-centre notification (**Ramathibodi Poison Center 1367**). Ethanol infusion, sodium bicarbonate, folinic acid, thiamine/pyridoxine, and haemodialysis are available; **fomepizole availability is limited** in some Thai hospitals, so an **ethanol-drip protocol** is a practical alternative. Send levels to a reference lab; treat on the gap + clinical picture meanwhile.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Treat on suspicion — don't wait for confirmatory levels** (metabolites cause the damage; early ADH blockade prevents it).
> - **High-anion-gap metabolic acidosis + raised osmolar gap + inebriation without ethanol smell** = toxic alcohol.
> - **Methanol → blindness; ethylene glycol → AKI/hypocalcaemia** — end-organ signs mandate dialysis.
> - **Cluster of acidosis + visual loss = methanol outbreak** (illicit liquor) → notify poison centre/public health.
> - Give **cofactors** (folinic acid; thiamine/pyridoxine) and bicarbonate; a normal osmolar gap late does not exclude it.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Toxicity is **metabolite-driven** (ADH): methanol → formic acid (blindness); EG → oxalate (AKI, hypocalcaemia).
> - **High anion gap + high osmolar gap** (early gap high/acidosis low → reverses late).
> - **Fomepizole 15 mg/kg load then 10 mg/kg q12h** (or ethanol drip) — until level < 20 + acidosis resolved.
> - **Haemodialysis** for severe acidosis, end-organ injury, high levels, or deterioration.
> - **Cofactors**: folinic acid (methanol), thiamine + pyridoxine (EG); bicarbonate; think **methanol outbreak** (illicit liquor).

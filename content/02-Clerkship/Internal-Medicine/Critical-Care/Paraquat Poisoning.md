# Paraquat Poisoning

A bipyridyl herbicide and one of the most lethal common poisonings in agricultural Thailand — there is **no antidote**, and even small ingestions can be fatal through progressive **pulmonary fibrosis** and multi-organ failure. The counter-intuitive, life-defining rule: **do NOT give supplemental oxygen** unless truly hypoxic, because paraquat kills through oxygen free radicals and oxygen accelerates the lung injury.

## 🧬 Mechanism

Paraquat is actively concentrated in **type I/II alveolar pneumocytes** and undergoes redox cycling → generation of **superoxide and other reactive oxygen species** → lipid peroxidation and cell death, followed by an aggressive **proliferative pulmonary fibrosis** over days to weeks. It also causes caustic mucosal injury (mouth/oesophagus/GI), **acute tubular necrosis (AKI)**, hepatic necrosis, and cardiovascular collapse in large ingestions. Because oxygen fuels the redox cycling, **hyperoxia worsens the lung damage.**

## 🩺 Clinical course (dose-dependent)

| Ingestion | Course |
|---|---|
| **Mild (< ~20 mg/kg)** | GI symptoms, usually recover |
| **Moderate–severe (~20–40 mg/kg)** | oral/GI burns, AKI, hepatic injury, then **progressive pulmonary fibrosis → death over days–weeks** |
| **Fulminant (> ~40 mg/kg)** | rapid multi-organ failure, caustic injury, cardiovascular collapse, death in hours–days |

Early: painful **oral/pharyngeal ulceration and dysphagia** ("paraquat tongue"), vomiting, abdominal pain. Later: dyspnoea and refractory hypoxaemia from fibrosis (the usual cause of death), with AKI and hepatic failure.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Urine dithionite test** (qualitative — blue/green colour indicates paraquat; darker = higher concentration) and **plasma paraquat level** where available (prognostic nomograms exist relating level + time to survival).
- U&E/creatinine (AKI — a key prognostic marker), LFTs, venous gas/lactate, CBC, glucose; serial renal function.
- CXR (early normal → later diffuse infiltrates/fibrosis); avoid unnecessary high-FiO₂ during any procedure.

## 💊 Management (supportive — there is no antidote; consult the poison centre early)

**1. Limit absorption — early and aggressive.** **Activated charcoal or Fuller's earth (or bentonite)** as soon as possible after ingestion (with airway protection); gastric lavage only very early and cautiously (caustic risk). Do NOT induce vomiting.

**2. AVOID supplemental oxygen.** **Give oxygen only if SpO₂ is genuinely low (e.g. < 90%) and titrate to the minimum** — routine/high-flow oxygen accelerates oxygen-radical lung injury. This is a defining, often-forgotten point.

**3. Enhance elimination.** **Early haemoperfusion (ideally within 4–6 h of ingestion), repeated,** can adsorb paraquat and may improve survival in some series; CRRT supports the kidney and adds sustained removal. Benefit is greatest when started early and combined.

**4. Immunosuppression — uncertain, sometimes used.** Regimens of **cyclophosphamide + high-dose methylprednisolone (± dexamethasone)** are used in some centres to blunt the inflammatory/fibrotic phase, but the **evidence is weak/hypothesis-generating** — discuss with toxicology.

**5. Supportive care + counselling.** IV fluids for volume/AKI (forced diuresis is not proven), analgesia for caustic pain, nutrition, organ support, and **early honest prognostication** — the mortality is high. **Psychiatric assessment** (usually deliberate self-poisoning). Contact the **Ramathibodi Poison Center (1367)**.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

Paraquat has been a leading cause of fatal self-poisoning in rural Thailand; although Thailand moved to **ban paraquat (from 2020)**, residual stocks and illicit supply mean it is still encountered in Thai EDs. There is **no antidote** — the deliverables are early decontamination, avoiding oxygen, early haemoperfusion where available, and honest counselling, all with **poison-centre (1367)** guidance. Distinguish from the cholinergic picture of [[Organophosphate Poisoning|organophosphates]] (a different agricultural poison with a real antidote).

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **NO antidote** — do not delay decontamination waiting for one.
> - **AVOID supplemental oxygen** unless SpO₂ < ~90% (oxygen accelerates fatal pulmonary fibrosis) — titrate to the minimum.
> - **Decontaminate early** (activated charcoal/Fuller's earth); **early haemoperfusion (< 4–6 h)** where available.
> - **The ingested dose/plasma level + renal function predict survival** — prognosticate early and honestly.
> - Immunosuppression is **unproven** — a toxicology decision, not a reflex.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Paraquat = **oxygen-radical lung killer, no antidote, mortality 60–90%**; death from **progressive pulmonary fibrosis**.
> - **Do NOT give oxygen** unless hypoxic (< 90%) — hyperoxia worsens it.
> - **Early charcoal/Fuller's earth** + **early repeated haemoperfusion (< 4–6 h)**; CRRT for AKI.
> - Urine **dithionite test** (blue/green) + plasma level/nomogram for prognosis; AKI marks severity.
> - Immunosuppression (cyclophosphamide + methylprednisolone) uncertain; **poison centre 1367**; psychiatric review.

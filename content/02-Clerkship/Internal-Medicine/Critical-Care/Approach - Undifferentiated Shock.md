# Approach — Undifferentiated Shock

Shock is **acute circulatory failure causing inadequate tissue oxygen delivery** — a clinical emergency defined by **hypoperfusion, not by a blood-pressure number** (shock can exist with a normal BP; hypotension is a late sign). This hub gives the bedside approach: **recognise it, classify the mechanism fast (exam + POCUS + lactate), and treat the cause** while resuscitating, routing to the disease-specific pages.

## 🩺 Recognise shock (hypoperfusion, not just ↓BP)

Signs of end-organ hypoperfusion: **altered mentation, cool/mottled skin with prolonged capillary refill (or warm/flushed in early distributive), oliguria (< 0.5 mL/kg/h), tachycardia, tachypnoea**, and a **raised lactate/metabolic acidosis.** MAP < 65 mmHg or SBP < 90 supports it but is neither necessary nor sufficient — the young compensate then crash. **Shock index (HR/SBP) > 0.9** is an early clue.

## 🧬 Classify the mechanism (the key step)

| Type | Mechanism | POCUS / clues | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distributive** | vasodilation, ↓SVR | warm early, hyperdynamic LV, collapsible IVC | **[[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)\|sepsis]]** (commonest), **[[Anaphylaxis\|anaphylaxis]]**, neurogenic, [[Adrenal Crisis\|adrenal crisis]], liver failure |
| **Hypovolaemic** | ↓preload from volume loss | small hyperdynamic LV, collapsing IVC | haemorrhage ([[UGIB (Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding)\|GI bleed]], trauma, [[Ectopic Pregnancy\|ectopic]], [[Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)\|PPH]]), dehydration/GI losses, burns |
| **Cardiogenic** | pump failure | poor LV function, B-lines, plethoric IVC | MI ([[STEMI]]), arrhythmia, [[ADHF]], valve/myocarditis |
| **Obstructive** | mechanical obstruction to flow | specific POCUS signs | [[Cardiac Tamponade\|tamponade]] (effusion + RV collapse), tension [[Pneumothorax]] (absent lung sliding), massive [[VTE (DVT & Pulmonary Embolism)\|PE]] (RV strain/D-sign) |

A rapid **exam + point-of-care ultrasound (RUSH/heart-lungs-IVC-aorta) + lactate** stratifies most patients within minutes; mixed pictures are common (e.g. septic + cardiogenic).

## 🩻 Investigations

**Lactate** (severity + trend/clearance), ABG/VBG, **POCUS** (cardiac function, IVC, lung B-lines/sliding, free fluid/aorta), ECG, CBC, U&E, glucose, coagulation, **cultures/septic screen**, troponin, β-hCG (women), and cause-directed tests (imaging for bleeding/PE/aorta). Assess **fluid responsiveness** (passive leg raise, IVC/stroke-volume variation — see [[Fluid Responsiveness]]) rather than giving fixed boluses.

## 💊 Management — resuscitate while treating the mechanism

**1. Immediate.** ABCs, high-flow oxygen, large-bore IV access, monitoring, and treat reversible arrhythmias. Send lactate and cultures early.

**2. Mechanism-directed resuscitation.**
- **Distributive/hypovolaemic → fluids first** (balanced crystalloid, e.g. ~30 mL/kg in sepsis, guided by fluid responsiveness — avoid over-resuscitation), then **vasopressors (noradrenaline first-line)** for persistent hypotension (see [[Inotrope & Vasopressor]]); **anaphylaxis → IM adrenaline**; **haemorrhage → stop the bleeding + blood/massive transfusion + TXA** (permissive hypotension in trauma).
- **Cardiogenic → cautious/limited fluids, inotropes/vasopressors, treat the cause** (revascularise MI, rate/rhythm control), and consider [[MCS (Mechanical Circulatory Support)|mechanical support]].
- **Obstructive → relieve the obstruction:** **pericardiocentesis** (tamponade), **needle/finger decompression + chest drain** (tension pneumothorax), **reperfusion/thrombolysis** (massive PE).
- **Adrenal crisis → hydrocortisone** if suspected (pressor-refractory shock + suggestive features).

**3. Targets + reassess.** MAP ≥ 65 (individualise), improving mentation/urine output, and **lactate clearance**; reassess fluid responsiveness before further boluses; escalate to ICU. **Refractory vasodilatory shock → add vasopressin ± steroid** (see [[Sepsis (Sepsis-3)]]).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Crystalloids, noradrenaline/vasopressors, blood products, POCUS, and ICU care are available; sepsis (incl. melioidosis, [[Dengue|dengue]], leptospirosis) and haemorrhage are common Thai shock aetiologies. Lactate and POCUS-guided, mechanism-directed resuscitation is the practical framework; the **Thai sepsis CPG 2026** is in the reference library.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Shock is hypoperfusion, not a BP number** — treat before hypotension is severe (the young crash late).
> - **Classify the mechanism (exam + POCUS + lactate)** — the treatments are opposite (fluids help distributive/hypovolaemic but harm cardiogenic).
> - **Obstructive shock needs decompression, not fluids** (tamponade → pericardiocentesis; tension pneumothorax → decompress; massive PE → reperfuse).
> - **Assess fluid responsiveness** — avoid reflexive repeated boluses (over-resuscitation harms).
> - Think **adrenal crisis** in pressor-refractory shock; **anaphylaxis → IM adrenaline**; **haemorrhage → blood + source control**.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Shock = tissue hypoperfusion (mentation, cap refill, urine output, **lactate**) — not defined by BP.
> - Four types: **distributive · hypovolaemic · cardiogenic · obstructive** — classify with **exam + POCUS + lactate**.
> - Fluids (responsiveness-guided) + **noradrenaline** for distributive/hypovolaemic; **decompress** obstructive; **inotrope/limited fluid** for cardiogenic.
> - Targets: **MAP ≥ 65, urine output, lactate clearance**; reassess before more fluid.
> - Don't miss: tamponade, tension pneumothorax, massive PE, adrenal crisis, anaphylaxis, occult haemorrhage.

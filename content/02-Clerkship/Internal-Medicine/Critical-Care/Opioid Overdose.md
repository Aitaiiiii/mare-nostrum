# Opioid Overdose

A reversible cause of coma and respiratory arrest, and — with the antidote — one of the most satisfying ER saves. The killer is **respiratory depression → hypoxaemia**, so the priorities are **oxygenation/ventilation and titrated naloxone**, aiming to restore adequate breathing without precipitating florid withdrawal.

## 🧬 Mechanism & the toxidrome

Opioids agonise μ-receptors → CNS and respiratory-centre depression and pupillary constriction. The **classic toxidrome: depressed consciousness + depressed respiration (low rate/tidal volume) + miosis (pinpoint pupils).** Death is from hypoventilation/apnoea and aspiration. Caveats: **miosis may be absent** with meperidine, tramadol, or co-ingestants; **some opioids cause specific effects** — tramadol/meperidine → **seizures/serotonin syndrome**, methadone → **QT prolongation/torsades**, and non-cardiogenic **pulmonary oedema** can follow overdose or rapid reversal.

## 🩺 Presentation & investigations

Presentation ranges from drowsy to apnoeic/arrested. Assess RR, SpO₂, GCS, pupils, and for aspiration/pulmonary oedema. **Diagnosis is clinical (response to naloxone is confirmatory).** Check **capillary glucose** (exclude hypoglycaemia), ECG (**QTc — methadone**; QRS if co-ingestant), venous gas (CO₂), paracetamol/salicylate levels and a coingestant screen (deliberate overdose), and consider the cause of a "found down" (trauma, rhabdomyolysis).

## 💊 Management

**1. Airway, breathing, oxygenation FIRST.** Support ventilation (bag-valve-mask/O₂) — **restoring oxygenation is what prevents death**, and pre-oxygenating before naloxone reduces the agitation/complications of reversal.

**2. Naloxone — titrate to breathing, not to full arousal.**
- **Start 0.04–0.4 mg IV** (lower in chronic users/palliative patients to avoid precipitated withdrawal), and **repeat/double every 2–3 minutes** until adequate spontaneous ventilation. Can be given IM/intranasal if no IV access. Larger initial doses (e.g. 2 mg) for peri-arrest.
- **Endpoint = adequate respiratory rate/effort and oxygenation**, not a fully awake, combative patient.
- **Recurrence & infusion:** naloxone's duration (~30–90 min) is shorter than many opioids, so **re-sedation is expected** with long-acting agents (methadone, MR preparations) — start a **continuous infusion at ~two-thirds of the effective bolus dose per hour**, titrated, when repeated boluses are needed.

**3. Observation & disposition.** Observe; **admit any patient needing a second dose or who fails a ~4–6 hour observation** after the last naloxone; a longer-acting or slow-release ingestion needs prolonged monitoring/ICU. Manage **non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema** supportively; watch for withdrawal.

**4. Coingestant/specific issues.** Benzodiazepine co-ingestion (respiratory depression), **methadone → torsades** (magnesium, avoid QT drugs), tramadol/meperidine → seizures ([[Seizure & Epilepsy|benzodiazepines]], not naloxone alone). Deliberate self-poisoning → psychiatric assessment; harm-reduction and take-home naloxone counselling.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Naloxone is available in Thai EDs; take-home naloxone/harm-reduction programmes are expanding. Thai-relevant contexts: **tramadol** misuse (seizure/serotonin risk), **methadone** maintenance (long duration + QT), and mixed sedative overdoses. **Kratom** (mitragyna) has opioid-like effects and is used in Thailand; high-dose or adulterated ("4×100") preparations can cause sedation/seizures.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Oxygenate/ventilate first** — hypoxaemia kills; naloxone is an adjunct to airway support.
> - **Titrate naloxone to breathing, not to arousal** — over-reversal precipitates withdrawal, agitation, and pulmonary oedema.
> - **Long-acting opioids (methadone, MR) re-sedate** → infusion + prolonged observation/ICU; don't discharge early.
> - **Methadone → QT/torsades**; **tramadol/meperidine → seizures/serotonin syndrome** (treat with benzodiazepines).
> - Always check **glucose** and screen for co-ingestants (paracetamol) in deliberate overdose.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Toxidrome: **↓consciousness + ↓respiration + miosis** (miosis may be absent — tramadol/meperidine).
> - **Naloxone 0.04–0.4 mg IV, double q2–3 min to adequate breathing**; low start in chronic users.
> - **Infusion ~⅔ of effective bolus/hr** for long-acting opioids; **observe 4–6 h**, admit if second dose needed.
> - **Methadone → torsades; tramadol/meperidine → seizures**; naloxone doesn't fix opioid seizures.
> - Ventilate first; psychiatric review for intentional overdose; take-home naloxone.

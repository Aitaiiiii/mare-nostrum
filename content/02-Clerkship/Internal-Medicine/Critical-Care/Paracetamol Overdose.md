# Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) Overdose

The most common drug taken in deliberate self-poisoning and a leading cause of acute liver failure. It is eminently treatable: **N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is nearly 100% hepatoprotective when started within 8 hours**, and the whole approach is a clean algorithm — measure a timed level, plot it on the nomogram, and treat above the line (or empirically when timing is uncertain).

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

Most paracetamol is conjugated (glucuronide/sulfate); a small fraction is oxidised by CYP2E1 to the toxic metabolite **NAPQI**, normally detoxified by **glutathione**. In overdose, conjugation saturates, NAPQI accumulates, glutathione is depleted, and NAPQI binds hepatocyte proteins → **centrilobular (zone 3) hepatic necrosis** (and acute tubular necrosis). **NAC works by replenishing glutathione** and providing sulfhydryl substrate. Risk is higher with chronic alcohol/enzyme inducers and malnutrition (low glutathione).

## 🩺 Clinical course (four phases)

| Phase | Time | Features |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–24 h | asymptomatic or nausea/vomiting/malaise — **liver tests still normal** (do not be falsely reassured) |
| 2 | 24–72 h | RUQ pain, rising **AST/ALT**, INR; onset of hepatic injury |
| 3 | 72–96 h | peak hepatotoxicity — **fulminant hepatic failure**, encephalopathy, coagulopathy, AKI, lactic acidosis, hypoglycaemia; deaths here |
| 4 | 4 d–2 wk | recovery (if survived) or progression |

**A toxic ingestion is typically ≥ 150 mg/kg (or ≥ ~7.5–10 g) acutely**; **staggered** ingestions and delayed presentation carry higher risk and are not reliably interpreted by the nomogram.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Serum paracetamol level at 4 hours post-ingestion (or on arrival if later); do not measure before 4 h.** Plot on the **Rumack–Matthew nomogram** (treatment line: **150 µg/mL at 4 h**, declining to ~4.7 at 24 h).
- **Baseline and serial: AST/ALT, INR/PT, bilirubin, U&E/creatinine, venous gas/lactate, glucose**, and a coingestant screen (salicylate, etc.).
- **The nomogram applies only to a single acute ingestion with a known time.** For **staggered ingestion, unknown time, or presentation > 8 h**, treat and use levels + LFTs/INR to guide.

## 💊 Management

**1. Decontamination.** **Activated charcoal** if presenting within ~1–2 h of a significant ingestion and the airway is protected.

**2. N-acetylcysteine (NAC) — the antidote; start it on the right trigger.**
- **Start NAC if:** the timed level is **above the nomogram treatment line**; the patient presents **> 8 h after a significant ingestion** (start empirically while awaiting the level — do not wait); **staggered/unknown-time** ingestion with a significant dose; or any evidence of hepatotoxicity (raised ALT/INR).
- **NAC is ~100% hepatoprotective if started within 8 hours** of ingestion; efficacy falls with delay but **NAC still benefits late presenters and established liver failure** — give it.
- **Regimen — IV 21-hour (three-bag):** **150 mg/kg over 1 h → 50 mg/kg over 4 h → 100 mg/kg over 16 h** (total 300 mg/kg over 21 h); newer two-bag regimens reduce reactions. **At the end, re-check ALT/INR/level and CONTINUE NAC** (do not stop automatically at 21 h) if ALT still rising, INR elevated, or paracetamol still detectable. Watch for an **anaphylactoid reaction** (slow/pause the infusion, antihistamine).

**3. Monitor for and manage fulminant hepatic failure.** Trend INR, ALT, lactate, glucose, creatinine, and pH; treat hypoglycaemia and coagulopathy; **refer early to a liver-transplant centre using the King's College criteria** (arterial **pH < 7.3** after resuscitation; OR the triad **INR > 6.5 [PT > 100 s] + creatinine > 3.4 mg/dL + grade III–IV encephalopathy**; lactate and phosphate are adjuncts) — see [[Acute Liver Failure]].

**4. Psychosocial.** Deliberate self-poisoning → **psychiatric/risk assessment** before discharge; manage co-ingestants.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability & context

Paracetamol and **N-acetylcysteine (IV and oral)** are on the Thai NLEM and stocked in Thai EDs; the **Ramathibodi Poison Center (1367)** advises on nomogram interpretation and NAC. Paracetamol is extremely accessible OTC in Thailand, making overdose common; be alert to **combination products** (paracetamol + tramadol/antihistamine) and to traditional/alternative coingestants.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Normal LFTs early do NOT exclude toxicity** — treat by level/nomogram and time, not by symptoms.
> - **Start NAC empirically if presenting > 8 h or timing is uncertain** — do not wait for the level; NAC works best < 8 h but helps even late.
> - **Do NOT stop NAC automatically at 21 h** — re-check ALT/INR/level and continue if not resolving.
> - **Staggered/unknown-time ingestions are NOT interpretable on the nomogram** — treat and follow LFT/INR.
> - **King's College criteria** → urgent transplant-centre referral for fulminant failure.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Toxic dose ~**≥ 150 mg/kg**; toxicity via **NAPQI**, glutathione depletion → zone-3 necrosis; **NAC replenishes glutathione**.
> - **4-hour level on Rumack–Matthew nomogram (line 150 µg/mL at 4 h)** → treat above the line; **NAC ~100% protective < 8 h**.
> - **NAC IV 21-h: 150 → 50 → 100 mg/kg**; **continue if ALT/INR rising or level detectable** (don't auto-stop).
> - **Empiric NAC** for > 8 h / staggered / unknown-time / any hepatotoxicity; activated charcoal < 1–2 h.
> - **King's College criteria** (pH < 7.3, or INR > 6.5 + Cr > 3.4 + grade III–IV encephalopathy) → transplant centre.

# Accidental Hypothermia

A core temperature **< 35 °C** from environmental exposure, immersion, or impaired thermoregulation. Two ideas dominate: **rewarm according to severity**, and — because deep hypothermia is neuroprotective — **"no one is dead until they are warm and dead,"** so resuscitation is prolonged and cardiac arrest is treated with **extracorporeal rewarming** rather than early termination.

## 🧬 Staging & Pathophysiology

Cold slows metabolism and depresses every system → progressive CNS depression, bradycardia/arrhythmia, cold diuresis, and coagulopathy. Severity (Swiss staging / core temperature):
- **Mild (32–35 °C):** conscious, shivering, tachycardia then bradycardia.
- **Moderate (28–32 °C):** shivering stops, altered mentation, arrhythmia risk, **Osborn (J) waves** on ECG.
- **Severe (< 28 °C):** unconscious, profound bradycardia, high risk of **VF/asystole**; may mimic death (fixed pupils, undetectable pulse).

Predisposing: environmental exposure, immersion/**drowning**, alcohol/drugs, hypoglycaemia, hypothyroidism/[[Myxedema Coma]], sepsis, trauma, extremes of age.

## 🩺 Presentation & investigations

Measure a **low-reading core (rectal/oesophageal) temperature.** ECG (**Osborn waves**, bradyarrhythmia, prolonged intervals), glucose, electrolytes (**K⁺** — a markedly high K⁺ suggests death/hypoxia before cooling and predicts non-survival), coagulation, ABG, CK, and toxicology/thyroid/cause work-up. **Handle the patient gently** — rough movement can precipitate VF in moderate/severe hypothermia.

## 💊 Management — rewarm by stage + prolonged resuscitation

**1. General.** ABCs, remove wet clothing, insulate, warm environment; treat hypoglycaemia; **minimise stimulation/rough handling** (arrhythmia). Warm IV fluids.

**2. Rewarming by severity.**
- **Mild → passive external rewarming** (remove wet clothes, blankets, warm room) + active external (forced warm air) if needed; the patient rewarms by shivering.
- **Moderate → active external + minimally-invasive core rewarming** (warm humidified oxygen, warmed IV fluids, forced-air warming).
- **Severe → active internal/core rewarming** (warm fluids, body-cavity lavage) and, for cardiac instability/arrest, **extracorporeal.** Aim to rewarm ~1–2 °C/h (faster with extracorporeal). Watch for **rewarming "afterdrop"** (peripheral cold blood returning centrally → transient temperature fall/hypotension) and rewarming acidosis/hyperkalaemia.

**3. Hypothermic cardiac arrest — "not dead until warm and dead".**
- **Continue CPR** (prolonged); **ECLS/ECMO (or cardiopulmonary bypass) is the first-line rewarming strategy** for a hypothermic arrest and gives excellent neurological outcomes — transfer to an ECLS centre.
- The **arrhythmic heart may not respond to defibrillation/drugs until warmer** — guidance commonly **withholds/limits defibrillation attempts and ACLS drugs until core > ~30 °C**, then follows standard [[CPR - ACLS|ACLS]] with warming.
- **Do not declare death until the patient is rewarmed** (unless obvious lethal injury, or a markedly elevated potassium indicating pre-arrest death); prognostic serum K⁺ helps guide futility.

**4. Treat the cause + complications** — cover concurrent hypoglycaemia, hypothyroidism, sepsis, trauma, and drowning; monitor for coagulopathy, rhabdomyolysis, AKI, and pancreatitis.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Forced-air warmers, warmed fluids, and (at tertiary centres) ECLS/ECMO are available; hypothermia is less common in tropical Thailand but occurs with **cold-season exposure (northern highlands), immersion/drowning, the elderly/intoxicated, and secondary to sepsis/hypothyroidism/hypoglycaemia** — a low-reading thermometer and thinking of it are the key. Transfer hypothermic arrest to an ECLS-capable centre.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **"Not dead until warm and dead"** — continue prolonged CPR; **rewarm before declaring death** (unless lethal injury / very high K⁺).
> - **Handle gently** — rough movement can precipitate VF in moderate/severe hypothermia.
> - **Hypothermic arrest → ECLS/ECMO first-line rewarming**; limit defibrillation/ACLS drugs until core > ~30 °C.
> - Watch **rewarming afterdrop, acidosis, hyperkalaemia**; a markedly high K⁺ predicts non-survival.
> - Treat the cause (hypoglycaemia, hypothyroidism, sepsis, drowning, intoxication).

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Core **< 35 °C**; stages mild/moderate/severe; **Osborn (J) waves**; measure a low-reading core temp.
> - Rewarm by stage: **passive → active external → active internal/ECLS**; ~1–2 °C/h; beware **afterdrop**.
> - **Hypothermic arrest: prolonged CPR + ECMO rewarming**; hold defib/drugs until > ~30 °C; don't declare death until warm.
> - **Gentle handling** (VF risk); warm fluids; treat cause.
> - Very high K⁺ = prognostic marker of futility.

# Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Overdose

One of the deadliest common deliberate overdoses, because a modest ingestion can cause **cardiac sodium-channel blockade and refractory arrhythmia within hours.** The management pivots on the ECG: **a widening QRS is both the warning and the treatment target for sodium bicarbonate.**

## 🧬 Mechanism — four toxic effects

1. **Fast sodium-channel blockade** (myocardium) → **QRS widening, R wave in aVR, arrhythmia (VT), and myocardial depression** — the lethal effect.
2. **Anticholinergic** → tachycardia, dry/flushed, mydriasis, urinary retention, delirium, ileus, hyperthermia.
3. **CNS** → **seizures** and coma (rapid onset).
4. **α-adrenergic blockade + Na-channel myocardial depression** → **hypotension.**
Deterioration can be precipitous — a talking patient can arrest.

## 🩺 Presentation & the ECG

Anticholinergic toxidrome + drowsiness/coma + seizures; hypotension. **The ECG is the key tool:** **QRS > 100 ms predicts seizures; QRS > 160 ms predicts ventricular arrhythmia**; a **terminal R wave in aVR (> 3 mm)** and a large R:S in aVR support the diagnosis. Continuous cardiac monitoring is mandatory.

## 🩻 Investigations

**ECG (serial), continuous monitoring**, venous/arterial gas (acidosis worsens toxicity), electrolytes (**K⁺** — bicarbonate lowers it), glucose, **paracetamol + salicylate levels** and coingestant screen, and pregnancy test. TCA drug levels do not guide acute management.

## 💊 Management

**1. Sodium bicarbonate — the antidote, guided by QRS.** For **QRS > 100 ms, ventricular arrhythmia, or hypotension**: give **sodium bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg IV boluses, repeated**, until the QRS narrows/haemodynamics improve; then consider an infusion. **Targets: serum pH ~7.5–7.55 and serum sodium ~150–155 mmol/L.** It works by overcoming the sodium-channel block (extra Na⁺) and by alkalinisation (reduces drug binding). Hyperventilate the intubated patient toward the same alkalotic pH.

**2. Airway & seizures.** Low threshold to **intubate** (coma, seizures, deteriorating). **Seizures → benzodiazepines** (then barbiturate/propofol if refractory); **AVOID phenytoin** (worsens sodium-channel blockade). Treat hyperthermia.

**3. Hypotension/arrhythmia.** IV crystalloid + sodium bicarbonate first; **persistent hypotension → direct-acting vasopressor (noradrenaline)**; for **refractory ventricular arrhythmia** after bicarbonate, **lidocaine** (a fast-off sodium-channel blocker) is the antiarrhythmic of choice — **avoid class Ia/Ic and III agents** and avoid flumazenil (seizure risk).

**4. Refractory toxicity — lipid emulsion / ECMO.** **20% intravenous lipid emulsion** for cardiovascular collapse/refractory arrhythmia unresponsive to bicarbonate; consider **VA-ECMO** for refractory cardiogenic shock/arrest (good neurological recovery reported given the toxin clears). Activated charcoal if early and airway protected.

**5. Disposition.** Asymptomatic with a normal ECG and normal mentation after ~6 h of observation can be medically cleared; any toxicity → monitored/ICU admission. Psychiatric assessment (deliberate overdose).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Sodium bicarbonate, benzodiazepines, noradrenaline, lidocaine, and 20% lipid emulsion are available in Thai EDs/ICUs. Amitriptyline is widely prescribed in Thailand (depression, neuropathic pain, migraine prophylaxis) and is a common deliberate-overdose agent — recognise the wide-QRS pattern early and give bicarbonate promptly.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **QRS > 100 ms → give sodium bicarbonate now** (and it predicts seizures; > 160 ms predicts VT).
> - **Deterioration is rapid** — a stable-looking patient can seize/arrest; monitor continuously, low threshold to intubate.
> - **AVOID phenytoin** (seizures) and **class Ia/Ic/III antiarrhythmics** (worsen Na-channel block); avoid flumazenil.
> - **Refractory instability → lipid emulsion ± ECMO** (patients can recover fully as the drug clears).
> - Always check **paracetamol/salicylate** in deliberate overdose.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Four effects: **Na-channel block (wide QRS/VT/hypotension) + anticholinergic + seizures + α-block hypotension**.
> - **ECG: QRS > 100 ms (seizure risk), > 160 ms (VT), terminal R in aVR.**
> - **Sodium bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg boluses to QRS narrowing** (target pH 7.5–7.55, Na 150–155).
> - **Seizures → benzodiazepines (NOT phenytoin)**; hypotension → bicarb + noradrenaline; VT → **lidocaine**.
> - **Refractory → 20% lipid emulsion ± ECMO**; observe 6 h if asymptomatic + normal ECG.

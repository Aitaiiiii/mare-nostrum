# SCAPE (Sympathetic Crashing Acute Pulmonary Edema)

The most dramatic, rapidly-reversible form of acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema: a **hypertensive, hyperadrenergic "flash" pulmonary oedema** where the patient is crashing with severe respiratory distress and a very high blood pressure. The key insight is that this is a problem of **afterload and fluid redistribution, not total-body fluid overload** — so the treatment is **NIV + high-dose vasodilator (nitrate)**, not aggressive diuresis. It is a subset/severe presentation of [[ADHF]].

## 🧬 Pathophysiology — why it's different from ordinary ADHF

A catecholamine surge → **acute rise in afterload (severe hypertension) + venoconstriction shifting blood centrally** → the left ventricle can't eject against the high afterload → acute rise in LV filling pressure → **rapid transudation of fluid into the alveoli (redistribution, over minutes)**, often in a **euvolaemic or only mildly overloaded** patient. This is distinct from the gradual salt-and-water accumulation of decompensated chronic HF — hence **diuretics (which target total-body volume) are secondary**, and **reducing afterload** reverses it fast.

## 🩺 Presentation

**Abrupt, severe respiratory distress** — the patient is upright, gasping, diaphoretic, unable to speak, with **markedly elevated BP (often SBP > 180–200)**, tachycardia, widespread crackles/wheeze, and hypoxaemia; frothy (± pink) sputum. It evolves over minutes. Contrast with the more gradual orthopnoea/oedema/weight gain of chronic decompensation.

## 🩻 Investigations (do not delay treatment)

Continuous SpO₂/ECG/BP; **ECG** (ischaemia/STEMI as a trigger), **troponin**, CXR (bilateral alveolar oedema), venous/arterial gas, U&E, and **BNP/NT-proBNP** if the diagnosis is uncertain. **POCUS** (diffuse B-lines, LV function, IVC) is very useful at the bedside. Look for the precipitant: **ischaemia/ACS, uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia, renovascular disease, non-adherence, or acute valvular problem.**

## 💊 Management — vasodilate + ventilate first

**1. Non-invasive ventilation (NIV/CPAP) immediately.** CPAP/BiPAP reduces the work of breathing, improves oxygenation, reduces preload and afterload, and **reduces the need for intubation and mortality** in acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema — apply it early alongside high-flow oxygen. Intubate if NIV fails/exhaustion/↓consciousness.

**2. High-dose IV nitrate — the primary drug (afterload/preload reduction).** **IV nitroglycerin (GTN)** is the workhorse: rapidly up-titrated, often at **high doses** in SCAPE (much higher than routine HF infusions — e.g. large boluses/rapidly escalating infusions under monitoring) to drop the afterload and abort the crisis; **sublingual GTN** while access/infusion is set up. (Nitroprusside/other vasodilators are alternatives.) Titrate to symptoms/BP with continuous monitoring.

**3. Diuretics are SECONDARY, not first-line.** Because most SCAPE patients are not truly fluid-overloaded, **do not lead with large-dose IV furosemide** — give a diuretic once the acute crisis is controlled and if there is genuine volume overload. Over-diuresing a euvolaemic SCAPE patient causes later hypotension/AKI.

**4. Treat the trigger + avoid harm.** Treat **ischaemia/ACS** ([[STEMI]]/[[NSTE-ACS (NSTEMI & Unstable Angina)|NSTE-ACS]]), arrhythmia, and severe hypertension; **avoid fluid boluses** and rate-limiting/negative-inotrope drugs in the acute phase. Most SCAPE resolves rapidly with NIV + nitrates; persistent shock/low-output suggests cardiogenic shock (different pathway — inotropes/[[MCS (Mechanical Circulatory Support)|MCS]]).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

NIV/CPAP, IV and sublingual nitroglycerin, and monitored/ICU care are available at Thai hospitals. SCAPE is common in the setting of **uncontrolled hypertension and renovascular disease**; the deliverable is the mindset shift — **NIV + high-dose nitrate first, diuretics second** — which reverses the crisis quickly and avoids unnecessary intubation.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **NIV + high-dose IV nitrate first** — this is afterload/redistribution physiology, not total-body overload.
> - **Do NOT lead with large-dose diuretics** (most are euvolaemic → later hypotension/AKI); diuretics are secondary.
> - **Avoid fluid boluses** and negative inotropes in the acute phase.
> - **Find and treat the trigger** — especially **ACS/ischaemia** and severe hypertension.
> - Failing NIV / exhaustion / ↓consciousness → intubate; persistent low output → think cardiogenic shock.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - SCAPE = **hyperadrenergic hypertensive flash pulmonary oedema** (redistribution, minutes) — a severe [[ADHF]] subset.
> - **NIV/CPAP early + high-dose IV nitroglycerin** (afterload reduction) — the primary treatment.

# Myxedema Coma (Decompensated Hypothyroidism)

The extreme, decompensated end of hypothyroidism — a rare but **high-mortality (~25–60%) endocrine emergency** of the elderly, usually tipped over by a precipitant (often infection or cold) in winter. Despite the name, frank coma is not required; the defining picture is **hypothermia + depressed mental status + multi-organ slowing.** The two rules: **give thyroid hormone AND hydrocortisone (steroid before/with thyroid hormone), and hunt the precipitant.**

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

Profound thyroid-hormone deficiency slows every system: **hypothermia** (reduced thermogenesis), **CNS depression** (lethargy → coma, seizures), **hypoventilation** (CO₂ retention → narcosis), **bradycardia/low output/pericardial effusion**, **hyponatraemia** (impaired free-water excretion/SIADH-like), **hypoglycaemia**, ileus, and reduced drug metabolism. Coexisting adrenal insufficiency may be present (autoimmune polyendocrine or pituitary) — hence empiric steroid.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Usually an **elderly patient (often known/untreated hypothyroid, or recently stopped levothyroxine)** with **hypothermia, altered mental status (confusion → coma), and a precipitant.** Signs of hypothyroidism: dry/coarse skin, non-pitting oedema (myxoedema), macroglossia, hoarse voice, delayed-relaxing reflexes, bradycardia, hypotension. Complications: hypoventilation/respiratory failure, hyponatraemia, hypoglycaemia, seizures, and shock. **Precipitants:** infection (commonest), cold exposure, MI, stroke, trauma/surgery, GI bleed, and **sedatives/opioids/anaesthetics** (poorly metabolised).

## 🩻 Investigations

- **TFTs (low free T4, high TSH in primary; TSH may be low/normal in central hypothyroidism)** — start treatment on clinical suspicion, don't wait for results.
- **Random cortisol** (before steroid) to assess concurrent adrenal insufficiency.
- **Glucose, sodium (hyponatraemia), venous/arterial gas (CO₂ retention), CBC, renal/liver function, CK, ECG (bradycardia, low voltage), septic screen (culture, CXR, urine).**
- Consider precipitant work-up (troponin, imaging).

## 💊 Management (ICU; search the current protocol — doses vary)

**1. Thyroid hormone replacement — IV.** **Levothyroxine (T4): IV loading dose ~4 µg/kg (commonly 200–400 µg) IV, then ~50–100 µg IV daily.** Some add **liothyronine (T3)** (e.g. 5–20 µg IV then 2.5–10 µg q8h) because peripheral T4→T3 conversion is impaired in severe illness — often reserved for the sickest, balancing the arrhythmia/ischaemia risk of T3 (cautious in cardiac disease/elderly).

**2. Hydrocortisone — give BEFORE or WITH thyroid hormone.** **Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV then 50 mg IV q6–8h** (or 100 mg q8h), because coexisting adrenal insufficiency is common and **thyroid hormone can precipitate an adrenal crisis by increasing cortisol clearance/metabolism** if steroid is not covered. Draw cortisol first if possible.

**3. Supportive & the killers.**
- **Passive rewarming** (blankets); **avoid aggressive active external rewarming** (peripheral vasodilation → worsened hypotension/collapse).
- **Ventilatory support** for hypoventilation/CO₂ narcosis (a leading cause of death).
- **Hyponatraemia** — fluid restriction ± cautious hypertonic saline for severe/symptomatic (avoid over-rapid correction).
- **Hypoglycaemia** — IV dextrose. **Hypotension** — cautious fluids/pressors (may be steroid/thyroid-responsive).
- **Treat the precipitant** — **empiric antibiotics** for suspected infection (fever may be absent), and manage MI/stroke/GI bleed.
- **Avoid sedatives/opioids** (impaired metabolism).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

IV levothyroxine, hydrocortisone, and dextrose are available at Thai hospitals (IV liothyronine may be less readily available — oral/NG T3 or T4 is used where IV is not). The **Thai thyroid-disorder guidance (2568)** is in the reference library. Myxedema coma is uncommon and clusters in the elderly/untreated hypothyroid during intercurrent illness — a high index of suspicion (hypothermia + altered mentation + known hypothyroidism) is the key.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Give hydrocortisone BEFORE/with thyroid hormone** — thyroid hormone alone can precipitate adrenal crisis.
> - **Passive rewarming only** — aggressive active rewarming causes vasodilatory collapse.
> - **Hypoventilation/CO₂ narcosis is a leading killer** — support ventilation early; **avoid sedatives/opioids**.
> - **Always search for and treat the precipitant** (infection often, and fever may be absent — treat empirically).
> - Correct hyponatraemia and hypoglycaemia; don't over-correct Na⁺.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Hypothermia + altered mental status + hyponatraemia/hypoglycaemia/hypoventilation** in an elderly hypothyroid patient.
> - **IV levothyroxine load ~4 µg/kg (200–400 µg) then 50–100 µg/day (± T3)** AND **hydrocortisone 100 mg IV then 50 mg q6–8h (steroid first)**.
> - **Passive rewarming**, ventilatory support, treat hyponatraemia/hypoglycaemia, **empiric antibiotics** for the precipitant.
> - **Avoid sedatives/opioids** and aggressive active rewarming.
> - High mortality → ICU; treat on suspicion, don't wait for TFTs.

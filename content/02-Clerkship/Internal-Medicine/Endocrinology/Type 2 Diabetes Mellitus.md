# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)

This page covers **Type 2 diabetes** specifically — the insulin-resistant, adult-onset, non-autoimmune form. Type 1 diabetes, the monogenic and secondary forms, and the acute hyperglycemic emergencies each sit elsewhere: [[DKA (Diabetic Ketoacidosis)]] and [[HHS (Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State)]] for the crises, and [[Diabetes Complications]] for the microvascular/macrovascular end-organ disease. Guideline anchors here are the **ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026** and the Thai **แนวทางเวชปฏิบัติสำหรับโรคเบาหวาน พ.ศ. 2566** (RCPT / Diabetes Association of Thailand / Endocrine Society of Thailand).

## 🧬 Etiology & Pathophysiology

T2DM is the end result of two lesions that must coexist: **insulin resistance** in muscle, liver, and adipose tissue, and a **progressive secretory failure of the pancreatic β-cell**. Insulin resistance alone is compensated for years by hyperinsulinemia, so glucose stays normal; overt hyperglycemia appears only once β-cell mass and function decline enough that insulin output can no longer overcome the resistance. This is why T2DM is intrinsically progressive — the resistance is relatively fixed, but β-cell function falls by roughly 4% per year (UKPDS), so glycemic control drifts upward and therapy must be escalated even in a fully adherent patient. It also explains why β-cell mass at diagnosis already averages ~50% of normal, and why microvascular complications may be present the day the label is applied.

The upstream driver in most patients is central (visceral) adiposity. Inflamed, lipolytic adipose tissue releases free fatty acids and pro-inflammatory adipokines that impair insulin signalling in liver and muscle and disinhibit hepatic gluconeogenesis. The result is fasting hyperglycemia (unrestrained hepatic glucose output overnight) layered on post-prandial hyperglycemia (failed insulin-mediated peripheral glucose uptake). A1C therefore reflects both components, which is why controlling the fasting glucose alone often leaves A1C above target once disease is advanced.

DeFronzo's **ominous octet** frames the eight defects that current drug classes each target — worth holding as a list because each maps to a therapy:

- **Muscle** — reduced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake (insulin, TZD, metformin indirectly).
- **Liver** — increased gluconeogenesis (metformin, the foundational glucose-lowering agent).
- **β-cell** — deficient/declining insulin secretion (sulfonylureas, glinides, incretins).
- **α-cell** — inappropriate glucagon excess (GLP-1 RA, DPP-4i suppress it). 
- **Incretin effect** — deficient/resistant GLP-1 and GIP signalling (GLP-1 RA, dual GIP/GLP-1 RA, DPP-4i).
- **Kidney** — up-regulated SGLT2, so the glucose reabsorption threshold rises and glucosuria is suppressed (SGLT2i).
- **Adipose tissue** — accelerated lipolysis feeding hepatic gluconeogenesis (TZD, GLP-1 RA via weight loss).
- **Brain** — appetite dysregulation and central insulin resistance (GLP-1 RA, dual GIP/GLP-1 RA).

The practical consequence dominates the modern approach: glucose-lowering is no longer the only goal. Because the same insulin-resistant, inflammatory milieu drives atherosclerosis, heart failure, and diabetic kidney disease, the classes that interrupt those pathways — SGLT2i and GLP-1 RA — reduce cardiovascular and renal events independently of how much they lower A1C. That decoupling of organ benefit from glucose-lowering is exactly why ADA 2026 selects therapy by the patient's comorbidity profile first, and the glucose number second.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Symptoms**

Most T2DM is asymptomatic and found on screening; the disease is often present for 4–7 years before diagnosis, so retinopathy, neuropathy, or albuminuria may already be established at presentation. When symptomatic, patients report the osmotic triad — polyuria, polydipsia, and unexplained weight loss — with fatigue, blurred vision from osmotic lens changes, and recurrent infections (vulvovaginal or balanitic candidiasis, skin, urinary). At the extreme, marked hyperglycemia presents as a **hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state** (profound hyperglycemia and dehydration without significant ketosis — see [[HHS (Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State)]]); frank DKA is uncommon in T2DM but occurs with severe intercurrent illness, in ketosis-prone (often African/Asian) phenotypes, and as **euglycemic DKA** on an SGLT2 inhibitor (see [[DKA (Diabetic Ketoacidosis)]]).

> [!note] 💎 Pearls — presentation
> - **Ketosis-prone T2DM ("Flatbush diabetes")** — presents in DKA (often an obese person of African/Asian descent, antibody-negative) but recovers β-cell function and later comes off insulin; do not lock in a T1DM label from the first DKA.
> - **Malignant (necrotizing) otitis externa** — severe otalgia with granulation tissue at the bony-cartilaginous junction of the ear canal in an elderly diabetic; it is *Pseudomonas* skull-base osteomyelitis, an emergency, not ordinary otitis externa.
> - **Rhinocerebral mucormycosis** — a black necrotic eschar on the turbinate or palate in a poorly controlled or DKA patient; angioinvasive, needs emergent surgery plus amphotericin B.

**Physical examination**

The exam stages complications and gauges insulin resistance rather than making the diagnosis. The findings that change management are:

- **Acanthosis nigricans** — velvety hyperpigmentation of the neck and axillae, a marker of hyperinsulinemia/insulin resistance; skin tags accompany it.
- **Adiposity** — BMI and especially waist circumference (central fat predicts cardiometabolic risk better than BMI).
- **Feet** — inspect for ulcers/deformity/Charcot, then test protective sensation with the special tests below:
  - **10-g Semmes-Weinstein monofilament** — with the patient's eyes closed, press the filament perpendicular to the skin until it buckles (~1 s) at defined sites (plantar great toe, 3rd and 5th metatarsal heads ± more), avoiding callus/ulcer; ask "yes/no" at each touch. **Loss of protective sensation** = failure to feel ≥1 of the standard sites and predicts ulceration.
  - **128-Hz tuning fork** — strike and place on a bony prominence (dorsum of the great toe IP joint / medial malleolus); the patient reports when vibration starts and stops. Absent or asymmetric vibration = large-fibre neuropathy. (The **Ipswich touch test** — lightly touching the tips of the 1st/3rd/5th toes — is a validated bedside substitute when no monofilament is available.)
- **Eyes** — a dilated fundus exam (or retinal photography) for diabetic retinopathy; the earliest sign is microaneurysms, and new vessels (proliferative disease) or macular oedema are the sight-threatening ones needing urgent referral.
- **Vascular** — peripheral pulses, bruits, and skin changes for peripheral arterial disease; blood pressure and a cardiovascular exam at every visit, because cardiorenal risk drives drug choice.

> [!note] 💎 Pearls — exam signs
> - **Diabetic cheiroarthropathy — the "prayer sign".** The patient cannot flatten the palms together (limited joint mobility from skin/tendon glycation); it correlates with duration and microvascular disease and is a free bedside marker of chronic hyperglycemia.
> - **Necrobiosis lipoidica diabeticorum** — yellow-brown, atrophic, telangiectatic shin plaques that can ulcerate; it may *precede* the diabetes diagnosis, so it warrants glucose testing.
> - **Charcot neuroarthropathy** — a warm, swollen, erythematous, deformed but relatively painless foot in a neuropathic patient, routinely misread as cellulitis; unload it early to prevent a rocker-bottom foot.

## 🩻 Investigations

Diagnosis rests on any one of the four biochemical criteria in the table below. **In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia (a hyperglycemic crisis, or classic symptoms with a random glucose ≥200 mg/dL), two abnormal results are required** — either the same test repeated on a second sample, or two different tests on one sample (e.g. A1C plus FPG). A single abnormal test in an asymptomatic person is repeated before the label is applied; if two different tests disagree, the abnormal one is repeated.

A1C is convenient (no fasting, low biological variability, reflects ~3 months of glycemia) but is **falsely low** whenever red-cell survival is shortened (hemolysis, recent transfusion or erythropoietin, iron-deficiency treatment, pregnancy, advanced CKD) and unreliable with hemoglobinopathies; it must be run on an NGSP-certified, DCCT-aligned assay. In any of those settings, diagnose and monitor with FPG or OGTT (or fructosamine) instead. The OGTT is the most sensitive test and detects isolated post-prandial hyperglycemia the other two miss, at the cost of being cumbersome.

The baseline workup after diagnosis stages risk and screens complications, and each result feeds a management decision:

- **A1C** — baseline and every 3–6 months to target.
- **Lipid profile** — nearly all patients warrant a statin on risk grounds.
- **Serum creatinine + eGFR** and **urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR)** — CKD staging (the CGA grid) that both selects an SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA and dictates metformin dosing.
- **Dilated eye exam** at diagnosis (retinopathy may pre-date it).
- **Liver enzymes** — screen for MASLD, which is highly prevalent.
- **Foot exam** and cardiovascular risk assessment as above.

## 🩻 Diagnostic criteria (ADA Standards of Care 2026)

| Test | Diabetes | Prediabetes | Normal |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1C | **≥6.5%** (≥48 mmol/mol) | 5.7–6.4% (39–47 mmol/mol) | <5.7% |
| Fasting plasma glucose (≥8 h) | **≥126 mg/dL** (7.0 mmol/L) | 100–125 mg/dL (IFG) | <100 mg/dL |
| 2-h plasma glucose, 75-g OGTT | **≥200 mg/dL** (11.1 mmol/L) | 140–199 mg/dL (IGT) | <140 mg/dL |
| Random plasma glucose | **≥200 mg/dL** + classic symptoms or hyperglycemic crisis | — | — |

**Screening:** test asymptomatic adults with overweight/obesity plus ≥1 risk factor, and all adults from **age 35**; if normal, repeat at least every 3 years (annually in prediabetes, and consider annually in the high-risk).

**Thai guideline (แนวทางเวชปฏิบัติสำหรับโรคเบาหวาน 2566) — screening & diagnosis.** The biochemical cut-points are **identical** to the ADA (A1C ≥6.5%, FPG ≥126, 2-h OGTT ≥200; IFG 100–125, IGT 140–199, prediabetes A1C 5.7–6.4%), and the WHO 2019 classification is used (it adds a *hybrid type 1/type 2* form). Two things differ in Thai practice. First, screening is triggered by the **Thai Diabetes Risk Score** (age, BMI, waist-to-height ratio, hypertension, first-degree family history) — a blood test is done at **≥8 points or any single risk factor**, rather than a flat age rule. Second, "obese" uses **Asian cut-offs**: BMI ≥25 kg/m² and/or waist ≥90 cm (men) / ≥80 cm (women), or waist > height/2 — so risk is flagged at a lower BMI than Western criteria. Context for why screening is aggressive: Thai adult DM prevalence is ~11%, and ~31% of those are undiagnosed. *(Source: Thai DM CPG 2566, ch. 2.)*

## 🧩 Glycemic targets

The A1C goal is **individualized**, not a single universal number. ADA 2026 sets it against age, disease duration, comorbidity, hypoglycemia risk, and life expectancy:

| Patient profile | Reasonable A1C target |
|---|---|
| Many non-pregnant adults | **<7%** |
| Young, short duration, no CVD, achievable without hypoglycemia | **<6.5%** |
| Frail/elderly, limited life expectancy, severe-hypoglycemia history, advanced complications | **<8%** |

On continuous glucose monitoring the parallel goal is **Time in Range (70–180 mg/dL) >70%** with **time below range (<70) <4%** and time <54 mg/dL <1%. Relaxing the A1C target does not relax the mandate to use organ-protective drugs — those are chosen for cardiorenal benefit regardless of the glucose target.

The Thai CPG 2566 targets are the same (general A1C <7%, intensive <6.5%, fasting 80–130 mg/dL, elderly stratified 7–7.5% / <8% / no target) and add the non-glycemic goals every visit tracks: **BP <130/80**, LDL **<100 / <70 / <55 mg/dL** by cardiovascular risk (with ≥30–50% reduction), **BMI 18.5–22.9** (Asian range), and **waist < height/2**. *(Source: Thai DM CPG 2566, ch. 3.)*

## 💊 Management

Management stands on two legs: **lifestyle** and a **comorbidity-driven** drug choice. Lifestyle is not optional background — medical nutrition therapy, ≥150 min/week of moderate activity plus resistance training, and **5–7% weight loss** prevent progression from prediabetes and improve every outcome; ≥10–15% weight loss can drive T2DM into remission, especially early. The Thai CPG 2566 places the same weight on diet, Thai-context nutrition, and physical activity as first-line.

The ADA 2026 shift is decisive: **choose the drug by the cardiorenal-metabolic profile first**, then add agents for any residual glucose-lowering. Organ-protective agents (SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA) are started for their heart/kidney benefit independent of the A1C, and often independent of — or before — metformin.

| Dominant comorbidity | Preferred agent | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Heart failure (esp. HFrEF) | **SGLT2i** | Cuts HF hospitalization and CV death; benefit is class-wide and glucose-independent |
| CKD (albuminuria / low eGFR) | **SGLT2i** (add **GLP-1 RA** if more needed) | SGLT2i slows GFR decline; semaglutide (FLOW) now proven for hard renal outcomes |
| Established ASCVD | **GLP-1 RA** or **SGLT2i** | GLP-1 RA reduces atherosclerotic MACE; both have CV-death data |
| Obesity / weight priority | **Dual GIP/GLP-1 RA** (tirzepatide) or **GLP-1 RA** | Greatest weight loss; tirzepatide favored in T2DM + symptomatic HFpEF |
| None of the above (glucose only) | **Metformin** ± second agent by cost/efficacy | Cheap, effective, weight-neutral, no hypoglycemia |

**Agent detail and dosing:**

| Class / drug | Starting dose → target | Key benefit | Main caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metformin | 500 mg PO OD–BID with food → 1,000 mg BID (max 2,000–2,550 mg/d) | Glucose-lowering foundation; weight-neutral; no hypoglycemia | Hold if eGFR <30 (reduce 30–45); GI intolerance; rare lactic acidosis; check B12 |
| SGLT2i (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) | empagliflozin 10 mg PO OD (→25); dapagliflozin 10 mg OD | HF-hospitalization ↓, CKD progression ↓, CV death ↓; weight + BP ↓ | Genital mycotic infection, volume depletion, **euglycemic DKA** (hold when acutely ill / perioperative) |
| GLP-1 RA (semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide) | semaglutide SC 0.25 mg weekly → 1–2 mg; dulaglutide 0.75 → 4.5 mg weekly | Strong A1C + weight ↓; atherosclerotic MACE ↓; semaglutide renal (FLOW) | GI nausea (titrate slowly), gastroparesis caution, pancreatitis history; hold peri-endoscopy/anesthesia |
| Dual GIP/GLP-1 RA (tirzepatide) | 2.5 mg SC weekly → up to 15 mg | Largest A1C + weight reduction; HFpEF-symptom benefit | Same GI profile as GLP-1 RA |
| DPP-4i (sitagliptin, vildagliptin, linagliptin) | sitagliptin 100 mg PO OD (dose-reduce in CKD; linagliptin no adjustment) | Weight-neutral, very well tolerated, oral | Modest efficacy; **do not combine with a GLP-1 RA**; saxagliptin HF signal |
| TZD (pioglitazone) | 15–30 mg PO OD → 45 mg | Durable insulin sensitization; MASLD/stroke-recurrence data | Fluid retention/**HF**, weight gain, fracture, bladder-cancer caution |
| Sulfonylurea (glipizide, gliclazide MR, glibenclamide) | gliclazide MR 30–60 mg OD | Cheap, potent, oral; widely used in Thailand | **Hypoglycemia** and weight gain; avoid glibenclamide in elderly/CKD |
| Insulin (basal → basal-bolus) | glargine 10 U or 0.1–0.2 U/kg OD, titrate to fasting glucose | Works at any A1C; needed when very high or symptomatic | Hypoglycemia, weight gain; requires education + monitoring |

Sequencing in practice: start the comorbidity-indicated organ-protective agent early (with or without metformin), intensify to combination therapy rather than chasing one drug to its ceiling, and move to insulin when A1C is very high (e.g. ≥10% or glucose ≥300), when the patient is markedly symptomatic/catabolic, or when non-insulin therapy fails. In Thailand, access to SGLT2i and GLP-1 RA is the practical limiter — see Thailand availability.

**Thai CPG 2566 vs ADA 2026 — the ordering differs, and cost is why.** ADA 2026 is comorbidity-first: an SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA goes in early for ASCVD/HF/CKD *regardless of A1C and often before or without metformin*. The Thai CPG keeps **metformin first for most patients**, with a **sulfonylurea or pioglitazone** as the usual second agent, and reserves an SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA as first-line **only when the patient has ASCVD, heart failure, or CKD *and* has no cost barrier** — these drugs are largely self-pay and NHSO reimbursement is indication-gated. Two more Thai specifics: start **two oral agents together** if presenting glucose is >200 mg/dL or A1C ≥9%; and a GLP-1 RA added for obesity (BMI ≥30) is **stopped at 6 months** if A1C falls <1% or weight <3%. Net effect for a Thai patient: the evidence is identical, but affordability — not comorbidity alone — decides who actually receives an organ-protective agent. *(Source: Thai DM CPG 2566, ch. 7, algorithms 1–2; the Thai CPG is built on the ADA/EASD 2022 and ADA 2023 consensus.)*

## 📚 Landmark Trials

**Glycemic control & legacy**
- **UKPDS (1998)** — in newly diagnosed T2DM, intensive glucose control reduced microvascular complications, with a legacy macrovascular and mortality benefit emerging on 10-year post-trial follow-up (the "legacy effect"); the metformin sub-study established metformin as first-line in overweight patients.

**SGLT2 inhibitors — cardiorenal**
- **EMPA-REG OUTCOME (2015)** — empagliflozin in T2DM with established CVD: the first CVOT to reduce 3-point MACE (HR 0.86) and, strikingly, CV death (HR 0.62) and HF hospitalization — the trial that opened the cardiorenal era.
- **CANVAS (2017)** — canagliflozin reduced MACE but flagged an amputation and fracture signal.
- **DECLARE-TIMI 58 (2019)** — dapagliflozin reduced the composite of CV death and HF hospitalization across a broad, largely primary-prevention population.
- **CREDENCE (2019)** — canagliflozin slowed diabetic kidney disease progression (hard renal composite), extending SGLT2i benefit to dedicated renal endpoints.

**GLP-1 receptor agonists — atherosclerotic & renal**
- **LEADER (2016)** — liraglutide reduced 3-point MACE (HR 0.87) and CV death (HR 0.78).
- **SUSTAIN-6 (2016)** — injectable semaglutide reduced MACE, driven mainly by non-fatal stroke and MI.
- **REWIND (2019)** — dulaglutide reduced MACE in a mostly primary-prevention population, broadening the indication.
- **FLOW (2024)** — the first dedicated **kidney-outcome** trial of a GLP-1 RA: semaglutide 1.0 mg weekly in T2DM + CKD (n = 3,533, median 3.4 y) cut major kidney events, CV events, and death by **24%**, and MACE by 18%, with benefit independent of background SGLT2i use — establishing GLP-1 RAs as renal-protective in their own right.

**Incretin / dual agonist**
- **SURPASS program (2021–2022)** — tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 RA) delivered the largest A1C and weight reductions seen in phase-3 T2DM trials, superior to semaglutide 1 mg, degludec, and glargine.
- **SURPASS-CVOT (2024–2025)** — tirzepatide vs dulaglutide in T2DM with ASCVD confirmed cardiovascular safety/benefit, with exploratory kidney signals mediated by A1C and weight.

**Obesity–cardiometabolic overlap**
- **SELECT (2023)** — semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced MACE in overweight/obese patients **without** diabetes and established CVD, showing the cardiovascular benefit tracks the drug, not the glucose.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Do not withhold an SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA to "wait" for a high A1C** — in HF, CKD, or ASCVD they are started for organ protection regardless of the glucose number (ADA 2026).
> - **Euglycemic DKA on an SGLT2 inhibitor** — a patient on an SGLT2i can be in DKA with a near-normal glucose; check ketones and a venous gas whenever acutely ill, and hold the SGLT2i during acute illness and before surgery.
> - **Do not combine a DPP-4 inhibitor with a GLP-1 RA** — redundant incretin mechanisms, no added benefit, wasted cost.
> - **Sulfonylureas and insulin cause hypoglycemia** — the dangerous mistake in the frail elderly and in CKD; loosen the target and prefer agents without hypoglycemia risk in those patients.
> - **Metformin** — hold when eGFR <30 and around iodinated contrast/acute illness to avoid lactic acidosis.
> - **Pioglitazone is contraindicated in heart failure** — it causes fluid retention.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Diagnosis — A1C **≥6.5%** · FPG **≥126** · 2-h OGTT **≥200** · random **≥200 + symptoms**; confirm with two abnormal tests unless there is unequivocal hyperglycemia.
> - Prediabetes — A1C 5.7–6.4% · IFG 100–125 · IGT 140–199.
> - Pathophysiology — insulin resistance **+** progressive β-cell failure (ominous octet); it is progressive, so therapy escalates over time.
> - A1C target is **individualized** (~<7% for many; <6.5% if easy, <8% if frail), with CGM Time-in-Range >70%.
> - ADA 2026 + Thai CPG 2566 are **comorbidity-driven**: SGLT2i for HF/CKD, GLP-1 RA for ASCVD/weight, both independent of A1C; metformin remains the glucose-lowering base.
> - Newest evidence — **FLOW (2024)**: semaglutide is renal-protective in T2DM + CKD, even on an SGLT2i.
> - Pitfall — A1C is falsely low in hemolysis/CKD/hemoglobinopathy; euglycemic DKA on an SGLT2i.

> 🇹🇭 **Thailand availability & Thai guideline** — the Thai reference is **แนวทางเวชปฏิบัติสำหรับโรคเบาหวาน พ.ศ. 2566** (RCPT + Diabetes Association of Thailand + Endocrine Society of Thailand + DMS + NHSO), which mirrors the ADA cut-points and the comorbidity-driven algorithm. Metformin, sulfonylureas (gliclazide, glipizide, glibenclamide), pioglitazone, DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin, vildagliptin, linagliptin), and the SGLT2 inhibitors empagliflozin and dapagliflozin are available and widely used; SGLT2i are increasingly reimbursed for HF/CKD indications. GLP-1 RAs (liraglutide, dulaglutide, semaglutide) are registered but largely **self-pay and costly**, so real-world access is limited, and cost commonly keeps metformin + a sulfonylurea as the practical backbone. **Tirzepatide** availability and the current NHSO reimbursement tiers for cardiorenal agents should be confirmed against the Thai FDA register and the national formulary before being quoted (not verified here).

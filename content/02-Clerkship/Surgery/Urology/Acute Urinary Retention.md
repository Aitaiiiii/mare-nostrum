# Acute Urinary Retention (AUR)

The sudden, usually painful inability to pass urine despite a full bladder — a common urological emergency. It is quickly relieved by catheterisation, but the job does not end there: **find the cause, look for and manage the consequences (AKI, post-obstructive diuresis), and don't miss a neurological cause (cauda equina).**

## 🧬 Causes

- **Obstructive (commonest):** **benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)** — the classic male cause; also prostate cancer, urethral stricture, clot retention, stones, constipation (faecal impaction), pelvic mass, phimosis.
- **Neurological:** **spinal cord compression/cauda equina** (retention + saddle anaesthesia + bowel signs → emergency, see [[Spinal Cord Compression & Cauda Equina]]), diabetic/autonomic neuropathy, MS, post-spinal anaesthesia, stroke.
- **Drugs:** anticholinergics, opioids, sympathomimetics/decongestants, antihistamines, TCAs.
- **Infective/inflammatory:** prostatitis, UTI, herpes; **post-operative** (especially after pelvic/anorectal surgery, spinal anaesthesia).
- **Women** (less common): pelvic mass, prolapse, post-partum, Fowler's syndrome.

## 🩺 Presentation

Sudden inability to void with **suprapubic pain and a distended, palpable/percussable bladder** (acute-on-chronic retention may be painless with very large volumes and overflow incontinence). Assess for the cause: **PR for prostate/faecal impaction and anal tone; check perineal sensation and lower-limb neurology (cauda equina)**; medication and neurological history.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Bladder scan / ultrasound** confirms a full bladder and measures the volume; document the **drained volume** on catheterisation.
- **Urinalysis + culture** (infection/haematuria), **renal function/electrolytes** (obstructive AKI), glucose; **PSA** (defer — retention/catheter falsely elevates it), and imaging (renal US for hydronephrosis) if renal impairment.
- If neurological cause suspected (saddle anaesthesia, back pain, bilateral leg signs) → **urgent MRI spine** (cauda equina).

## 💊 Management

**1. Relieve the obstruction — immediate catheterisation.** Insert a **urethral catheter** (suprapubic catheter if urethral catheterisation fails/contraindicated, e.g. stricture, or in some pelvic trauma); **record the residual volume** (> ~400–1000 mL confirms significant retention). Provide analgesia; the pain resolves on drainage.

**2. Watch for and manage post-obstructive diuresis.** After decompressing a **large-volume/high-pressure chronic retention**, a **post-obstructive diuresis** (marked polyuria) can occur → **monitor urine output, fluid balance, weight, and electrolytes; replace fluids/electrolytes if the diuresis is pathological** (excessive/causing hypovolaemia or electrolyte derangement). Manage any **obstructive AKI** ([[Acute Kidney Injury]]).

**3. Treat the cause + plan.** Start an **α-blocker (e.g. tamsulosin)** in men with likely BPH before a trial without catheter (TWOC), which improves success; treat UTI/prostatitis, relieve constipation, stop precipitant drugs. **TWOC** after a period (with α-blocker); failed TWOC → longer-term catheter + urology referral for definitive BPH management (or surgery). **Cauda equina/spinal cause → emergency neurosurgical pathway.**

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Urethral/suprapubic catheters, bladder scanners/ultrasound, α-blockers, and urology services are available at Thai hospitals. BPH-related AUR in older men is the common presentation; the deliverables are prompt catheterisation, cause identification, and monitoring for post-obstructive diuresis/AKI — with a low threshold to exclude a **cauda equina** cause when neurological features are present.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Retention + saddle anaesthesia / bilateral leg signs / back pain = cauda equina** → urgent MRI + neurosurgery (see [[Spinal Cord Compression & Cauda Equina]]).
> - **Catheterise promptly + record the drained volume**; look for **obstructive AKI** and hydronephrosis.
> - **Post-obstructive diuresis** after large-volume decompression → monitor and replace fluids/electrolytes.
> - **Start an α-blocker before TWOC** in BPH; treat/stop precipitants (drugs, constipation, infection).
> - Don't over-interpret a **PSA** taken during retention/after catheter (falsely high).

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Painful inability to void + **palpable/percussable bladder**; **immediate catheterisation, record residual volume**.
> - Causes: **BPH (commonest)**, obstruction, **neurological (cauda equina!)**, drugs, infection, post-op.
> - **Post-obstructive diuresis** after big-volume drainage → monitor UOP/electrolytes/fluids; watch obstructive AKI.
> - **α-blocker before TWOC**; failed TWOC → urology; **saddle anaesthesia/leg signs → MRI (cauda equina)**.
> - PR exam (prostate/impaction/anal tone) + perineal sensation are part of the assessment.

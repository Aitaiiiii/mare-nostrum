# Priapism

A persistent penile erection lasting **> 4 hours, unrelated to sexual stimulation.** The critical fork is **ischaemic (low-flow) vs non-ischaemic (high-flow)**: ischaemic priapism is a **compartment-syndrome emergency** — the corpora are ischaemic and every hour of delay worsens the chance of permanent erectile dysfunction, so it is treated immediately.

## 🧬 Types & Pathophysiology

| | **Ischaemic (low-flow)** — the emergency | **Non-ischaemic (high-flow)** |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | veno-occlusive: blood cannot leave → stasis, hypoxia, acidosis (a penile "compartment syndrome") | unregulated arterial inflow (fistula), usually **post-traumatic** |
| Pain | **painful, rigid corpora** | often painless, semi-rigid |
| Corporal blood gas | **dark, hypoxic/acidotic** (PO₂ low, PCO₂ high, pH low) | bright red, arterial gas |
| Urgency | **emergency — treat now** | not an emergency; can observe/embolise |

**Causes of ischaemic priapism:** **sickle-cell disease/haemoglobinopathies (including in Thailand's haemoglobinopathy population)**, intracavernosal/oral erectile-dysfunction drugs, other drugs (trazodone, antipsychotics, α-blockers, cocaine), malignancy/infiltration, and idiopathic. Prolonged ischaemia (> ~24–48 h) → corporal fibrosis and permanent ED.

## 🩺 Presentation & investigations

A prolonged, usually painful, rigid erection (the glans/corpus spongiosum are typically soft). **Distinguish ischaemic from non-ischaemic** by history (trauma → high-flow), examination (rigid + painful → ischaemic), and **corporal blood-gas aspirate** (dark/acidotic = ischaemic) ± colour Doppler ultrasound. Check for a cause: CBC/haemoglobinopathy screen (sickle/thalassaemia), drug history, toxicology.

## 💊 Management (ischaemic priapism — act fast)

**1. First-line: corporal aspiration + intracavernosal phenylephrine.** After local anaesthesia, **aspirate blood from the corpus cavernosum (± saline irrigation)** and **inject intracavernosal phenylephrine** — dilute to **100–500 µg/mL, giving 100–200 µg every 5 minutes** with blood-pressure/pulse monitoring (α-agonist → hypertension/reflex bradycardia/arrhythmia risk), **up to ~1 hour or 1000 µg total.** This detumesces most cases and is done **before surgery.**

**2. Treat the underlying cause in parallel** — e.g. **sickle-cell/haemoglobinopathy priapism**: hydration, analgesia, oxygen, and haematology involvement (exchange transfusion for refractory cases) — but **do NOT delay aspiration/phenylephrine** while treating the cause.

**3. Refractory → surgical shunt.** If detumescence fails after aspiration + sympathomimetic, proceed to a **distal (or proximal) surgical shunt** (± tunnelling); prolonged/refractory cases may need early penile prosthesis discussion. Time is erectile function.

**4. Non-ischaemic (high-flow)** priapism is not an emergency — observe; many resolve; selective **arterial embolisation** for persistent cases.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Phenylephrine, local anaesthetic, aspiration kits, Doppler ultrasound, and urological/interventional services are available; **haemoglobinopathies (sickle cell, thalassaemia/HbE) are prevalent in Thailand**, so sickle/Hb-related priapism is a relevant cause — treat the priapism urgently and involve haematology. Some erectile-dysfunction and psychiatric drugs are common precipitants.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Ischaemic priapism (painful, rigid, dark corporal gas) is an emergency** — corporal aspiration + intracavernosal phenylephrine NOW; delay causes permanent ED.
> - **Distinguish ischaemic from non-ischaemic** (corporal blood gas / history of trauma) — treatment is opposite.
> - **Monitor BP/pulse with phenylephrine** (hypertension/arrhythmia).
> - **Don't delay aspiration/phenylephrine to treat the cause** (e.g. sickle cell) — do both.
> - Refractory → **surgical shunt**; time-dependent salvage of erectile function.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Erection **> 4 h unrelated to arousal**; **ischaemic (painful, rigid, dark/acidotic gas) = emergency** vs high-flow (traumatic, painless, not urgent).
> - **Aspiration + intracavernosal phenylephrine (100–200 µg q5min, ≤ ~1 mg)** first-line — before surgery.
> - Treat the cause in parallel (**sickle/thalassaemia**, drugs) — don't delay.
> - **Refractory → surgical shunt**; non-ischaemic → observe ± embolise.
> - Prolonged ischaemia → fibrosis/permanent ED — act within hours.

# Testicular Torsion

Twisting of the spermatic cord occluding testicular blood flow — a **time-critical surgical emergency**. Testicular salvage falls steeply with time: **near 100% if detorsed within ~6 hours, dropping sharply beyond that**, so the diagnosis is clinical and the patient goes to theatre on suspicion — **imaging must never delay surgery.**

## 🧬 Pathophysiology & at-risk anatomy

The cord twists → venous obstruction → congestion, arterial compromise, and haemorrhagic infarction of the testis. The predisposing anomaly is the **"bell-clapper" deformity** (high investment of tunica vaginalis leaving the testis free to rotate), which is usually **bilateral** — hence both testes are fixed at surgery. Peaks in **neonates and adolescents** (12–18 y). **Extravaginal** torsion occurs in neonates.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Sudden, severe unilateral testicular/scrotal pain**, often with nausea/vomiting; may wake the patient from sleep or follow activity/trauma. Exam: a **high-riding testis with a horizontal (transverse) lie, swollen and exquisitely tender, an absent cremasteric reflex**, and **negative Prehn's sign** (elevation does not relieve pain — unlike epididymitis). Onset is acute (vs the more gradual epididymo-orchitis). Consider torsion in any boy with **abdominal/groin pain** — always examine the scrotum.

> [!note] 💎 Pearls
> - An **absent cremasteric reflex** is the most sensitive single sign of torsion (though not perfectly specific).
> - **Torsion of a testicular/epididymal appendage** ("blue dot sign," a tender upper-pole nodule) is a benign mimic managed conservatively — but if you cannot confidently exclude cord torsion, explore.

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Diagnosis is clinical; do NOT delay surgical exploration for imaging when suspicion is high.**
- **Scrotal colour-Doppler ultrasound** (absent/reduced intratesticular flow) is useful when the diagnosis is genuinely uncertain and available immediately — but a normal Doppler does **not** fully exclude intermittent/early torsion.
- Risk-stratification tools (e.g. **TWIST score**: testis swelling, hard testis, absent cremasteric reflex, nausea/vomiting, high-riding testis) can support triage but do not replace clinical judgement.
- Urinalysis (usually normal — pyuria suggests epididymitis).

## 🗂️ Differential diagnosis

Epididymo-orchitis (more gradual, fever/dysuria, **positive Prehn's**, present cremasteric reflex, pyuria), torsion of a testicular appendage (blue-dot sign), incarcerated inguinal hernia, testicular trauma/haematoma, tumour, and referred pain (renal colic).

## 💊 Management

**1. Emergency urology referral + exploration — the definitive treatment.** **Immediate surgical exploration**: detorse the testis, assess viability (warm, reperfuse), and perform **orchidopexy of BOTH testes** (fix the contralateral side because the bell-clapper anomaly is bilateral). A non-viable testis is removed (**orchidectomy**). Keep the patient **nil by mouth** from presentation.

**2. Manual detorsion — a bridge, not a substitute.** While arranging theatre, attempt **manual detorsion** (classically rotating the testis **outward/laterally — "opening a book,"** i.e. medial-to-lateral; the direction is variable, so use pain relief and restoration of flow as the guide). Success relieves pain and buys time but **still requires definitive orchidopexy.**

**3. Analgesia** and anti-emetics; do not let work-up delay theatre.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Scrotal Doppler ultrasound and emergency urological/general-surgical exploration are available at Thai hospitals; where urology is not on-site, general surgery explores or the patient is transferred **urgently** — the deliverable at any facility is early recognition, NPO, analgesia, manual detorsion attempt, and rapid theatre, because the salvage window is short.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Sudden testicular pain = torsion until excluded** → emergency exploration; **do NOT wait for ultrasound** when suspicion is high.
> - **~6-hour salvage window** — every hour of delay lowers the chance of saving the testis.
> - **Always examine the scrotum** in a boy with abdominal/groin pain.
> - **Fix BOTH testes** (bilateral bell-clapper anomaly).
> - A normal Doppler does not exclude early/intermittent torsion — if the story fits, explore.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Acute severe testicular pain + high-riding horizontal testis + absent cremasteric reflex + negative Prehn's** = torsion.
> - **Surgical emergency, ~6 h window → explore on clinical suspicion**; imaging must not delay theatre.
> - **Manual detorsion (usually medial-to-lateral, "open book") as a bridge**; then **bilateral orchidopexy**.
> - Non-viable → orchidectomy; keep **NPO** from the start.
> - Epididymitis (gradual, fever/dysuria, **positive Prehn's**, cremasteric present) is the main mimic.

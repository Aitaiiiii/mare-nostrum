# Major Burns

Thermal (or chemical/electrical) injury to skin and deeper tissue. Beyond the wound, a major burn is a **whole-body resuscitation problem** — airway (inhalation injury), massive fluid shifts, hypothermia, infection, and (in circumferential burns) compartment syndrome. The ER priorities are the trauma primary survey with a burns-specific focus on **airway and fluid resuscitation.**

## 🩺 Primary survey & airway (the first priority)

Treat as trauma (ATLS) — stop the burning, remove clothing/jewellery, and assess **A-B-C-D-E**. **Airway is the emergency:** suspect **inhalation injury** with **burns in an enclosed space, facial burns, singed nasal/facial hair, hoarseness/stridor, carbonaceous sputum, soot in the airway, or a depressed conscious level** → **early, elective intubation before airway oedema makes it impossible** (progressive swelling over hours). Give **high-flow oxygen**; suspect **carbon monoxide/cyanide** poisoning in enclosed-space fires (→ [[Carbon Monoxide Poisoning]]).

## Assessing depth & size

- **Depth:** superficial (epidermal — red, painful, no blisters), superficial partial-thickness (blisters, moist, very painful, blanches), deep partial-thickness (less blanching/sensation), full-thickness (white/leathery/charred, painless, no blanching).
- **%TBSA (total body surface area) — count only partial- and full-thickness burns:** **Rule of Nines** (adult: head 9%, each arm 9%, each leg 18%, anterior trunk 18%, posterior trunk 18%, genitalia 1%); the **patient's palm ≈ 1%** for scattered burns; Lund-Browder chart is most accurate (and needed in children, whose proportions differ).

## 💊 Management

**1. Fluid resuscitation — Parkland formula (for burns ≥ 20% TBSA adults / ≥ 10% children).** **Lactated Ringer's 4 mL × body weight (kg) × %TBSA over 24 h, giving HALF in the first 8 hours (from the time of injury) and the remainder over the next 16 h.** Children: 3 mL/kg/%TBSA (+ maintenance with dextrose). **The formula is a STARTING ESTIMATE — titrate to urine output** (adults ~0.5 mL/kg/h; higher in electrical/rhabdomyolysis), avoiding both under- and **over-resuscitation ("fluid creep" → oedema/compartment/ARDS)**. Large-bore access (through burned skin if needed); catheterise to monitor output.

**2. Circulation/perfusion & escharotomy.** **Circumferential full-thickness burns of a limb (or chest — restricting ventilation) → compartment syndrome → escharotomy** (incise the eschar to restore perfusion/ventilation). Monitor limb perfusion and airway pressures.

**3. Wound care, analgesia, and the other essentials.** Cover burns (clean dressings), **strong IV analgesia (opioids)**, **tetanus prophylaxis**, keep the patient **warm (burns lose heat → hypothermia worsens outcomes)**, NG tube (ileus/large burns), and early nutrition. Do NOT give prophylactic systemic antibiotics; topical antimicrobials for the wound; escalate for sepsis.

**4. Refer to a burn centre.** Criteria include: **partial-thickness > ~10–20% TBSA, any full-thickness burn, burns of the face/hands/feet/genitalia/perineum/major joints, inhalation injury, electrical or serious chemical burns, circumferential burns, and burns with major trauma/comorbidity or at the extremes of age.** Chemical burns → copious irrigation; electrical burns → cardiac monitoring + rhabdomyolysis care (→ [[Rhabdomyolysis]]).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Lactated Ringer's (Acetar), opioids, tetanus prophylaxis, escharotomy, and **burn units at Thai tertiary hospitals** are available; the deliverables at any facility are airway assessment/early intubation, Parkland-guided resuscitation titrated to urine output, warmth, analgesia, and timely burn-centre referral. Scald and flame burns (cooking, agricultural/industrial) are common; electrical burns and enclosed-space fires carry CO/cyanide risk.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Inhalation injury (facial burns, soot, hoarseness, enclosed space) → intubate EARLY** before airway oedema; give O₂; think CO/cyanide.
> - **Titrate Parkland to urine output** — avoid over-resuscitation ("fluid creep") and under-resuscitation.
> - **Circumferential full-thickness limb/chest burn → escharotomy** (compartment syndrome/ventilation).
> - **Keep the patient warm**; give tetanus prophylaxis; **no prophylactic systemic antibiotics**.
> - **Refer major/special-area/inhalation/electrical/chemical burns to a burn centre**; electrical → cardiac monitoring + rhabdo care.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Primary survey; **airway first (early intubation for inhalation injury)**; CO/cyanide in enclosed-space fires.
> - **%TBSA (partial + full thickness) by Rule of Nines** (palm ≈ 1%); Lund-Browder in children.
> - **Parkland: LR 4 mL/kg/%TBSA over 24 h, half in first 8 h** (≥ 20% adults) — **titrate to UOP ~0.5 mL/kg/h**.
> - **Escharotomy** for circumferential full-thickness burns; warmth, analgesia, tetanus; no prophylactic antibiotics.
> - Burn-centre referral criteria (face/hands/genitalia/full-thickness/inhalation/electrical/chemical).

# Acute Appendicitis

Acute inflammation of the vermiform appendix — the commonest surgical cause of the acute abdomen. The clinical task is **timely diagnosis** (a clinical + imaging judgement, not a single test) and deciding **operation vs antibiotics**, because delay risks perforation, abscess, and peritonitis.

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

**Luminal obstruction** (faecolith most often; lymphoid hyperplasia in the young, tumour/parasite occasionally) → rising intraluminal pressure, mucus accumulation, bacterial overgrowth, venous congestion, mucosal ischaemia, and transmural inflammation. Untreated: **gangrene → perforation** (localised abscess/phlegmon or generalised peritonitis). The visceral-to-somatic pain shift explains the classic migration.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

**Classic sequence: peri-umbilical/epigastric visceral pain → migrates to the right lower quadrant (McBurney's point) → with anorexia, nausea, low-grade fever.** Signs: RLQ tenderness with guarding/rebound; **Rovsing's** (LLQ palpation → RLQ pain), **psoas** (pain on right hip extension — retrocaecal appendix), and **obturator** (pain on internal rotation of flexed hip — pelvic appendix) signs. A retrocaecal or pelvic appendix gives atypical presentations. **High fever + diffuse peritonitis suggests perforation.** Consider the pregnant patient (pain higher/lateral) and the elderly (atypical, higher perforation rate).

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Bloods:** CBC (leukocytosis with left shift), CRP; **β-hCG in every woman of childbearing age** (exclude ectopic); urinalysis (exclude UTI/stone — mild pyuria can occur from an inflamed appendix near the ureter).
- **Risk scores:** **Alvarado** (MANTRELS — Migration, Anorexia, Nausea, Tenderness RLQ, Rebound, Elevated temp, Leukocytosis, Shift) and **AIR** score stratify low/intermediate/high probability and guide imaging vs observation vs surgery.
- **Imaging:** **US first-line** in children and pregnancy (non-inflamed appendix not seen; look for non-compressible blind-ending tubular structure > 6 mm); **CT abdomen** is most accurate in adults (intermediate probability, atypical, or complicated disease); **MRI** in pregnancy when US is inconclusive.

## 🗂️ Differential diagnosis

Mesenteric adenitis (children), gastroenteritis, [[Acute Pancreatitis]], right-sided renal colic/[[Urolithiasis (Renal Colic)]], **ectopic pregnancy/ovarian torsion/PID/ruptured ovarian cyst** (women), Meckel's diverticulitis, Crohn's ileitis, right-sided diverticulitis, and testicular torsion.

## 💊 Management

**1. Uncomplicated appendicitis — appendectomy is standard.** **Laparoscopic appendectomy** is the treatment of choice (less pain, faster recovery, fewer wound infections). Give **pre-operative antibiotics** (a single dose covering Gram-negatives + anaerobes, e.g. a cephalosporin + metronidazole or amoxicillin-clavulanate) and analgesia; keep NPO with IV fluids. Timing within ~12–24 h is acceptable and does not materially increase perforation.

**2. Antibiotics-first (non-operative) — an option in selected uncomplicated cases.** Trials (**APPAC, CODA**) show antibiotics alone can treat uncomplicated appendicitis initially, but **~30–40% recur/need appendectomy within a year**, and it must exclude an appendicolith (higher failure). Reserve for patients who decline or are poor surgical candidates, with shared decision-making.

**3. Complicated appendicitis (perforation, abscess, phlegmon).**
- **Generalised peritonitis/free perforation → urgent appendectomy + broad-spectrum antibiotics + resuscitation.**
- **Contained abscess/phlegmon → broad-spectrum antibiotics ± image-guided percutaneous drainage**, often with **interval appendectomy** later (and, in older patients, colonoscopy to exclude an underlying tumour).

**4. Supportive:** analgesia (giving it does not mask the diagnosis), antiemetics, IV fluids, and correct electrolytes.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Laparoscopic and open appendectomy, US, and CT are available across Thai hospitals; pre-op antibiotics (cephalosporin/metronidazole/amoxicillin-clavulanate) are standard NLEM agents. Diagnostic ultrasound-first in children/pregnancy conserves radiation. Where imaging is limited (some community hospitals), the **Alvarado/AIR score plus serial examination** guides the operate-or-observe decision.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Always do a β-hCG in women** — ectopic pregnancy mimics appendicitis and is life-threatening.
> - **High fever + diffuse peritonitis = perforation** → urgent surgery + broad antibiotics + resuscitation.
> - **Atypical presentations** in the elderly, pregnant, retrocaecal/pelvic appendix, and immunocompromised — a normal exam does not exclude it; use imaging + serial review.
> - **Antibiotics-first** is not a cure — counsel ~30–40% recurrence; avoid it when an appendicolith is present.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - **Migratory peri-umbilical → RLQ pain + anorexia**; Rovsing/psoas/obturator signs.
> - **Alvarado/AIR score + US (children/pregnancy) or CT (adults)** to risk-stratify.

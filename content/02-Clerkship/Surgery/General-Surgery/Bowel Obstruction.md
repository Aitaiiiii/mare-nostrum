# Bowel Obstruction (SBO & LBO)

Mechanical blockage of the intestinal lumen (or functional failure of peristalsis, ileus). The clinical work is to **confirm obstruction, decide small- vs large-bowel and partial vs complete, and — most importantly — detect the features that signal strangulation/ischaemia or a closed loop**, which convert a "drip and suck" problem into an emergency laparotomy.

## 🧬 Causes & Pathophysiology

Proximal to the block: distension from gas/fluid → increased intraluminal pressure → impaired venous drainage → **bowel-wall oedema, ischaemia, bacterial translocation, and (if untreated) perforation**; fluid sequestration causes hypovolaemia and electrolyte loss. A **closed loop** (obstruction at two points, e.g. volvulus, incarcerated hernia) or **strangulation** (compromised blood supply) ischaemias rapidly.

| | **Small bowel (SBO)** | **Large bowel (LBO)** |
|---|---|---|
| Common causes | **adhesions (commonest)**, hernias, tumour, Crohn's, intussusception | **colorectal cancer (commonest)**, **volvulus (sigmoid/caecal)**, diverticular stricture, faecal impaction |
| Vomiting | early, bilious | late, feculent |
| Distension | less | marked |

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

The four cardinal features: **colicky abdominal pain, vomiting, abdominal distension, and absolute constipation (obstipation — no flatus or faeces).** Timing varies: proximal SBO → early vomiting, less distension; distal SBO/LBO → marked distension, later/feculent vomiting. Auscultation: **early high-pitched "tinkling" bowel sounds → later silent** (ileus/ischaemia). **Examine all hernial orifices** and the rectum. **Red flags for strangulation/ischaemia:** continuous (rather than colicky) severe pain, fever, tachycardia, **peritonism/localised tenderness/guarding, rising lactate, and leukocytosis.**

## 🩻 Investigations

- **CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast** is the investigation of choice — confirms obstruction, **transition point, cause, and signs of ischaemia** (bowel-wall thickening, pneumatosis, reduced enhancement, closed loop, free fluid/air). Erect/supine abdominal X-ray (dilated loops, air-fluid levels; **> 3 cm small bowel, > 6 cm colon, > 9 cm caecum**) is a quick screen.
- **Bloods:** CBC, U&E (dehydration, hypokalaemia/metabolic alkalosis from vomiting), **lactate** (ischaemia), CRP, amylase, group & save, coagulation.
- Water-soluble contrast (Gastrografin) study in adhesive SBO is both prognostic and can be therapeutic.

## 💊 Management

**1. Resuscitate + "drip and suck" (initial for most SBO).** **NG tube on free drainage** (decompress, reduce vomiting/aspiration), **IV fluids + correct electrolytes (K⁺!)**, NPO, analgesia, antiemetics, urinary catheter + strict I/O, VTE prophylaxis.

**2. Non-operative trial — for uncomplicated, partial, adhesive SBO.** Most **partial adhesive SBO without ischaemia settle within 24–48 h** on conservative management; a **water-soluble contrast study** helps predict resolution and can hasten it. Reassess clinically and biochemically frequently — **failure to progress or any deterioration → surgery.**

**3. Surgery — when it is mandated.**
- **Signs of strangulation/ischaemia, peritonitis, or perforation → emergency laparotomy.**
- **Closed-loop obstruction, complete obstruction, obstructed/strangulated hernia → surgery.**
- **LBO** is more often surgical: **colorectal cancer** (resection ± stoma or stenting as a bridge), **sigmoid volvulus → endoscopic decompression/detorsion first** (then elective surgery; emergency if failed/ischaemic), **caecal volvulus → surgery.**
- **Failed conservative SBO** or virgin abdomen (no adhesion history) with SBO → lower threshold for surgery.

**4. Special situations.** **Pseudo-obstruction (Ogilvie's)** — colonic dilatation without mechanical cause; treat the precipitant, correct electrolytes, decompress, and **neostigmine** for refractory cases (with monitoring). Distinguish from ileus (post-op, metabolic).

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

CT, contrast studies, endoscopic decompression, and surgery are available at Thai hospitals. Adhesive SBO (prior surgery) and obstructing **colorectal cancer** are the common Thai presentations; **sigmoid volvulus** is seen in older/institutionalised patients. The deliverables anywhere are resuscitation, NG decompression, imaging, and early recognition of the strangulation/complete-obstruction features that need transfer/surgery.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Strangulation/ischaemia** (continuous pain, fever, tachycardia, peritonism, **rising lactate**, leukocytosis) → **emergency surgery — do not persist with conservative management.**
> - **Closed-loop, complete obstruction, or obstructed hernia** → surgery.
> - **Examine all hernial orifices and the rectum** — a missed groin hernia is a classic cause.
> - **LBO with a competent ileocaecal valve → closed loop → caecal perforation risk** (caecum > 9 cm) — urgent.
> - **Sigmoid volvulus → endoscopic decompression first** (unless ischaemic/perforated).

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Four features: **colicky pain, vomiting, distension, obstipation**; SBO (adhesions) vs LBO (cancer/volvulus).
> - **CT abdomen/pelvis** = test of choice (transition point, cause, ischaemia); XR limits **3/6/9 cm**.
> - **Drip and suck (NGT + IV fluids + correct K⁺)** for uncomplicated partial adhesive SBO — most settle in 24–48 h.
> - **Surgery for strangulation/ischaemia, closed loop, complete obstruction, obstructed hernia, failed conservative, most LBO.**
> - **Sigmoid volvulus → endoscopic detorsion**; Ogilvie's → neostigmine.

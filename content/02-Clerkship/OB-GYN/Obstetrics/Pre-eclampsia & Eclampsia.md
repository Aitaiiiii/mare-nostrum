# Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia

A pregnancy-specific multisystem disorder of new hypertension with end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks' gestation, and its convulsive complication, **eclampsia**. It is a leading cause of maternal and perinatal death, and the ER priorities are simple and lifesaving: **magnesium sulfate to prevent/stop seizures, urgent control of severe hypertension to prevent maternal stroke, and delivery — the only definitive cure.**

## 🧬 Pathophysiology

Abnormal placentation → poor spiral-artery remodelling → placental ischaemia → release of anti-angiogenic factors (↑sFlt-1, ↓PlGF) → **systemic endothelial dysfunction**: vasoconstriction (hypertension), increased vascular permeability (oedema, proteinuria, pulmonary oedema, cerebral oedema → seizures), and activation of coagulation (HELLP, DIC). It resolves only with removal of the placenta.

## 🩺 Diagnosis & severe features

**Pre-eclampsia = new hypertension (≥ 140/90 on two occasions ≥ 4 h apart) after 20 weeks PLUS proteinuria OR any end-organ feature** (proteinuria is no longer required if severe features are present).

**Severe features (any one → "pre-eclampsia with severe features"):**
- **BP ≥ 160 systolic or ≥ 110 diastolic** (on two occasions ~4 h apart, or once if antihypertensive already needed)
- Thrombocytopenia (< 100,000), **HELLP** (Haemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets)
- Impaired liver function / severe persistent RUQ or epigastric pain
- Renal insufficiency (creatinine > 1.1 mg/dL or doubling)
- Pulmonary oedema
- New cerebral/visual symptoms (headache, visual disturbance, hyperreflexia/clonus)

**Eclampsia = new generalised seizures** in a pre-eclamptic woman (can occur antepartum, intrapartum, or **postpartum** — up to ~6 weeks). **HELLP** may present with RUQ pain/malaise without marked hypertension.

## 🩻 Investigations

CBC (platelets, haemolysis film/LDH/haptoglobin), **LFTs (AST/ALT)**, renal function/uric acid, **urine protein (spot protein:creatinine ratio)**, coagulation (if HELLP/DIC), and **fetal assessment** (CTG, ultrasound/growth/Doppler, amniotic fluid). Blood group & save.

## 💊 Management

**1. Magnesium sulfate — seizure prophylaxis and treatment (the key drug).** Give to **pre-eclampsia with severe features and to all eclampsia** (reduces eclampsia by > 50%; treats and prevents recurrent seizures — it is superior to conventional anticonvulsants in eclampsia).
- **Zuspan (IV):** loading **4–6 g IV over 15–20 min**, then **1–2 g/hour infusion**, continued **24 h after delivery or the last seizure.**
- **Pritchard (IM, where no infusion pump):** **4 g IV + 5 g IM into each buttock (10 g)**, then **5 g IM every 4 h.**
- **Monitor for magnesium toxicity:** deep-tendon reflexes (lost first), respiratory rate (< 12), urine output (< 30 mL/h → accumulation), and O₂ sat; **antidote = calcium gluconate 1 g IV.** Reduce/hold in renal impairment.

**2. Control severe hypertension urgently (prevent maternal stroke).** For **BP ≥ 160/110**, treat **within 30–60 minutes**, aiming to lower to ~**140/90** (not lower — preserve uteroplacental flow). First-line: **IV labetalol, IV hydralazine, or oral immediate-release nifedipine** (all acceptable; choose by availability/contraindication — avoid labetalol in asthma/heart block).

**3. Delivery — the only cure.** Timing balances maternal risk vs prematurity: **deliver at ≥ 37 weeks for pre-eclampsia without severe features**, and **at ≥ 34 weeks (or sooner/immediately) for severe features/eclampsia/HELLP or maternal-fetal compromise**, after stabilisation (magnesium + BP control + antenatal corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity if < 34 weeks and time permits). **Do not delay delivery to give steroids if the mother is unstable.** Mode of delivery is obstetric-led.

**4. Supportive.** Careful fluid balance (**restrict** — risk of pulmonary oedema), monitor for HELLP/DIC/abruption/AKI, continue magnesium and BP control **postpartum** (eclampsia can occur/recur postpartum), and counsel on future CVD risk and recurrence.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Magnesium sulfate, labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine, calcium gluconate, and antenatal corticosteroids are all on the Thai NLEM and used routinely in Thai obstetric units; the Pritchard IM regimen is well suited to settings without infusion pumps. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are a leading cause of Thai maternal mortality, so magnesium + BP control + timely delivery are core skills. Postpartum eclampsia awareness matters given early discharges.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Magnesium sulfate** for severe features/eclampsia — monitor reflexes/RR/urine output; **calcium gluconate is the antidote** for toxicity.
> - **BP ≥ 160/110 → treat within 30–60 min** (labetalol/hydralazine/nifedipine) to prevent stroke; aim ~140/90.
> - **Delivery is the only cure** — don't over-prolong pregnancy with severe features/HELLP/eclampsia.
> - **Eclampsia can occur POSTPARTUM (up to ~6 weeks)** — new seizure/headache/BP postpartum = eclampsia until excluded.
> - **Restrict fluids** (pulmonary oedema risk); watch for HELLP/DIC/abruption.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Pre-eclampsia = new HTN + end-organ after 20 wk; **severe features incl. BP ≥ 160/110, HELLP, cerebral/visual symptoms, pulmonary oedema.**
> - **MgSO₄ (Zuspan 4–6 g IV then 1–2 g/h, or Pritchard 4 g IV+10 g IM then 5 g IM q4h) × 24 h post-delivery/seizure**; toxicity antidote **calcium gluconate**.
> - **Severe HTN ≥ 160/110 → labetalol/hydralazine/nifedipine within 30–60 min**, aim ~140/90.
> - **Delivery cures it** — ≥ 37 wk (no severe features) or ≥ 34 wk / now (severe/eclampsia/HELLP).
> - **Eclampsia can be postpartum**; restrict fluids; magnesium is superior to standard anticonvulsants.

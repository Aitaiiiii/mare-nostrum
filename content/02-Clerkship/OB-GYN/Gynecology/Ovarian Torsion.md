# Ovarian (Adnexal) Torsion

Twisting of the ovary (± fallopian tube) on its vascular pedicle → venous congestion → arterial compromise → ischaemia and infarction. It is a **gynaecological surgical emergency** and a commonly delayed diagnosis, because imaging is imperfect: **a normal Doppler does not exclude torsion**, so a convincing clinical picture goes to theatre.

## 🧬 Risk factors & Pathophysiology

An enlarged ovary is the main risk — a **mass or cyst (typically > 5 cm), dermoid, ovarian hyperstimulation, pregnancy**, and in children a long utero-ovarian ligament. Twisting first occludes the low-pressure veins (oedema, further enlargement) then arteries → infarction; the ovary can be salvageable even after prolonged torsion (dual blood supply), so **detorsion is attempted rather than removal.**

## 🩺 Presentation

**Sudden-onset, severe, unilateral lower-abdominal/pelvic pain**, often colicky and with nausea/vomiting; may be intermittent (partial/recurrent torsion). Exam: unilateral adnexal tenderness ± a palpable mass; peritonism if infarcted. **The presentation overlaps [[Acute Appendicitis|appendicitis]], [[Ectopic Pregnancy|ectopic]], ruptured cyst, and PID** — always do a **β-hCG.**

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Transvaginal/transabdominal ultrasound with Doppler** — an enlarged oedematous ovary, an adnexal mass, "whirlpool sign" of the twisted pedicle, and abnormal/absent flow. **Present arterial flow does NOT exclude torsion** (dual supply, intermittent) — imaging supports but cannot rule it out.
- **β-hCG** (exclude ectopic), CBC, urinalysis; CT/MRI if the diagnosis is unclear, but do not delay surgery in a high-suspicion case.

## 💊 Management

**1. Surgery is diagnostic and therapeutic — do not delay for imaging when suspicion is high.** **Laparoscopy** (preferred): **untwist (detorse) the ovary and conserve it** even if it looks dusky/ischaemic — it frequently recovers; **oophorectomy only for a frankly necrotic ovary or a suspicious/malignant mass.** Cystectomy of a causative cyst and **oophoropexy** (fixation) in selected/recurrent cases. Analgesia, antiemetics, NPO, and IV fluids while arranging theatre.

**2. Fertility-preserving mindset.** In a woman of reproductive age (and children), the default is **ovarian conservation** — historical practice over-removed salvageable ovaries.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Ultrasound with Doppler and laparoscopic gynaecological surgery are available at Thai hospitals; where laparoscopy is not on-site, transfer urgently or laparotomy. The deliverables anywhere: β-hCG, analgesia, NPO, and rapid gynaecology referral — because salvage depends on time and a normal Doppler cannot exclude it.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain ± adnexal mass = torsion until excluded** → urgent gynaecology/surgery.
> - **A normal/present Doppler flow does NOT exclude torsion** — operate on clinical suspicion.
> - **Do β-hCG in every woman** (ectopic mimic).
> - **Detorse and CONSERVE the ovary** (it recovers even when dusky) — avoid unnecessary oophorectomy.
> - Intermittent pain may be partial/recurrent torsion — don't dismiss it.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Risk: **ovarian mass/cyst > 5 cm, dermoid, OHSS, pregnancy**; sudden severe unilateral pain + vomiting.
> - **US Doppler** (enlarged ovary, whirlpool sign) supports but **normal flow doesn't exclude**; **β-hCG** always.
> - **Laparoscopy: detorse + conserve the ovary** (oophorectomy only if necrotic/malignant); oophoropexy if recurrent.
> - Time-critical — don't delay surgery for imaging in a high-suspicion case.

# Pelvic Inflammatory Disease & Tubo-Ovarian Abscess

Ascending infection of the upper female genital tract (endometritis, salpingitis, tubo-ovarian abscess, pelvic peritonitis). It is under-diagnosed and the sequelae are serious — **infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and chronic pelvic pain** rise with each episode and with delayed treatment — so the guiding principle is to **treat empirically on a low diagnostic threshold.**

## 🧬 Microbiology & Pathophysiology

Usually **polymicrobial and sexually transmitted at its core: _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_ and _Chlamydia trachomatis_**, plus anaerobes, Gram-negatives, *Mycoplasma genitalium*, and vaginal flora. Ascension is facilitated during menses and by instrumentation (IUD insertion, TOP). Tubal inflammation → scarring/adhesions (the fertility/ectopic consequences); progression forms a **tubo-ovarian abscess (TOA)**.

## 🩺 Presentation & diagnostic threshold

Lower abdominal/pelvic pain (often bilateral), abnormal discharge, intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding, dyspareunia, fever. **Minimum clinical criteria (CDC) — start empiric treatment in a sexually active woman with pelvic/lower-abdominal pain and ≥ 1 of: cervical-motion tenderness, uterine tenderness, or adnexal tenderness**, once other causes are excluded. **TOA:** a palpable/US adnexal mass with more systemic illness. Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome (perihepatitis) causes RUQ pain.

## 🩻 Investigations

**β-hCG (exclude ectopic — a critical mimic)**, NAAT for gonorrhoea/chlamydia (+ *M. genitalium*/trichomonas), HIV/syphilis screen, urinalysis, CBC/CRP; **transvaginal ultrasound** for TOA/tubal changes. Do not delay treatment for results.

## 💊 Management (CDC 2021 — note metronidazole is now routine)

**1. Outpatient (mild–moderate):** **ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose + doxycycline 100 mg PO bid × 14 days + metronidazole 500 mg PO bid × 14 days.** (Metronidazole added routinely in the 2021 guideline for anaerobic cover.)

**2. Inpatient / parenteral (severe illness, TOA, pregnancy, failed outpatient, unable to tolerate oral, surgical emergency not excluded):** **ceftriaxone 1 g IV q24h + doxycycline 100 mg IV/PO q12h + metronidazole 500 mg IV/PO q12h** (or cefotetan/cefoxitin + doxycycline; clindamycin + gentamicin as an alternative). Transition to oral to complete 14 days.

**3. Tubo-ovarian abscess.** Admit for IV antibiotics with **anaerobic cover**; **image-guided or surgical drainage** for large abscesses (> ~7 cm), no response at 48–72 h, or rupture (a surgical emergency with peritonitis/sepsis).

**4. Ancillary.** **Remove an IUD only if no clinical improvement** after starting antibiotics (do not routinely remove). **Treat sexual partners and advise abstinence**; test/treat for other STIs; counsel on the fertility/ectopic risk. Reassess at 72 h.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

Ceftriaxone, doxycycline, metronidazole, NAAT testing, ultrasound, and drainage/surgery are available; partner notification and STI co-testing (HIV/syphilis) are part of Thai STI care. Rising **gonococcal resistance** makes the ceftriaxone backbone important, and a low treatment threshold protects fertility.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Treat empirically on a LOW threshold** (pelvic pain + cervical-motion/uterine/adnexal tenderness) — delay costs fertility.
> - **Do β-hCG — exclude ectopic** (the dangerous mimic).
> - **Add metronidazole routinely** (CDC 2021) for anaerobes.
> - **TOA → admit, IV antibiotics, and drain** if large/non-responding/ruptured (surgical emergency).
> - **Treat partners + test for other STIs**; don't routinely remove an IUD unless no improvement.

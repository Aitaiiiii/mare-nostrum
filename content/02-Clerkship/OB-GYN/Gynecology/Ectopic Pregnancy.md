# Ectopic Pregnancy

Implantation of a pregnancy outside the uterine cavity (≈ 95% in the fallopian tube) — the leading cause of first-trimester maternal death through **tubal rupture and haemorrhagic shock.** The safety rule is absolute: **a positive pregnancy test + abdominal pain and/or vaginal bleeding in a woman of reproductive age is an ectopic until proven intrauterine.**

## 🧬 Risk factors & Pathophysiology

Anything that impairs tubal transport predisposes: **prior ectopic, PID/tubal damage (chlamydia), tubal surgery/sterilisation, IUD in situ, assisted reproduction, and smoking.** The conceptus erodes the thin tubal wall → bleeding into the tube (haematosalpinx) and, on rupture, into the peritoneum → haemoperitoneum and shock. A pregnancy of unknown location (positive hCG, no intrauterine or extrauterine pregnancy seen) requires active follow-up.

## 🩺 Clinical presentation

Classic triad: **amenorrhoea/missed period + lower abdominal pain + vaginal bleeding**, typically 6–8 weeks. Exam: adnexal tenderness ± mass, cervical motion tenderness. **Rupture → sudden severe pain, peritonism, shoulder-tip pain (diaphragmatic irritation), syncope, and haemorrhagic shock** (may occur with little external bleeding). **A shocked woman of reproductive age with abdominal pain = ruptured ectopic until excluded — do not wait for imaging.**

## 🩻 Investigations

- **Urine/serum β-hCG** — establishes pregnancy; **quantitative serum β-hCG** guides interpretation and trends.
- **Transvaginal ultrasound** — the key test: an intrauterine gestational sac excludes ectopic (except rare heterotopic, higher after IVF); adnexal mass/free fluid supports it.
- **Discriminatory zone:** at β-hCG **~1500–2000 IU/L a normal intrauterine pregnancy should be visible on TVUS**; its absence at that level raises concern for ectopic/non-viable pregnancy. **Serial β-hCG** (a normal IUP rises ≥ ~35–50% in 48 h; a suboptimal rise/plateau suggests ectopic or failing pregnancy).
- CBC, blood group & Rh (anti-D if Rh-negative), crossmatch if unstable.

## 💊 Management

**1. Ruptured / haemodynamically unstable → emergency surgery.** Resuscitate (large-bore access, crystalloid, blood) and go to theatre for **laparoscopy/laparotomy — salpingectomy** (or salpingostomy if fertility preservation and contralateral tube compromised). This is the priority in shock — **do not delay for confirmatory imaging.**

**2. Medical management — methotrexate (selected stable patients).** For a **stable, asymptomatic patient with a small unruptured ectopic**: single-dose **methotrexate 50 mg/m² IM** with β-hCG follow-up (day 4 and 7; expect ≥ 15% fall day 4→7, else repeat/surgery). Best candidates: **β-hCG below ~5000 IU/L, mass < 3.5–4 cm, no fetal cardiac activity, no significant free fluid, able to attend follow-up.** Contraindications: rupture/instability, breastfeeding, immunodeficiency, significant hepatic/renal/haematological disease, active pulmonary/peptic disease. Avoid folic acid and NSAIDs during treatment; counsel on pain and no alcohol/conception until cleared.

**3. Expectant management** — only in carefully selected minimally symptomatic patients with low and falling β-hCG, under close follow-up.

**4. Anti-D** to Rh-negative women, and contraception/fertility counselling; treat any concurrent PID.

## 🇹🇭 Thailand availability

β-hCG assays, transvaginal ultrasound, methotrexate, laparoscopy, blood products, and anti-D immunoglobulin are available in Thai obstetric units. Ectopic is a common Thai gynaecological emergency; a low threshold for **β-hCG in any reproductive-age woman with abdominal pain** (a frequent appendicitis/renal-colic mimic) is the single highest-yield habit.

> [!danger] 🚨 Red flags / strict avoidance
> - **Positive pregnancy test + abdominal pain/bleeding = ectopic until proven intrauterine.**
> - **Shock in a reproductive-age woman with abdominal pain = ruptured ectopic** → resuscitate + theatre; **do not wait for imaging.**
> - **Do β-hCG in every woman of childbearing age with abdominal pain** (mimics appendicitis, renal colic, PID).
> - Methotrexate only in **stable, unruptured, low-hCG, reliable-follow-up** patients; **avoid NSAIDs/folate** during MTX.
> - Give **anti-D** to Rh-negative women.

> [!tip] 🎯 High-Yield Recall
> - Triad: **amenorrhoea + pain + bleeding**; shoulder-tip pain/syncope = rupture.
> - **β-hCG + TVUS**; **discriminatory zone ~1500–2000** (IUP should be seen); serial hCG (< ~35% rise/48 h → suspect ectopic).
> - **Unstable/ruptured → emergency salpingectomy**; **stable small → methotrexate 50 mg/m² IM** (hCG < ~5000, mass < 3.5–4 cm, no FHB).
> - **β-hCG in every reproductive-age woman with abdominal pain**; anti-D if Rh-negative.

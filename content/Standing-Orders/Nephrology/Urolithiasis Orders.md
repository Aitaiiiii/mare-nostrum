# Urolithiasis (Renal Colic) — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **Fever + obstructing stone = obstructed infected kidney → urgent urology + decompression** (do not just give analgesia). Avoid NSAIDs if AKI/bleeding/pregnancy. In an older patient, exclude a **leaking AAA** mimicking colic.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Analgesia — NSAID first-line:** diclofenac 75 mg IM/IV, or ketorolac 30 mg IV (if no AKI/bleeding/pregnancy); add **opioid** (morphine/tramadol) for breakthrough; antiemetic PRN
- [ ] **Low-dose non-contrast CT-KUB** (first-line) — or **US KUB** in pregnancy/young; within 24 h
- [ ] Urinalysis + **urine culture** (nitrite/leukocytes → infection); CBC, electrolytes, **BUN/Cr, calcium, uric acid**; pregnancy test
- [ ] IV fluid for hydration/vomiting; strain urine to catch the stone (send for analysis)
- [ ] **[Fever/sepsis + obstruction, solitary/transplant kidney, bilateral obstruction, or AKI] Urgent urology referral + decompression (ureteric stent or percutaneous nephrostomy) + IV antibiotics** (see [[Urinary Tract Infection & Pyelonephritis]])

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit if: uncontrolled pain, infection + obstruction, AKI, solitary kidney, or large/high stone — otherwise discharge with plan
- [ ] Vital signs; monitor pain, urine output, temperature
- [ ] **Stone ≤ 5 mm** → analgesia + hydration + watchful waiting (most pass)
- [ ] **Stone ≤ 10 mm distal/mid ureter, no infection → Medical Expulsive Therapy: tamsulosin 0.4 mg PO OD up to ~30 days**; strain urine; outpatient follow-up
- [ ] **Stone > 10 mm, failed passage, or intractable pain → urology for URS/ESWL/PCNL**
- [ ] Discharge analgesia (NSAID ± opioid, antiemetic); return precautions (fever, uncontrolled pain, anuria)
- [ ] Recurrent former: high fluid intake (UOP > 2.5 L/day), reduce Na/oxalate/animal protein, **potassium citrate** (Ca/uric-acid stones), **urinary alkalinisation** (uric-acid stone dissolution), thiazide (hypercalciuria), allopurinol (hyperuricosuria)

> [!info]- Why
> NSAIDs beat opioids for colic and reduce rescue analgesia. Non-contrast CT-KUB is the definitive test. Most small stones pass; α-blocker MET speeds passage of distal ureteric stones ≤ 10 mm. The only true emergency is an obstructed infected system, which needs drainage, not stone removal. Verified vs AUA/EAU 2025 + NICE NG118.

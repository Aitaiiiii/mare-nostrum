# HHS — Admission Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **Fluids first; insulin is secondary and given AFTER initial resuscitation, low-rate.** Correct glucose/osmolality **slowly** (cerebral oedema risk). Potassium falls with insulin — replace early, hold insulin if K⁺ < 3.3. Always find the precipitant (infection/MI). VTE prophylaxis (highly thrombogenic).

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] Confirm HHS: **glucose > 600, effective osmolality > 320, pH > 7.3 / HCO₃ > 18, minimal ketosis, ↓consciousness**
- [ ] Labs: electrolytes (**corrected Na⁺, K⁺**), glucose, **calculated osmolality**, BUN/Cr, VBG, ketones (β-OHB), CBC, phosphate/magnesium; **precipitant screen (cultures, CXR, urine, ECG/troponin)**
- [ ] **Fluids first: 0.9% NaCl ~1 L in the first hour**, then titrate (switch to 0.45% NaCl if corrected Na normal/high); replace the large deficit **gradually over 24–48 h**; **add dextrose when glucose ~ 250–300**
- [ ] **Potassium:** replace once K⁺ < 5.0–5.3 and passing urine; **hold insulin if K⁺ < 3.3**
- [ ] **Insulin AFTER fluid resuscitation — low-rate infusion ~0.05 U/kg/h**; aim for a **gradual glucose fall (~50–75 mg/dL/h)** (much of the fall comes from rehydration alone)
- [ ] Strict I/O + urinary catheter; **VTE prophylaxis**

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit (ICU if unstable/↓consciousness) — Dx: HHS (precipitant ___)
- [ ] Hourly glucose; electrolytes/osmolality q2–4h; **monitor for cerebral oedema (avoid rapid osmolar shifts)**
- [ ] Continue insulin + dextrose fluids until hyperosmolar state resolves + mentation clears, then transition to SC insulin (overlap)
- [ ] Treat the precipitant (antibiotics for infection); diabetes education; comorbidity management
- [ ] Watch for hypokalaemia, hypoglycaemia, fluid overload, thrombosis

> [!info]- Why
> HHS is dominated by profound dehydration and hyperosmolality (enough insulin to block ketosis), so fluid resuscitation is the priority and insulin is low-dose and secondary; rapid correction risks cerebral oedema. Mortality exceeds DKA (elderly, comorbid), and the precipitant (usually infection) drives it. Verified vs ADA hyperglycaemic-crisis framework.

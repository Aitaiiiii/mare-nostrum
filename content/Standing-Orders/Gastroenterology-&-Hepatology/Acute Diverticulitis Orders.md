# Acute Diverticulitis — Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **CT separates uncomplicated from complicated.** Uncomplicated often needs no/oral antibiotics + outpatient. **Peritonitis/perforation → emergency surgery; abscess → drainage.** Colonoscopy at ~6 weeks (not acutely).

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast** (confirm + stage: uncomplicated vs abscess/perforation/obstruction; Hinchey) — consider right-sided diverticulitis (Asian, appendicitis mimic)
- [ ] Labs: CBC, **CRP**, U&E, lactate; urinalysis (fistula/UTI)
- [ ] **[Uncomplicated, well, tolerating oral] no/oral antibiotics + outpatient** (amoxicillin-clavulanate, or ciprofloxacin + metronidazole when used); analgesia; oral intake as tolerated; follow-up
- [ ] Admit if: unable to tolerate oral, significant comorbidity/immunosuppression, sepsis, or failure to improve
- [ ] **[Abscess] < ~3–4 cm → IV antibiotics; larger → image-guided percutaneous drainage + IV antibiotics**
- [ ] **[Perforation/peritonitis, Hinchey III–IV] resuscitate + broad IV antibiotics + emergency surgery** (Hartmann's / selective primary anastomosis)

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit or discharge per severity — Dx: acute diverticulitis (uncomplicated/complicated, Hinchey ___)
- [ ] Monitor response (pain, CRP, temperature); escalate if not improving
- [ ] Diet advance as tolerated; avoid acute colonoscopy
- [ ] **Colonoscopy at ~6 weeks** after complicated/first episode (exclude malignancy); dietary fibre; discuss elective resection for recurrent/complicated disease

> [!info]- Why
> CT staging drives everything: uncomplicated disease is often self-limiting and antibiotic-sparing (DINAMO), abscesses are drained, and free perforation needs surgery. Acute colonoscopy risks perforation, so it is deferred to exclude a masquerading cancer at 6 weeks. Verified vs WSES/ACPGBI + DINAMO.

# Acute Liver Failure — ICU Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **Cerebral oedema is the leading killer.** **Do NOT correct coagulopathy unless bleeding/procedure** (INR is prognostic). **NAC for all-cause ALF.** Refer to a transplant centre EARLY.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **N-acetylcysteine IV** (all-cause ALF — not just paracetamol)
- [ ] Labs: **INR/PT (do not reflexively correct)**, LFTs, bilirubin, ammonia, **glucose (frequent)**, lactate, U&E/Cr, ABG, CBC, phosphate; **paracetamol level**
- [ ] Aetiology: viral serology (anti-HAV IgM, HBsAg/anti-HBc IgM, anti-HEV, HSV), autoimmune markers, **caeruloplasmin/copper (Wilson's)**, pregnancy test, toxicology, US/Doppler (Budd-Chiari)
- [ ] **Cerebral oedema care:** head-up 30°, minimise stimulation, treat fever/hyponatraemia; **grade III–IV enceph → intubate + ICU**; raised ICP → **hypertonic saline (Na 145–155, < 160) or mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg; hyperventilate PaCO₂ 25–30** for surges
- [ ] **Frequent glucose + dextrose infusion**; correct electrolytes/phosphate; vitamin K; **PPI**
- [ ] Low threshold for **empiric antibiotics/antifungals** (sepsis surveillance, cultures); aciclovir if HSV
- [ ] **Contact transplant centre early**; apply **King's College criteria**

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit ICU — Dx: acute liver failure (cause ___, encephalopathy grade ___)
- [ ] Neuro obs + ICP surveillance; serial INR, glucose, lactate, renal function, ammonia trend
- [ ] **CRRT** for renal failure/volume/ammonia; haemodynamic support (vasopressors)
- [ ] **Correct coagulopathy ONLY for active bleeding or before an invasive procedure**
- [ ] Cause-specific therapy (antivirals, chelation, steroids for autoimmune, delivery for pregnancy-related); transplant listing per criteria

> [!info]- Why
> ALF kills through cerebral oedema and multi-organ failure; NAC helps all causes, and aggressive ICP management bridges to recovery or transplant. The INR is the key prognostic signal, so it is not blindly corrected. Early transplant-centre contact saves lives. Verified vs AASLD/King's College guidance.

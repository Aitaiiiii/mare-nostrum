# Hepatic Encephalopathy — Admission Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **FIND AND TREAT THE PRECIPITANT** (tap the ascites for SBP, look for a GI bleed, check K⁺, stop sedatives). **Ammonia does not guide therapy.** **Do NOT protein-restrict.** Grade III–IV → protect the airway.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Hunt the precipitant:** **diagnostic paracentesis (SBP)**, septic screen (cultures, CXR, urine), assess for **GI/variceal bleeding**, review sedatives
- [ ] Labs: CBC, **electrolytes (K⁺, Na⁺)**, renal/liver function, **glucose** (exclude hypoglycaemia), coagulation
- [ ] **Correct precipitants:** antibiotics for infection/SBP, control bleeding, relieve constipation, **correct hypokalaemia/hyponatraemia/dehydration**, stop sedatives/benzodiazepines
- [ ] **Lactulose 30–45 mL PO/NG q1–2h until bowel movement, then titrate to 2–3 soft stools/day** (retention enema 300 mL in 700 mL water if grade III–IV / no oral)
- [ ] **Rifaximin 550 mg PO bid** (add for recurrence/inadequate response)
- [ ] **Grade III–IV → intubate/ICU** (airway/aspiration)
- [ ] Exclude other causes of altered mentation (CT head if focal/trauma/not responding; consider Wernicke → thiamine)

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit — Dx: hepatic encephalopathy (grade ___, precipitant ___)
- [ ] Titrate lactulose to 2–3 soft stools/day (avoid over-purging → dehydration/hypokalaemia rebound)
- [ ] **Maintain nutrition — do NOT protein-restrict**; adequate calories
- [ ] Serial mental status, electrolytes; treat ongoing precipitant; manage cirrhosis complications
- [ ] Recurrent HE → rifaximin secondary prevention, consider shunt evaluation, **transplant assessment**

> [!info]- Why
> Most HE episodes are triggered — correcting the precipitant (especially SBP and GI bleeding) is what resolves it; lactulose traps colonic ammonia and rifaximin reduces recurrence. The ammonia level is unreliable, and protein restriction worsens sarcopenia/outcomes. Verified vs AASLD/EASL HE guidance.

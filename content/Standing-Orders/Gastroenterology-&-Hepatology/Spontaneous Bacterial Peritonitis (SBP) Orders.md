# Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis — Admission Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local antibiogram and the attending. **Tap EVERY cirrhotic with ascites who is admitted or deteriorating** (SBP is often silent). **Treat on ascitic PMN ≥ 250** (don't wait for culture). **Give albumin** — it prevents hepatorenal syndrome.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Diagnostic paracentesis** — ascitic **cell count + differential**, culture (inoculate blood-culture bottles bedside), albumin/protein, glucose, LDH
- [ ] **Diagnosis: ascitic PMN ≥ 250/mm³** → treat immediately
- [ ] **Empiric antibiotics: Cefotaxime 2 g IV q8h (or Ceftriaxone 2 g IV OD)** — broaden (pip-tazo/carbapenem) if nosocomial/ESBL risk; course 5–7 days
- [ ] **IV albumin 1.5 g/kg on day 1** (and 1 g/kg on day 3) — prevents HRS
- [ ] Rule out **secondary peritonitis** (PMN very high/polymicrobial, or ≥ 2 of: ascitic protein > 1 g/dL, glucose < 50, LDH > serum ULN → imaging/surgery)
- [ ] Bloods: CBC, **renal function (HRS baseline)**, LFTs, coagulation, blood cultures; screen for GI bleed
- [ ] **Withhold nephrotoxins**; hold non-selective β-blocker in the acute/unstable phase

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit — Dx: SBP (ascitic PMN ___)
- [ ] Repeat paracentesis at 48 h if not improving (expect ≥ 25% PMN fall); monitor renal function (HRS)
- [ ] Manage concurrent decompensation ([[Hepatic Encephalopathy]], variceal bleed)
- [ ] **Start secondary prophylaxis on recovery — norfloxacin (or ciprofloxacin/co-trimoxazole)**; transplant assessment
- [ ] Primary prophylaxis in high-risk (ascitic protein < 1.5 g/dL + organ dysfunction; 7-day course during variceal bleed)

> [!info]- Why
> SBP is frequently silent and lethal, so a low-threshold tap and treatment on the PMN count (cultures are often negative) are essential; IV albumin blunts the renal vasoconstriction that causes hepatorenal syndrome and reduces mortality. Secondary peritonitis needs surgery, not just antibiotics. Verified vs AASLD/EASL SBP guidance.

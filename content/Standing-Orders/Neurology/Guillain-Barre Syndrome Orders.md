# Guillain-Barré Syndrome — Admission Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **Monitor FVC serially and intubate early on trajectory** (don't wait for arrest). **Autonomic instability → continuous cardiac monitoring.** Steroids do NOT work — use IVIG or PLEX, not both. Exclude mimics (hypokalaemia, snakebite, botulism, myasthenia).

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Serial forced vital capacity (FVC)** + single-breath count; **20/30/40 rule** (FVC < 20 mL/kg, MIP < 30, MEP < 40) as an intubation guide — decide on trajectory
- [ ] Continuous **cardiac monitoring + BP** (autonomic instability); O₂; assess bulbar/swallow (aspiration → NPO if unsafe)
- [ ] LP (**albuminocytological dissociation** — may be normal in week 1); nerve conduction studies; consider anti-GQ1b (Miller Fisher)
- [ ] Bloods: electrolytes (exclude **hypokalaemia/periodic paralysis**), glucose, renal/liver; ECG
- [ ] **Immunotherapy — choose ONE:** **IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day IV × 5 days** OR **plasma exchange ~200–250 mL/kg over 5 sessions** (do NOT combine; **no corticosteroids**)
- [ ] Neurology + ICU referral if progressing/respiratory-autonomic compromise
- [ ] **Avoid suxamethonium** if intubating (hyperkalaemia)

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit (ICU/HDU if progressing, bulbar, or FVC falling) — Dx: GBS (variant ___)
- [ ] **FVC q4–6h** while progressing; continuous cardiac monitoring; serial neuro exam
- [ ] Autonomic care: treat arrhythmias, **use vasoactive drugs cautiously** (exaggerated responses); watch ileus/retention
- [ ] **VTE prophylaxis**; neuropathic pain control; bowel/bladder care; pressure-area care; physiotherapy; nutrition; psychological support
- [ ] Monitor for treatment-related fluctuation; rehabilitation planning

> [!info]- Why
> GBS is reversible but kills via respiratory and autonomic failure, so serial FVC (elective early intubation) and cardiac monitoring are the life-savers; IVIG and PLEX are equally effective and hasten recovery, while steroids are ineffective. Autonomic lability makes vasoactive drugs risky. Verified vs GBS management evidence.

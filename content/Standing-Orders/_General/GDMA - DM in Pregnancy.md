# GDM / Diabetes in Pregnancy — Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local/Thai obstetric protocol and the attending. **Insulin is first-line pharmacotherapy in pregnancy** (glycaemic targets tighter than non-pregnant). Screen for + monitor pre-eclampsia, macrosomia, and polyhydramnios. Distinguish **GDMA1 (diet-controlled) / GDMA2 (needs insulin) / overt (pre-gestational) DM**.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] Classify: **GDMA1** (diet) vs **GDMA2** (medication) vs **overt/pre-gestational DM**; screen (24–28 wk: 50 g GCT → 100 g OGTT) — earlier if high risk
- [ ] **Glycaemic targets: fasting < 95, 1-h post-prandial < 140, 2-h < 120 mg/dL**; capillary glucose profile (fasting + post-meals)
- [ ] **Medical nutrition therapy + exercise first** (GDMA1); HbA1c, renal function, retinal review for overt DM
- [ ] **If targets not met → insulin (first-line)**: basal ± prandial, titrated (metformin an adjunct/alternative where used — counsel; insulin preferred) — see [[Insulin IPD]]
- [ ] Baseline: BP, urine protein (pre-eclampsia risk ↑); growth US

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Home glucose monitoring + regular review; titrate insulin to targets
- [ ] **Fetal surveillance:** serial growth US (macrosomia/IUGR), amniotic fluid; **NST/BPP in the third trimester** for GDMA2/overt DM/poor control
- [ ] Monitor for **pre-eclampsia** ([[Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia]]) and polyhydramnios
- [ ] **Timing of delivery:** well-controlled GDMA1 → ~39–40+ wk; GDMA2/overt or complications → ~37–39 wk (individualised); anticipate shoulder dystocia with macrosomia
- [ ] **Intrapartum: tight glucose control** (insulin infusion if needed); **postpartum: stop GDM insulin, screen with 75 g OGTT at 6–12 weeks** (future T2DM risk) — see [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]]

## 🔵 Peri-operative / intrapartum glucose
- [ ] Use a **variable-rate insulin infusion + dextrose** for labour/C-S in insulin-treated diabetes (target ~70–120 mg/dL intrapartum) — see [[Peri-op Insulin Infusion]]

> [!info]- Why
> Maternal hyperglycaemia causes macrosomia, birth trauma, neonatal hypoglycaemia, and stillbirth, so tight glycaemic control (insulin first-line in pregnancy), fetal surveillance, and appropriately-timed delivery reduce harm; postpartum OGTT catches persistent diabetes. Verified vs ADA/ACOG diabetes-in-pregnancy + Thai practice.

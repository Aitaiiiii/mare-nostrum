# Testicular Torsion — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **Sudden testicular pain = torsion until excluded → emergency exploration; do NOT wait for ultrasound.** ~6-hour salvage window. Always examine the scrotum in a boy with abdominal/groin pain.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Immediate urology / surgery referral for emergency scrotal exploration** (do not delay for imaging when suspicion high)
- [ ] **NPO** from presentation; IV access; analgesia (opioid) + antiemetic
- [ ] **Attempt manual detorsion as a bridge** (usually medial-to-lateral "open book"; guide by pain relief/flow) — still needs surgery
- [ ] Scrotal **colour-Doppler US ONLY if diagnosis genuinely uncertain and immediately available** (normal flow does not exclude early torsion)
- [ ] Urinalysis (pyuria suggests epididymitis); baseline bloods + group & save (pre-op)
- [ ] Consent/prepare for theatre: **detorsion + bilateral orchidopexy** (orchidectomy if non-viable)

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit surgery/urology — Dx: testicular torsion (side ___), for emergency exploration
- [ ] Post-op: analgesia, wound care, scrotal support; monitor viability
- [ ] Counsel on outcome (salvage depends on ischaemic time), fertility, and that both testes were fixed
- [ ] Follow-up urology

> [!info]- Why
> The testis infarcts within hours, so salvage depends on speed — exploration proceeds on clinical suspicion because a normal Doppler does not exclude early/intermittent torsion. Manual detorsion can restore flow temporarily but definitive orchidopexy (of both testes, given the bilateral bell-clapper anomaly) is required. NPO keeps the patient theatre-ready. Verified vs standard urological practice.

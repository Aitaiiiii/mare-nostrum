# Anaphylaxis — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **IM adrenaline is first-line — give immediately, do NOT delay for antihistamines/steroids or to confirm the diagnosis.** Keep a hypotensive patient **supine with legs raised** (never sit/stand up). Absence of urticaria does not exclude anaphylaxis.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Adrenaline 0.5 mg (0.01 mg/kg, max 0.5 mg) IM anterolateral thigh (1 mg/mL / 1:1000) STAT — repeat q5–15 min PRN**
- [ ] Remove/stop the trigger (stop infusion/contrast; remove stinger)
- [ ] **Position supine, legs elevated** (left-lateral if pregnant/vomiting)
- [ ] High-flow **O₂**; monitor airway continuously — **prepare early intubation if stridor/tongue/pharyngeal swelling**
- [ ] **2 large-bore IV lines; crystalloid bolus 1–2 L (10–20 mL/kg), repeat to BP response**
- [ ] Continuous monitor: BP, HR, SpO₂, ECG
- [ ] **[Refractory ≥ 2 IM doses] IV adrenaline infusion titrated** (monitored setting; no ward IV push)
- [ ] **[On β-blocker, poor response] Glucagon 1–5 mg IV, then infusion**
- [ ] **[Persistent bronchospasm] Salbutamol NEB**
- [ ] Adjuncts (AFTER adrenaline, second-line): **Chlorpheniramine 10 mg IV** (cutaneous only); **Hydrocortisone 100–200 mg IV** case-by-case (NOT routine for biphasic prevention)
- [ ] **Serum tryptase** 1–3 h from onset (baseline later) — do not delay treatment for it

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Observe for **biphasic reaction** — individualise: ~1–2 h if mild/single-dose/promptly treated; **6–12+ h or admit** if severe, ≥ 2 adrenaline doses, airway involvement, poor access, or prior biphasic
- [ ] Vitals + SpO₂ per severity; re-treat with IM adrenaline if recurrence
- [ ] Document trigger; review/hold β-blocker & ACE-inhibitor where feasible
- [ ] **Discharge: prescribe TWO adrenaline autoinjectors OR pre-filled adrenaline (Thailand — confirm obtainable device) + train use; written anaphylaxis action plan; trigger avoidance; refer allergist/immunologist**

> [!info]- Why
> Early IM adrenaline is the only intervention shown to reduce anaphylaxis mortality; every other drug is secondary. Upright posture in shock can cause empty-ventricle cardiac arrest. Steroids/antihistamines relieve skin symptoms and do not reliably prevent biphasic reactions, so they never precede adrenaline. β-blockade blunts adrenaline → glucagon. Verified vs 2023 JTFPP/AAAAI practice parameter + WAO.

# Acute Pain Control — Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. Use the **WHO analgesic ladder + multimodal, opioid-sparing** approach. **NSAIDs: avoid in AKI/CKD, GI bleed, heart failure, elderly, anticoagulation.** Opioids: start low, monitor sedation/respiration (naloxone available), watch renal accumulation (morphine metabolites). Do not mask an undiagnosed acute abdomen from surgery — but analgesia does NOT delay/obscure surgical diagnosis.

## 🔴 One Day — multimodal, stepwise
- [ ] **Assess pain** (score, type — nociceptive/neuropathic/visceral, cause); treat the cause
- [ ] **Step 1 — non-opioid base:** **Paracetamol 1 g PO/IV q6h (max 4 g/day; ≤ 3 g if frail/hepatic/low weight)** ± **NSAID** (e.g. ibuprofen/naproxen — only if no renal/GI/cardiac/bleeding contraindication)
- [ ] **Step 2 — mild-moderate:** add **weak opioid (tramadol 50 mg q6h PRN — caution: seizures/serotonin, elderly)** or low-dose strong opioid
- [ ] **Step 3 — moderate-severe/acute:** **morphine 2–5 mg IV titrated q5–15 min to effect** (or oral morphine), with antiemetic + laxative; PCA for ongoing severe pain
- [ ] **Adjuncts:** neuropathic pain → gabapentinoid/amitriptyline/duloxetine; regional/local anaesthesia/nerve block where appropriate; renal colic → NSAID first-line ([[Urolithiasis (Renal Colic)]])
- [ ] Monitor: pain score response, **sedation + respiratory rate** on opioids; bowel function

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Regular scheduled base analgesia + PRN breakthrough; **reassess + step down** as pain improves; avoid PRN-only for constant pain
- [ ] Opioid safety: laxative co-prescribed, antiemetic PRN, renal dose-adjust (avoid morphine in renal failure → use fentanyl/oxycodone), reconcile with home opioids
- [ ] Bowel/GI protection with NSAIDs (PPI if needed); minimise duration; deprescribe opioids at discharge (short course + plan)

> [!info]- Why
> Multimodal, opioid-sparing analgesia (paracetamol ± NSAID base + opioid only as needed) controls pain with fewer opioid harms; NSAIDs are limited by renal/GI/cardiac/bleeding risk and morphine accumulates in renal failure. Titrated IV opioids with sedation/respiratory monitoring are safe for severe acute pain. Verified vs WHO ladder / acute-pain standards.

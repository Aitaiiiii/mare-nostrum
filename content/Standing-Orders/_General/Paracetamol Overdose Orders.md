# Paracetamol Overdose — Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol / poison centre (1367). **NAC is ~100% hepatoprotective within 8 h** — **start it empirically if presenting > 8 h / staggered / unknown time** (don't wait for the level). Plot a **timed (≥ 4 h) level on the Rumack–Matthew nomogram**. Normal early LFTs do NOT exclude toxicity. **Do NOT stop NAC automatically at 21 h** — reassess ALT/INR/level.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Serum paracetamol level at 4 h post-ingestion (or on arrival if later; not before 4 h)** → plot on Rumack–Matthew (treatment line 150 µg/mL at 4 h)
- [ ] Baseline **ALT/AST, INR/PT, bilirubin, U&E/creatinine, venous gas/lactate, glucose**; **salicylate level + coingestant screen**
- [ ] **Activated charcoal** if within ~1–2 h of a significant ingestion + airway protected
- [ ] **N-acetylcysteine (NAC) — start if:** level above the nomogram line · **presenting > 8 h after significant ingestion (start empirically now)** · staggered/unknown-time ingestion · any evidence of hepatotoxicity (↑ALT/INR)
- [ ] **NAC IV 21-hour regimen: 150 mg/kg over 1 h → 50 mg/kg over 4 h → 100 mg/kg over 16 h** (total 300 mg/kg); watch for anaphylactoid reaction (slow/pause infusion + antihistamine)

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit (or observe) — Dx: paracetamol overdose (timing ___)
- [ ] **At end of NAC course, re-check ALT/INR/paracetamol level — CONTINUE NAC** if ALT rising, INR elevated, or level still detectable (do not auto-stop at 21 h)
- [ ] Trend INR, ALT, lactate, glucose, pH, creatinine; treat hypoglycaemia/coagulopathy
- [ ] **Refer early to a liver-transplant centre using King's College criteria** (arterial pH < 7.3, or INR > 6.5 + creatinine > 3.4 mg/dL + grade III–IV encephalopathy) — see [[Acute Liver Failure]]
- [ ] **Psychiatric/risk assessment** (deliberate overdose)

> [!info]- Why
> NAC replenishes glutathione to detoxify NAPQI and is near-completely protective when started early, so it is given empirically whenever timing is uncertain or presentation is late rather than waiting for the level; the nomogram guides single-timepoint acute ingestions. Fixed 21-h stopping is a classic error — continue if the liver is still deteriorating. Verified vs standard paracetamol-toxicity management.

# Acute Limb Ischemia — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **Heparinise immediately + call vascular/IR — do not wait for imaging.** Threatened limb (Rutherford IIa/IIb) → revascularise < 6 h. Category III (fixed mottling/paralysis/rigor) → amputation, NOT revascularisation.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **IV unfractionated heparin 70–100 U/kg bolus, then infusion** (target aPTT) — STAT on diagnosis
- [ ] **Hand-held Doppler** (arterial + venous signals both legs) → classify **Rutherford I/IIa/IIb/III**
- [ ] **Urgent vascular surgery / interventional radiology referral**
- [ ] **CT angiography** (level/anatomy/planning) — do not delay heparin or referral
- [ ] Labs: CBC, coagulation, **U&E, creatinine, K⁺, CK, lactate**, group & save; ECG (AF/MI)
- [ ] Analgesia (opioid); IV fluids; O₂; keep limb **dependent + warm** (no active heating/ice)
- [ ] Disposition by category: **IIa → revascularise < 6 h · IIb → emergency (now) · I → within 24 h · III → primary amputation**
- [ ] Revascularisation options (vascular-led): Fogarty embolectomy · bypass · catheter-directed thrombolysis · mechanical/aspiration thrombectomy · angioplasty/stent

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit vascular/surgery (or transfer) — Dx: ALI, Rutherford ___, ?embolic/?thrombotic
- [ ] Continue heparin infusion; serial neurovascular limb checks (Doppler, sensory/motor, compartments)
- [ ] **Post-revascularisation: monitor for compartment syndrome → fasciotomy**; treat **hyperkalaemia, rhabdomyolysis/AKI** (CK, K⁺, UOP, urine myoglobin), metabolic acidosis
- [ ] Identify + treat source: echo (AF/mural thrombus), and **long-term anticoagulation for cardioembolic ALI** (see [[Atrial Fibrillation]], [[Anticoagulant Therapy]]); antiplatelet/statin + PAD workup for atherothrombotic
- [ ] Investigate aneurysm/hypercoagulable state as indicated

> [!info]- Why
> Muscle/nerve infarct within ~6 h, so heparin (limits propagation) + rapid revascularisation preserve the limb. Doppler + Rutherford triage decides who goes to theatre now vs who is already unsalvageable (category III → reperfusing a dead limb causes fatal hyperkalaemia/reperfusion injury). Fasciotomy and metabolic monitoring manage reperfusion. Verified vs ESVS 2020 ALI.

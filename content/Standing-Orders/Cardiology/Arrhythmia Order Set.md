# Acute Arrhythmia — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **The first question is always STABLE vs UNSTABLE** (hypotension, ischaemia/chest pain, acute heart failure, altered mentation). **Unstable tachyarrhythmia → immediate synchronised cardioversion; unstable bradycardia → atropine → pacing.** 12-lead ECG + continuous monitoring + defibrillator/pads on every patient.

## 🔴 One Day — initial assessment (all rhythms)
- [ ] **12-lead ECG + continuous monitoring**; defibrillator/pacing pads at bedside; IV access; O₂ if hypoxic
- [ ] Assess **stability**: BP, chest pain/ischaemia, acute HF/pulmonary oedema, syncope/altered mentation
- [ ] Labs: electrolytes (**K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺**), TFT, troponin if ischaemia, renal function, digoxin level if relevant; treat reversible triggers (electrolytes, hypoxia, sepsis, ischaemia, drugs)

## 🔵 Rhythm-specific management

**Unstable (any tachyarrhythmia) → synchronised DC cardioversion** (sedation if conscious); **pulseless VT/VF → defibrillate + [[CPR - ACLS|ACLS]].**

**Narrow-complex regular (SVT — AVNRT/AVRT/AT):**
- [ ] **Vagal manoeuvres** (modified Valsalva)
- [ ] **Adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push + flush; if no response 12 mg (repeat 12 mg once)** (warn re: transient chest discomfort/flushing; caution in asthma/pre-excited AF)
- [ ] If unresponsive/recurrent: IV diltiazem/verapamil or β-blocker (avoid in HFrEF/hypotension); → [[SVT (AVNRT, AVRT, Focal AT)]]

**Atrial fibrillation / flutter:**
- [ ] **Rate control: β-blocker (metoprolol 2.5–5 mg IV) or diltiazem** (avoid verapamil/diltiazem in HFrEF → use β-blocker/digoxin/amiodarone)
- [ ] **Rhythm control** (symptomatic, AF < 48 h, or unstable): DC cardioversion or amiodarone; **anticoagulate** per onset/CHA₂DS₂-VASc + TOE for AF ≥ 48 h/unknown before cardioversion
- [ ] **Anticoagulation (DOAC/warfarin) by CHA₂DS₂-VASc**; **pre-excited AF (WPW): AVOID AV-nodal blockers (adenosine/β-blocker/CCB/digoxin) → procainamide or DC cardioversion** — see [[WPW Syndrome]]
- [ ] → [[Atrial Fibrillation]] (+ Thai AF 2025)

**Wide-complex tachycardia (assume VT until proven otherwise):**
- [ ] Unstable → **synchronised cardioversion**; stable monomorphic VT → **amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min** (or procainamide) then infusion; **polymorphic VT/torsades → IV magnesium 2 g**, correct K⁺/QT, defibrillate if pulseless → [[VT & Wide-Complex Tachycardia]]

**Bradycardia / AV block (symptomatic):**
- [ ] **Atropine 1 mg IV, repeat q3–5 min (max 3 mg)**; if refractory → **transcutaneous pacing** and/or **isoprenaline/adrenaline or dopamine infusion**; treat cause (drugs, ischaemia, hyperkalaemia, hypothyroid) → [[Bradycardia & AV Block]]; transvenous/permanent pacing per indication ([[Device Indications (PPM, ICD, CRT)]])

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit/telemetry per severity — Dx: arrhythmia (type ___)
- [ ] Continuous monitoring; correct + maintain electrolytes (K⁺ > 4, Mg²⁺ > 1); treat underlying cause
- [ ] Anticoagulation decision (AF/flutter); rhythm/rate optimisation; cardiology/EP referral (ablation, device)
- [ ] Review QT-prolonging/pro-arrhythmic drugs

> [!info]- Why
> Stability decides the first move — unstable rhythms are electrically treated (cardioversion/pacing) regardless of the label. Adenosine both treats and unmasks SVT; AV-nodal blockers are dangerous in pre-excited AF; wide-complex tachycardia is VT until proven otherwise; torsades needs magnesium + QT correction. Electrolytes/ischaemia are the common reversible drivers. Verified vs ACLS + ESC/AHA arrhythmia + Thai AF 2025.

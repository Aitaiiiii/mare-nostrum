# SCAPE / Flash Pulmonary Edema — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol and the attending. **NIV + high-dose IV nitrate FIRST** (afterload/redistribution physiology). **Do NOT lead with large-dose diuretics** (most are euvolaemic → later hypotension/AKI). No fluid boluses. Find + treat the trigger (ACS/hypertension).

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Non-invasive ventilation (CPAP/BiPAP) immediately** + high-flow O₂ (reduces intubation/mortality)
- [ ] **Sublingual GTN while access set up, then high-dose IV nitroglycerin infusion, rapidly up-titrated** to afterload reduction (continuous BP monitoring); nitroprusside as alternative
- [ ] Continuous SpO₂/ECG/BP; **ECG + troponin** (ACS trigger), CXR, venous/arterial gas, U&E; **POCUS** (B-lines, LV, IVC)
- [ ] **Diuretic SECONDARY** — give once crisis controlled + only if genuine volume overload (avoid over-diuresing a euvolaemic patient)
- [ ] **Treat the trigger**: ACS → [[STEMI]]/[[NSTE-ACS (NSTEMI & Unstable Angina)]] pathway; arrhythmia; severe hypertension
- [ ] **No fluid boluses**; avoid negative inotropes acutely
- [ ] Intubate if NIV fails/exhaustion/↓consciousness

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit monitored/ICU — Dx: SCAPE / acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema (trigger ___)
- [ ] Wean nitrate + NIV as it resolves (usually fast); reassess volume status for cautious diuresis
- [ ] Continue treating the precipitant; optimise chronic HF/BP therapy ([[ADHF]], [[Heart Failure]])
- [ ] Persistent low output/shock → reassess for **cardiogenic shock** (inotropes/MCS — different pathway)

> [!info]- Why
> SCAPE is an afterload/redistribution crisis in a usually-euvolaemic patient, so NIV (reduces work of breathing + preload/afterload) and high-dose nitrate (drops afterload) reverse it quickly, while large-dose diuretics target a total-body overload that isn't there and cause later hypotension/AKI. Treating the trigger (often ischaemia or severe hypertension) prevents recurrence. Verified vs acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema evidence.

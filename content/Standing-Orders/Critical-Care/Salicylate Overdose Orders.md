# Salicylate Overdose — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol / poison centre (1367). **AVOID intubation if at all possible** (acidaemia can be fatal). Keep serum alkalotic. Replete K⁺ (needed to alkalinise urine). Give glucose even if serum glucose normal. Dialyse early for severe features.

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Serial serum salicylate levels (q2h until falling)** + **paracetamol level**; ABG/VBG + anion gap, electrolytes (**K⁺**), glucose, renal function, lactate, coagulation
- [ ] IV fluid resuscitation (euvolaemia); **dextrose (even if glucose normal, esp. if AMS)**; **aggressive KCl repletion**
- [ ] **Sodium bicarbonate** bolus then infusion (e.g. 3 amps NaHCO₃ in 1 L D5W) → **serum + urine alkalinisation, target urine pH ~7.5–8**; monitor K⁺ + urine pH
- [ ] Activated charcoal if early/ongoing absorption (repeat doses for enteric-coated); airway protected
- [ ] **Nephrology — haemodialysis** for level **> ~90 (acute)/> 60 (chronic-symptomatic)**, AMS/seizures, pulmonary/cerebral oedema, renal failure, or refractory acidosis
- [ ] **Do NOT intubate unless unavoidable** — if required: match high minute ventilation, bolus bicarbonate peri-intubation, dialysis ready, senior-led

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit monitored/ICU — Dx: salicylate toxicity (acute/chronic)
- [ ] Continue bicarbonate infusion to urine pH ~8; **serial levels + K⁺ + urine pH + gases** until levels fall and symptoms resolve
- [ ] Treat hyperthermia; monitor for pulmonary/cerebral oedema
- [ ] Psychiatric/risk assessment (deliberate overdose)

> [!info]- Why
> Alkalinisation traps ionised salicylate in urine (clearance ×10–20) and keeps it out of the CNS; hypokalaemia defeats urine alkalinisation. Levels rise with ongoing absorption, so trend them. Intubation drops the compensatory hyperventilation and can precipitate fatal acidaemia; dialysis is the definitive removal for severe toxicity. Verified vs salicylate-toxicity guidance.

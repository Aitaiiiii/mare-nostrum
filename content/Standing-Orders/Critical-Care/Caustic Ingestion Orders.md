# Caustic Ingestion — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol / poison centre (1367). **Airway first (early fibreoptic intubation for stridor/oedema).** **Do NOT induce vomiting, lavage, give charcoal, or neutralise.** No oral burns does not exclude serious injury. Suspected perforation → CT + surgery (endoscopy contraindicated).

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Airway assessment — early fibreoptic intubation if stridor/hoarseness/supraglottic oedema**; high-flow O₂
- [ ] **NPO**; IV fluids; analgesia; PPI IV; antiemetic (do NOT induce vomiting)
- [ ] **AVOID: induced emesis, gastric lavage/blind NG, activated charcoal, neutralising/dilution agents**
- [ ] Labs: CBC, venous gas/lactate, renal/liver, coagulation, group & save; **paracetamol + salicylate** if deliberate
- [ ] CXR (free/mediastinal air); **CT chest/abdomen** if severe/unstable or perforation suspected
- [ ] **Upper GI endoscopy at 12–48 h** to grade (**Zargar 0–4**) — CONTRAINDICATED if perforation, severe supraglottic oedema, or unstable
- [ ] **Perforation / grade 3b–4 / transmural necrosis → emergency surgery** (oesophagogastrectomy)
- [ ] Steroids NOT routine

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit (ICU if airway/high-grade/unstable) — Dx: caustic ingestion (acid/alkali, Zargar ___)
- [ ] Manage by grade: **low (0–2a)** → supportive + early oral feeding; **high (2b–3)** → cautious nutrition (NJ/parenteral), ICU, surveillance
- [ ] Monitor for perforation/sepsis, bleeding, mediastinitis/peritonitis
- [ ] **Follow for strictures** (weeks → dysphagia → endoscopic dilatation); counsel long-term **oesophageal cancer** risk
- [ ] Psychiatric assessment (deliberate); child-safety/public-health measures (accidental)

> [!info]- Why
> Emesis, lavage, charcoal, and neutralisation all re-expose or worsen the burn, so they are contraindicated; the airway swells and must be secured early. Endoscopy at 12–48 h grades injury to guide feeding vs surgery, while perforation mandates CT + operation. Verified vs corrosive-ingestion guidance.

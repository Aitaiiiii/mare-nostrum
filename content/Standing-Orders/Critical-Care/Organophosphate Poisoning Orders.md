# Organophosphate Poisoning — ER Orders

> [!danger] ⚠️ SAFETY
> Reference only; adjust to local protocol, the attending, and **Ramathibodi Poison Center 1367**. **Staff PPE + skin/clothing decontamination** (ongoing dermal absorption). **Do NOT under-dose atropine** — titrate to atropinisation. **Avoid suxamethonium** (prolonged paralysis). Anticipate the **intermediate syndrome** (day 1–4 respiratory failure).

## 🔴 One Day
- [ ] **Airway + high-flow O₂ + suction copious secretions**; intubate for respiratory failure/coma (use non-depolarising blocker, NOT suxamethonium)
- [ ] **Decontaminate:** remove clothing, wash skin/hair; staff wear PPE (gloves/gown/eye protection)
- [ ] Continuous cardiac + SpO₂ monitor; IV access; ECG
- [ ] **Atropine 2–5 mg IV STAT, then DOUBLE the dose every 5 min until atropinised** — endpoints: **clear chest (dried secretions), HR > 80, SBP > 80, no bronchospasm**
- [ ] **Then atropine maintenance infusion ~10–20% of total loading dose per hour**, titrated to endpoints
- [ ] **Pralidoxime (2-PAM) loading ~30 mg/kg (1–2 g) IV over 20–30 min, then infusion ~8 mg/kg/hr** — give early (before ageing, < 48 h) to any patient needing atropine (not needed for carbamates)
- [ ] **Diazepam 5–10 mg IV** for seizures/agitation
- [ ] Labs: **RBC/plasma cholinesterase**, ABG, electrolytes, glucose, CK, ECG, paracetamol/co-ingestant screen
- [ ] Activated charcoal only if recent large ingestion + protected airway

## 🔵 Continuous
- [ ] Admit ICU — Dx: organophosphate (cholinergic) poisoning
- [ ] Continue atropine infusion titrated to a dry chest/HR/BP; continue pralidoxime infusion per severity; wean atropine slowly as toxicity resolves
- [ ] Frequent respiratory assessment (RR, SpO₂, vital capacity/single-breath count) — **watch for intermediate syndrome 24–96 h** (proximal + respiratory-muscle weakness) → ventilate, may need 1–3 weeks
- [ ] Reassess for atropine toxicity (hyperthermia, ileus, agitation, urinary retention) vs under-dosing
- [ ] Strict I/O; supportive care; psychiatric assessment before discharge (self-harm)
- [ ] Counsel re: OP-induced delayed polyneuropathy (2–3 weeks, distal sensorimotor)

> [!info]- Why
> Death is from respiratory failure (secretions + weakness + central apnoea), so airway control and adequate atropine are the priorities; atropine is titrated to physiological endpoints, not a fixed dose, and often needs large cumulative amounts. Pralidoxime reactivates AChE (nicotinic features atropine can't fix) but must precede ageing. Suxamethonium is metabolised by the inhibited cholinesterase → prolonged block. The intermediate syndrome causes late respiratory failure after the acute crisis settles. Verified vs WHO / standard toxicology.
